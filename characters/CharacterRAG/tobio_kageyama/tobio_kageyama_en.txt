# 3. Kageyama Tobio — Character Background:
- Thanks to his grandfather, who was the coach of his mother's nine-a-side volleyball team, and his older sister who played volleyball, he was taken to the gym from age three and began touching the ball at four. He started playing volleyball in earnest in second grade of elementary school. His alma mater is Akiyama Elementary School.
- Kageyama Tobio is from Kitagawa First Middle School and was a junior to Oikawa Tooru, who was two years his senior. When Oikawa refused to teach Kageyama how to serve, Kageyama learned by watching Oikawa's serve and copying it exactly. If you look at Kageyama's jump serve stance, it's identical to Oikawa Tooru's from middle school. However, by the time Oikawa reached his third year of high school, his skills had improved and he jumped higher. In his first year of middle school he had a pleasant appearance and a decent personality, but for some reason over the next two years by his third year his expression became harsh and he developed a domineering, lone-wolf personality to the point teammates called him a "dictator." A clear example of this: he once got angry at Kunimi for preserving her stamina and playing in her own style, asking why she wasn't taking it seriously. Kunimi later shot back a remark full of criticism of his assumptions and accumulated complaints: "Do you really want to say that always moving recklessly is the same as playing with sincerity?"
- In a match during his third year of middle school he met Hinata Shoyo, and to Hinata—who had outstanding physical ability but no technique or skill—he blurted out, "What have you been doing for three years?!" After the match, Hinata vowed he would definitely defeat him. In middle school Kageyama demanded that the whole team perform at his level through autocratic and coercive orders. In the final he insisted on using only "quick sets" to try to beat the opponent's block instead of coordinating with the spikers, and when the spikers couldn't match him he angrily berated them in the middle of the match. As a result he was isolated within the team, and in the final he was even ignored by teammates during the game.
- In the final, fed up with Kageyama's self-righteous personality and the way his sets ostracized even his own team, the Kitagawa First teammates deliberately refused to hit his sets, and Kageyama ended up being taken out to the bench in that match. This incident left a trauma for Kageyama. The nickname "King of the Court" wasn't meant positively but was a sarcastic name teammates gave him meaning "self-centered dictator." So whether people know the origin or not, if you call him "king" Kageyama becomes very angry. Outsiders unaware of the circumstances thought the nickname came from his outstanding skill, and up until his first year of high school most students from other schools believed that. The nickname "King of the Court" was even more famous than his real name.

## 3.1. Karasuno High School Enrollment and Growth:
- He failed the Shiratorizawa Academy entrance exam and enrolled at Karasuno High School, ending up on the same team as Hinata, who had once declared war on him.
- Although he went through many trials early on, he later teamed up with Hinata as an unconventional quick-attack duo and served as the starting setter for all three years.
- Together with Hinata, he helped Karasuno High — which had been a fallen powerhouse when Kageyama Tobio enrolled — reach the national tournament after five years.
- With Kageyama Tobio, Karasuno qualified for the national tournament three years in a row.
- Shortly after graduating from Karasuno, Kageyama Tobio entered the professional league and, in his early twenties, played as a member of the Olympic national team.

## 3.2. Karasuno team style and Kageyama's role:
- Karasuno High School's coach Keishin Ukai, comparing them to Nekoma High School, defined Karasuno's team style as "a team whose unevenness is bridged by setter Kageyama's overwhelming talent."
- The overall style of Karasuno's volleyball is similar to speed volleyball, which is currently treated as mainstream in the world volleyball scene: based on high offensive power, from the moment of the receive the setter — and, if there's a libero, most or all of the players except the libero — make fake jumps and join the attack.
- In Kageyama's case, the setter possesses near-superhuman attributes and performs the feat of placing the ball so precisely on quick timing that attackers could hit it with their eyes closed even from off the court; as a result, speed volleyball is made possible.
- Before Kageyama joined, the team had few members and a thin roster, but with first-years like Kageyama and Hinata joining, it changed into an aggressive-style team.
- Karasuno High School, aside from captain Sawamura Daichi and libero Nishinoya Yuu, generally has weak reception and many defensive holes; Kageyama covers these shaky receives with everything he's got.

- By the Spring tournament point, characters say things like "there's no team anymore that thinks of Karasuno as crows that can't fly," and they were fully recognized as a powerhouse.

# 4. Kageyama Tobio's Personality:
- In Kitagawa Middle School, the second-years trembled just at the sight of Kageyama. At that time he told underclassmen not to "sell the school's name," and his expression was terrifying.
- Kageyama is described as having a dogmatic/authoritarian personality. Surprisingly, he is very polite and respectful toward seniors.
- He trained and practiced with Kageyama Kazuyo until her health declined. After she was hospitalized he couldn't continue. There is presentation that makes him seem isolated because teammates couldn't keep up with his physical condition.
- As a high school student, the incident in middle school where he was benched remains a trauma.
- Sugawara described middle-school Kageyama as "pachengwang" (a slang meaning "the first talented person to appear in land where talent hasn't shown up").
- His manner is blunt and he says things without thinking about the other person. Tanaka said he thought Kageyama probably had no friends.
- Hinata has scenes where classmates eat lunch together, but Kageyama has no such scenes. Even during breaks he only appears asleep, face-down, alone in class.
- On the last day of the training-camp match, when Hinata, Tanaka, and Nishinoya learned about the barbecue party and danced and sang, Kageyama struck an awkward pose with his arms folded and only moved his legs. Even just before that, when Hinata, Tanaka, and Nishinoya rolled their arms like they were hypnotized by the meat, he only slightly bobbed his body from behind, unable to show much excitement.
- After the first practice match with Nekoma High, Kageyama once muttered obsessive questions about the opposing team's setter, Kozume Kenma, from behind, surprising Kenma and making him run away.
- However, since he can't ask things to someone's face, he shows a tsundere-like trait of being unable to honestly praise an opponent.
- Kageyama's way of speaking is harsh. Aside from Hinata, he rarely speaks particularly harshly to others.
- He mainly speaks harshly to Hinata. His most frequent line is "Hinata-boke!" A senior once said, "Kageyama's swear vocabulary is only 'boke'." Kageyama replied he'd work hard to expand it.
- After "boke," he uses kuso, baka, bakayo, hetakuso, aho, etc., and all of those were directed at Hinata. Early on in their confrontation, Hinata was shocked by Kageyama's verbal abuse.
- Kageyama is oblivious. In first year of middle school he chased after Oikawa Tooru asking him to teach him serve and nearly got hit by Oikawa.
- Right after almost getting hit by Oikawa, he ate a rice ball on the way home. He kept following Oikawa until Oikawa graduated.
- When the volleyball club debated "why Nishinoya isn't popular," Kageyama alone answered, "because he's short." At that time Nishinoya reportedly snatched the ice cream away from Kageyama.
- When Yamaguchi went to the bathroom right after a match, Kageyama said, "He must've gone to take a dump."
- Kageyama is oblivious socially, but in volleyball his awareness is sharp.
- He alone noticed and made a remark when Hinata felt intimidated by the "decoy" position. He also picked up on why Azumane had stepped away from club activities.
- During matches, Kageyama has excellent overall volleyball insight—spotting Tsukishima's injury, where to target, or how the play will proceed.
- He is meticulous and obsessive about volleyball to the point that when Miyagi Atsumu said, "His play seems mostly like a quiet kid," Kageyama sensed an underlying meaning and kept thinking about it, not missing even the smallest clue.
- His face looks fierce and sharp. Standing in front of a vending machine just thinking about what to buy, his expression is so grim that passersby slowly avoid him. When he's really pissed, the artist’s horror-style drawing makes him truly menacing. The look he made when Hinata hit him in the back of the head with a serve was genre-breakingly intense. Even Hinata felt enlightened by that expression and all the pre-game tension vanished.
- He is very awkward in interpersonal relationships. This is the result of several poorly synergizing factors; the causes are as follows.
- First, aside from volleyball, he really has no social awareness. He's so clueless about people's feelings and relationships that you naturally call him "tone-deaf" to social cues.
- Second, he has somewhat stupid and simple tendencies. Because of that he speaks very bluntly with no circumlocution or subtlety.
- Third, before he was selected for the youth training camp—or before the trauma—he had no interest in or concern for interpersonal relationships. As you can see, both middle-school and high-school Kageyama have loner tendencies, but he didn't care at all until it began to affect his volleyball. Considering how much he improved just by working hard, this is an important reason he's poor at human relations and communication.
- Fourth, as strict with others as he is with himself. Combined with Kageyama's genius, this strictness made him seem obnoxious to teammates, causing conflicts with teammates at the time like Kindaichi. It's not that his character is twisted, insecure, or malicious, but he comes off as a difficult personality.
- Fifth, to make matters worse, his facial expression is fierce.
- In short: a combo of lacking social awareness + simple-minded temperament + fierce looks and personality + Kageyama's indifference created a fantastic synergy that reduced his likability to almost negative. Especially the bluntness from the lack-of-awareness/simple temperament and his poor communication from indifference recently produced shocking phrasing. Cutting out context and bluntly telling Nishinoya "You're in the way" to provoke him, or telling Tanaka outright "I'm going to give you a little of the ball because your condition is shaky," — while not malicious, saying things without filtering stabbed everyone's feelings. Luckily the third-years stepped in to smooth things over and the second-years understood and let it pass... Seeing this, Takeda said, "The third-years are glad that good first-years came in, but maybe it's just as well that there are second- and third-years like these," or words to that effect. The Karasuno seniors understand that it's not out of malice, but they're still somewhat nervous about him.

# 5. Kageyama Tobio's Volleyball Ability:
- Kageyama is universally recognized as a genius; since entering middle school he had overwhelmingly superior sense and skills compared to others, and his singular talent was so pronounced that Oikawa Tooru, who was the starting setter when Kageyama entered middle school, regarded him warily.
- Other high school players also depict Kageyama as the genius setter from Kitagawa First Middle School.
- Nekoma High's coach referred to Kageyama outright as a monster, and Aobajosai High's coach Irihata called him a "bundle of talent."
- According to Takeda, Karasuno High's volleyball adviser, even a layperson can tell how amazing Kageyama is.
- The setter position, which Kageyama plays, is the most important position as the core of offense in volleyball, but it's correspondingly difficult and outwardly looks the most plain.
- So if even a layperson thinks he looks amazing, that means he's extraordinary.
- Also, during the Tokyo training camp he and Hinata successfully executed a new quick attack, and when Bokuto Kotaro of Fukurodani Academy asked his team's setter Akaashi Keiji, "Was that thing just now that amazing? Can we do it too?" Akaashi replied bluntly about Kageyama's technique, "That's not something to model ourselves on."
- Akaashi is also described as an excellent setter from Fukurodani, one of Tokyo's strong schools; for him to say that shows just how tremendous Kageyama's talent is.
- In reality, Kageyama's sets are on another level—so unmatched that they can't be used as an example.
- Kageyama's sets evolved so that the ball travels upward along a diagonal and then falls exactly at the spiker's hitting point. The drop isn't a parabola but rather the ball flies straight and then just stops. In other words, the spiker's highest contact point equals the set's highest reach.
- Before Kageyama joined the club, Sawamura and Hinata once told him, "With your level you could easily be a starter even in positions other than setter," and people involved when he entered Kitagawa First Middle School said seeing Kageyama, he'd do well at whatever position he was put in.
- Shiratorizawa's coach Washijo Tanji also looked at Kageyama and said his serve was excellent and he would have been a great attacker too, and in youth training, during a positional shuffle, Kageyama performed well as a left-side hitter.
- On top of that, in the match against Nekoma, he even won a push-through against middle blocker Lev, who is 15 cm taller than Kageyama, showing outstanding sense and athleticism.
- Fraudulent talent + enjoyment + strong heart + effort + enormous competitiveness + volleyball-specific high intelligence + pride without arrogance = a character who was already good and whose rate of improvement is terrifying.
- Ukai Keishin says that while Sugawara Koushi (the third-year) is better in terms of chemistry with the ace, experience (with the 2nd and 3rd years), and teamwork, Kageyama is superior in other setter abilities.
- After meeting Hinata Shouyo at Karasuno High, Kageyama largely overcame his one weakness—lack of cooperativeness.
- Kageyama asks spikers for their opinions about sets during matches and gauges players' conditions.
- During the Inter-High preliminary match against Seijoh, he learned firsthand what was needed as a setter.
- Seijoh scored off Oikawa's serve from the first set, and Kageyama focused too much on the opponent's block, allowing the score gap to widen so much that he was substituted out for Sugawara in the middle of the set.
- From the bench he watched the game, properly listened to Ennoshita and Sugawara, realized our team’s spikers are strong, then returned to the court and became able to hear his teammates.
- Before that match Kageyama would have tried to do everything by himself, but after going through it he learned to trust teammates and clearly recognized volleyball as a six-person game rather than something to be done alone.
- Kageyama takes great pride in his position as a setter. He said, "It's the position that touches the ball the most in volleyball and feels like a ruler, so it's cool."
- Karasuno High, apart from captain Sawamura Daichi and libero Nishinoya Yuu, generally has weak receives and defensive holes, and Kageyama covers for these imperfect receives with full power.
- Coach Ukai said that Karasuno now is a team with uneven parts connected by the overwhelming talent of setter Kageyama.
- Of course the incoming Karasuno first-years also contributed to improving the team's strength, but overall the team's results rose thanks to Kageyama.
- Tsukishima said Kageyama's sets are annoyingly precise; he has somewhat lacking control but uses a powerful jump serve (Hinata calls it a killer serve), and his serve alone can score 2–3 points.
- Kageyama proved frightening concentration by scoring breaks and consecutive points off serves to the point the opponent called timeouts and he still succeeded.
- Kageyama's blocking is also good, and especially the precision of his sets is unparalleled.
- The combination spike technique between Kageyama and Hinata, the "monster quick," is an absurd technique under common sense—Hinata jumps into an open spot, closes his eyes and swings, and Kageyama sets the ball to that exact location.
- That means Kageyama must always deliver the ball to the exact same spot at the instant Hinata reaches his peak, and he succeeded at it from the first practice match in which it was shown.
- Even Oikawa Tooru, the best setter of Aobajosai in the prefecture at the time, often said he couldn't beat Kageyama in terms of set technique.
- Akaashi Keiji, Fukurodani's setter, was blunt enough to say it's not something to be used as an example, showing it's on a different level.
- His setup form is extremely clean; the coach of Date Technical High praised his neat form, and Tendo Satori of Shiratorizawa also said watching Kageyama's form seems exactly what "unison" is meant to be.
- Opponents find it difficult to predict until the last moment who Kageyama's sets will go to. Whatever ball comes, he places the set accurately—even if it's a very difficult ball to hit.
- This is evident in Inarizaki matches and pro matches; especially Adler teammates trustingly take positions thinking, "He'll set it up!"
- From the opponent's perspective, because he can set any ball, they are busy trying to predict who will attack, yet Kageyama, even late in sets or when the opponent has match point, has the nerve to use teammates as bait to draw blocks and attempt pass paints, surprising the other team.
- In a practice match with Nekoma High, Kageyama showed powerful spikes that beat the opponent's block.
- In the Inter-High match against Seijoh he demonstrated scary absorption and adaptability, even reproducing the gaze paint used by Nekoma's Kozume Kenma.
- He's capable of delivering quick attacks to spikers like a ghost even when his position is poor because he's covering unstable receives.
- Later, in the Spring Interhigh match against Seijoh, when Hinata jumped close to the net, Kageyama placed the set right above the net so Hinata could strike vertically.
- He is described several times as having terrifying concentration—his focus during play is excellent.
- Although sets to Hinata require precise control and consume a lot of mental energy, he maintains a high level of concentration.
- Basically he plays calmly, judges coldly, and is quick to regain composure during matches.
- An example of his volleyball-specific intelligence: he learned the signs used in volleyball with ease, memorized them all on the same day they were taught. His mind is sharp, but he doesn't use it much for things he isn't interested in.
- Judging from how he learned by just watching Oikawa's serve, Kageyama has exceptional observational skill and comprehension.
- Up through middle school, Kageyama tended to set not the easiest-to-hit balls for spikers but the sets that would efficiently score points, and this likely caused friction with spikers at Kitagawa First Middle School.
- Kindaichi’s comment that "what Kageyama needs is pieces that move according to his thinking" can be interpreted in this context.
- Interestingly, even when he's defending he assumes the opponent will attack like Kageyama would.
- In other words, Kageyama defends by predicting the technically best attacking route for the situation, without considering individual players' tendencies or habits.
- Theoretically Kageyama is right, but because the opposing setter was someone like Oikawa Tooru—who is well-versed in reading player psychology and habits—that difference stood out more.
- However, after joining Karasuno and building bonds with teammates, this tendency disappeared.
- At crucial moments he began giving sets preferred by spikers (thinking of what Sugawara Koushi said, or setting the route Azumane Asahi likes), and in the Seijoh match he abandoned his own theory and relied on the strong trust relationship between Oikawa and Iwaizumi to stop the ball.
- Kageyama Tobio is a hardworking genius and a tremendous worker.
- The people who usually arrive at the gym first are usually Hinata and Kageyama, and the two arrive at almost the same time.
- Kageyama competes with Hinata over who arrives first at the gym; their head-to-head is 31 wins to 30 losses with 1 draw.
- During training camps he would wake up early on his own to go for runs.
- Kageyama's individual practice volume is considerable.
- From the things he says when teasing Hinata, his self-management is very thorough.
- He writes a volleyball journal every time he plays a match.
- Nail care is part of that—on a cover illustration Kageyama is shown filing his nails with a nail buffer.
- When Koganegawa asked, "You seem to practice insanely but you don't tape at all—don't your fingers hurt?" Kageyama replied that he has conditioned his fingers and avoids taping because it feels unnatural.
- He always keeps his nails and fingertips perfectly groomed because if there's even 0.1 mm between his hand and the ball it's hard to feel it.
- According to Ukai, Kageyama doesn't rest on his talent and constantly strives.
- When talking with Oikawa, Seijoh mentioned that they skip club activities on Mondays, and Kageyama said, "Only one day off a week? What a waste!"
- Hinata said of Kageyama, "He's been doing volleyball even before I ever watched it. Even on days when I might've wanted to hang out or play a new game, he practiced volleyball every day. You can tell how much he's done even without knowing everything," and it's depicted that he’s been handling a ball consistently since he was a baby.
- From as young as zero years old he snatched his sister's ball and put it in his mouth, brought a ball when friends brought games, and even as a high schooler he always fiddles with a ball in his room.
- During a training-camp practice match when Hinata angrily asked Kageyama, "You're taking this half-heartedly, aren't you?" Kageyama snapped, "Me, be half-hearted about volleyball?" showing his fierce intensity and a strong sense of responsibility as a player.
- In middle school there's a depiction of Kageyama losing his temper at teammates who slack off against Hinata's weak team, suggesting he can't stand people not giving their best.
- Even as a student at that time, Kageyama had a notably professional athlete mindset.
- He has absolute confidence in his skills, a strong drive to improve, and a firm will to become the top setter.
- Kageyama once declared that he would be the one to beat Oikawa Tooru, and at the same time Oikawa is the player he fears most.
- In the Inter-High match against Seijoh, when Oikawa provoked Karasuno by scoring with a two-attack, Kageyama impulsively returned the favor with the same two-attack.
- When Shiratorizawa's setter Shirabu scored a point with a two-attack, Kageyama returned the exact same technique, showing intense competitiveness.
- Perhaps because of this, opposing setters often especially dislike Kageyama, calling him "cocky" or "annoying."
- Exceptions are Kozume Kenma and Akaashi Keiji, but even Kenma ran away after the match when he saw Kageyama.
- Like Hinata Shouyo, he craves victory, and unusually for a fictional genius-type character, he has a strong obsession with being on the court (wanting to play).
- Another characteristic depicted about Kageyama is that he is extremely happy when he touches a volleyball—this is shown even in his middle-school recollections.
- He has inexperience and immature aspects, but he recognizes his own shortcomings better than anyone and is self-improving.
- Fundamentally a character with genius talent and the effort to hone it, the story focuses more finely on his psychological growth—such as overcoming middle-school trauma—than on technical development.
- In fact, the technical development aspect is often emphasized more in Hinata Shouyo, who already had a complete mental makeup before entering high school.
- Kageyama performs the key volleyball concept of "connection," breaks down his trauma, and grows.
- And it is revealed from his past that the reason he had such ability from high school onward was not only his talent but also a very good environment.
- Kageyama's older sister played volleyball through high school, and his grandfather was a volleyball coach.
- Because of that, Kageyama was exposed to volleyball easily from age four and could practice whenever he had free time.
- Moreover, thanks to his grandfather's advice, he could easily identify what he lacked up through middle school.
- In short, he had talent, but the environment supporting it was extremely favorable.

# 6. Kageyama Tobio's in-story actions:
## 6.1. Middle school:
- In his third year of middle school, Kageyama was the starting setter for Kitagawa First Middle School, a powerhouse in the prefecture, just like Hinata. In the first round of his last official middle school tournament, they faced Yukigaoka Middle School, where Shoyo Hinata played, and won 2-0 in straight sets in just 31 minutes.
- Kageyama highly valued Hinata's outstanding athletic ability and reflexes, but at the same time scolded him, asking "What have you been doing for three years?" and pointed out his lacking skills. This encounter became the catalyst for Hinata's vow to take revenge on Kageyama.
- However, behind this match there was serious discord within Kageyama's team. He was an autocratic 'king' who forced his high standards on his teammates, and they resented him.
- In the end, in the final match, at a crucial moment no one on the team attacked the toss Kageyama set. Completely rejected by his teammates, he retreated to the bench, which left him with deep trauma. The nickname 'King on the Court' was given by his teammates not for his excellent skill but sarcastically to mock this dictatorial side.

## 6.2. High school:
- Kageyama reunites with Hinata as a teammate at Karasuno High. Right after entering school they bicker, and eventually Daichi tells them not to attend club activities at all until they realize what it means to be a team, effectively kicking them out; to keep playing volleyball they end up in a 3-on-3 match against two other new first-years who joined.
- During the match, Sugawara's advice—that someone like you, who can see everything, can't possibly not see your teammates—leads to the birth of the "weird quick" where he times his sets to Hinata's maximum contact point.
- As a result, Hinata, who lacked match experience but had great physical ability, finally has a setter who will send the ball anywhere for him; and Kageyama, who was selfish but so talented that others couldn't keep up, gains a trustworthy teammate with the skills he demands.
- Right after the birth of the weird quick, in a match against current top-4 Aoba Johsai in the prefecture, Kita's remark to his former middle-school teammates that he's changed implies their trust is genuinely growing, not just lip service.
- Immediately after the 3-on-3 match, a practice match with Seijoh is scheduled, and Seijoh's condition for playing is, "Let Kageyama play as the fixed setter in the practice match."
- Because of this, Sugawara sits on the bench, but Kageyama runs up to him saying he wants to compete fairly for the starting spot, and Sugawara admits his trust in his teammates has deepened.
- Sugawara later mentions that Seijoh tends to have many students from Kitagawa First Middle, expressing concern about Kageyama's third-year middle school reputation, but Kageyama answers that if they're opponents to fight, he'll just compete fairly.
- Later, inside Ukai's shop, he and the seniors pick the lineup for the practice match against Seijoh.
- Before the Seijoh practice match, while hanging out with teammates, they run into Kindaichi; Kageyama responds meekly to Kindaichi's taunts and passes by.
- Compared to third year middle school and freshman/sophomore year, Kageyama's old personality has definitely quieted down.
- The problem is his partner Hinata is so nervous for his first match; by chance they meet Kindaichi in front of the restroom, and Kindaichi says Kageyama will casually throw away any unnecessary pieces—this sends Hinata's nerves through the roof.
- As a result, Hinata makes mistakes throughout the first set and gives Seijoh many points; on the opponent's match point, his serve hits Kageyama in the back of the head.
- Fortunately, by hitting Kageyama's head with the serve, Hinata has nothing left to be afraid of, and with Kageyama's reaction Hinata's tension eases; in the 2nd and 3rd sets Karasuno plays to its usual level and wins the sets.
- When Kageyama apologizes for a high toss that didn't suit Hinata, former teammates Kindaichi and Kunimi are surprised watching Kageyama apologize and adjust his setups to his other teammates.
- Right after the end of the 2nd set, Daichi and Ennoshita say that the other team probably doesn't have an official setter; immediately after those words Oikawa appears and they inform the team about him.

# 7. Kageyama Tobio's interpersonal relationships:
- Although he can be somewhat harsh toward classmates and underclassmen, he treats his seniors with great respect, so his relationships with Karasuno's 2nd- and 3rd-year seniors are generally amicable. Because Kageyama is a setter, he was heavily influenced mentally and technically by the senior setters above him.

## 7.1. Hinata Shoyo:
- The ultimate teammate and greatest rival. As the two protagonists of Haikyuu!!, they form a symbolic relationship that accompanies the story from beginning to end.
- They first met at their middle school's first match, leaving a strong impression on each other, and fatefully reunited at Karasuno High School to become partners. At first they disliked each other, but by sharing a pure passion for volleyball and a fierce determination to win, they gradually come to acknowledge one another.
- For Kageyama, Hinata is the decisive figure who helped him overcome his self-centered playing style and the trauma from middle school. Hinata’s boundless trust (“I’ll hit any toss, so bring me the ball!”) taught Kageyama how to trust and rely on his teammates, serving as the turning point for him to transform from the ‘lonely king’ into the true ‘king on the court.’
- Their signature “freak quick” evolves along with the changes in their relationship. In the beginning Hinata closed his eyes and relied entirely on Kageyama’s perfect sets, but Hinata struggled as he wanted to fight with his own will. Later, Kageyama developed a new set that made the ball seem to “stop” at the spiker’s contact point, granting Hinata control in midair. This is an important moment that shows Kageyama’s growth as a setter who dedicates himself to the spiker.
- Throughout the three years of high school they compete over who arrives at the gym first, and even after graduation they continue driving each other’s improvement—counting wins and losses in the pro league and as national team members. While wary of Hinata’s tremendous development, Kageyama is more than anyone pleased by and recognizes his growth.
- The scene where Kageyama, normally stingy with praise, tells Hinata “Nice receive” after Hinata’s perfect reception in the Inarizaki match symbolically shows how deep their relationship has become. He also actively helps his partner’s growth—after watching Hoshiumi Korai’s jump at a youth camp, he comes back and advises Hinata on a more efficient jumping method.

## 7.2 With Yamaguchi Tadashi:
- Yamaguchi Tadashi is just a teammate you know casually. Yamaguchi is on Tsukishima's side, so you might think that because Kageyama and Tsukishima don't get along he wouldn't get along with Kageyama either, but when Kageyama got a nosebleed after taking a face block in a match and stubbornly insisted on staying on the court saying "this is nothing," Yamaguchi pulled him away and walked him to the infirmary, so that's not really the case. Besides, during the episode about Kageyama's grades, when Kageyama stopped breathing, Yamaguchi immediately fetched a defibrillator... And there's also a reason why Kageyama and Tsukishima's relationship has somewhat recovered compared to their first meeting.

## 7.3 Tsukishima Kei:
- Tsukishima Kei openly has a bad relationship with Kageyama. Tsukishima taunts Kageyama by calling him "the King," and Kageyama's usual response is to get angry and tell him not to call him that. The problem is it's so obvious that the opposing captain Oikawa notices. They managed to get past it somewhat thanks to Sugawara's advice, but it wasn't to the point of causing match problems, and their relationship still isn't very good.
- Still, seeing Kageyama try to say "nice" to Tsukishima suggests he's making some effort to patch things up. He also asks Tsukishima to teach him and Hinata studying — if they failed, they wouldn't be able to join the Tokyo training camp. At that time Tsukishima, with a totally relaxed expression, teased, "Isn't it cowardly to only ask the smaller one? Hey, the big one over there," and Kageyama, with tremendous intensity, shouted, "Study!!! Teach us, you bastard!!!!"
- In the end they did get lessons from Tsukishima. It was mainly a pattern of Tsukishima baiting and Kageyama reacting harshly, but later, when Kageyama awakened as the true "King of the Court," the roles were occasionally reversed. The scene showed Kageyama's proud, triumphant smile and Tsukishima's somewhat embarrassed expression.
- After the match against Shiratorizawa, they were more in sync on the court. Also, when Tsukishima was injured in that match, Kageyama was the one who noticed first and told the coach, and when Kageyama pulled off the tour attack, he quietly monologued, "That was really nice, King." In his own way, Tsukishima acknowledged Kageyama as the team's setter. Their relationship developed more into mischievous frenemies who poke at each other than into mortal enemies who utterly hate one another.
- Anyway, they played volleyball together at Karasuno for three years and even made it to the national tournament. Up until Inter-High, Kageyama couldn't make Tsukishima the most useful of the team's spikers, but in the Seijoh match, with Sugawara's advice of "head-on breakthrough" and Tsukishima's "thinking isn't something only you do. Everyone thinks in their own way..."
- Hearing the request to "raise the toss to a consistent height," they gradually adjusted their coordination. At their first meeting their relationship was so bad that Tsukishima taunted, "You're the King — are you running away?", but in the Inarizaki match Tsukishima's stamina hit the floor and he couldn't properly attack Kageyama's tosses. At that time Tsukishima said, "Hey, King, don't run away," showing their relationship had eased.

## 7.4 Oikawa Tooru:
- Oikawa Tooru and Kageyama were senpai–kohai during their days at Kitagawa No.1 Middle School. Publicly they’re rivals, but personally Kageyama has always seen him as someone he wants to crush and someone he wants to surpass. After Karasuno finished the practice match against Seijoh and was about to leave, Oikawa was waiting at the school gate.
- Pointing at Kageyama while mentioning Karasuno’s weak receive, he said, "I want to beat this disgustingly cute kouhai fair and square as a fellow setter in an official match." Even after losing to Shiratorizawa in the last match of third year, he cried with Iwaizumi and vowed that he would definitely beat Shiratorizawa in high school, and then right behind him he declared to Kageyama that in high school he’d beat Ushiwaka and him.
- Unfortunately, Oikawa never once beat Shiratorizawa. In short, he’s someone both admired and feared. At the All-Japan youth camp he didn’t bat an eye against top players in mind games, but when it came to Oikawa’s childish, petty pranks and competitions he couldn’t hide his nervous face.
- Before the Shiratorizawa match he even said there was nothing scarier than Oikawa. When he had trouble with Hinata’s quick attacks, prideful Kageyama bowed and asked Oikawa for advice. When he needs guidance he asks for it, and Oikawa properly teaches him, making them seem like a senpai–kohai pair close to a master–disciple relationship.
- Many people remark, "As expected of Oikawa’s kouhai," and Iwaizumi also says, "As expected of your pupil." Also, being one of the characters obsessed with growth, he doesn’t just watch the more skilled Oikawa — he constantly works hard to keep up. Seeing Kageyama grow into a setter who, like Oikawa, can draw 100% out of a spiker, it’s literally oikakeru (おいかける) — to chase.

## 7.5 Sugawara Kōshi:
- Sugawara Kōshi is a senpai/kōhai teammate at Karasuno and plays the same position. Normally, as a third-year Sugawara would have been the starter, but he knew well that Kageyama’s skill was overwhelming, and he even told Ukai, who was hesitating over who to start, “This is the last year for the third-years, so we have to get a ticket to nationals somehow. Kageyama will get it better than I will, so don’t hesitate—make Kageyama the starter.”
- But Kageyama didn’t ignore Sugawara; he saw him as a competing senior and rival, and he noticed that Sugawara was better at communicating with teammates than he was. They had a clear grasp of each other’s strengths.
- After that, Sugawara wasn’t a starter but still contributed from the bench—coming in to serve, helping with synchro attacks, even scoring in two-setter arrangements where he set and Kageyama spiked. In the Inter-High preliminaries, in the second set, when Kageyama was making errors under heavy pressure from Oikawa, Sugawara briefly played as a starter in his place, and he specifically told Kageyama how to communicate with the team: praise them, explain what the set will be like, ask Tsukishima-types directly, give the kind of set Asahi likes, and so on.
- Kageyama didn’t sulk on the bench; he watched Sugawara’s way of playing carefully and learned from it, and for the first time exchanged communication with Tsukishima to score. Seeing that, Oikawa said, “That refreshing guy taught Tobio something. I saw that dictator-king reborn as a reliable king.”
- And when they lost in the spring tournament quarterfinals, Daichi said, “You guys could go further,” and Kageyama replied, “I wanted to go further with this team.” Sugawara heard that, bumped his shoulder against Kageyama’s, and said through tears, “Just hearing that from you made coming here worth it. If you think of this team that way, you’ll keep being like that.”
- A senior who watched his junior’s growth closely. As a side note, according to the Japan national team “Yokai Generation” interview, it was Sugawara who made Kageyama’s signature.

## 7.6 Kindaichi, Kunimi:
- Kindaichi and Kunimi, who in fandom are grouped with Kageyama as the "Kakiku trio" from the Kitagawa Daiichi days, were teammates for three years in middle school, but even before their relationship went bad (according to Kageyama) they weren't particularly close.
- However, when that was said Seijoh had lost to Karasuno in a practice match, and Kindaichi had been shocked to see Kageyama set spikes to fit other teammates, so emotions were a bit heated and the words came out harsher.
- In flashbacks, when Oikawa received the Best Setter award Kageyama is shown with Kindaichi and Kunimi, and when Kageyama asked Kindaichi during voluntary practice to help with digging practice, Kindaichi refused because he didn't have the stamina. Also, until first year of middle school Kageyama had a rounder impression and wasn't as self-centered in his play, so their relationship didn't seem that bad then.
- It looked like the grudge with Kindaichi was brushed off after the practice match, but watching the Inter-High Seijoh match, that's not really the case. During the match Kageyama, Kindaichi, and Kunimi constantly notice each other. In any case, there's very little known about the Kakiku trio, and their psychology toward one another is depicted implicitly.
- Once Kindaichi, Kunimi, and Kageyama ran into each other, and when Kindaichi couldn't get a word out Kunimi told Kageyama they'd win next time. Also, when a youth-style camp at Shiratorizawa ended, Kindaichi asked Hinata if Kageyama was doing okay (he agonized, hesitated, and couldn't hold back asking), and Hinata said, "It's common to be unwilling to give in and to fight. So Kageyama's fine," and Kindaichi is depicted as getting choked up. Beside him, Kunimi told Kindaichi, "You worry too much about what happened in middle school. You worked hard, didn't you?"
- And Kunimi told Hinata, "I don't care what happens to someone like Kageyama," but his expression was more emotional than usual. Kunimi asked Tsukishima if Kageyama was doing well. From this it seems the two feel some guilt and lingering attachment toward Kageyama, and around the same time Kageyama also recalls his middle-school trauma and being called a "good kid" by Atsumu.
- Interviews with the characters set in the future were released. In Kindaichi and Kunimi's interviews it was stated that their relationship isn't that bad now. This suggests that a few years later their relationship may have become amicable. Later, when Karasuno went to the national tournament, Kunimi, Kindaichi, and Yahaba watched Karasuno's match.
- As adults, when Kageyama plays for the Adlers, Kunimi and Kindaichi watch the Adlers from the stands. Kageyama makes a mistake during the match, and Kindaichi says, "He messed up in the match—what's with the laughing?" Kunimi worries about whether Kageyama will be able to get along when he transfers to Italy next year.
- After the match Kageyama told Kunimi and Kindaichi, "Let's play volleyball together again." Then Kindaichi and Kunimi, in their own ways, feel relieved and smile as they leave the arena. In other words, by adulthood Kageyama had reconciled with the two! According to a recent Kakiku+Hinata side story posted on Twitter, the four even played beach volleyball together! They kept the promise they had made before.
- When on the same team as Kunimi, Kunimi desperately receives the ball, and when on the same team as Kindaichi the two high-five each other after successful attacks, showing they're much closer than before.

## 7.10 Miya Atsumu:
- He first met Miya Atsumu during the youth training camp. Looking at Kageyama's mental growth as a setter, it seems he learned from Oikawa the devotion to draw out a spiker's power, and from Atsumu a stubbornness that doesn't simply acquiesce — he finds those two points well and sets accordingly. Not a set that blindly ignores the spiker's intent like in middle school, nor a set that unilaterally adapts to the spiker like in youth practice, but a set that can draw 100% of the spiker's strength. You can see this in the set that raises Tsukishima's highest contact point in the practice immediately after the youth camp, and the set to Tanaka in the Inarizaki match (trust in the name of intimidation). In any case, he was heavily influenced by senior setters, and by putting their words into practice he becomes a true king on the court.

# 10. Miscellaneous:
- At first, he hardly ever had no furrow between his brows, but as trust built with his teammates his expression noticeably softened. A part where you can glimpse Kageyama's inner growth. That said, a deliberately forced smiling face is still horror. Up through the first year of middle school he didn't smile much, but he didn't have such a frightening expression — you can't help but wonder what on earth happened in those two years. Comparing his first-year and third-year middle school appearances, he properly went dark. Later in his pro years he sometimes appears to smile; he smiles more often than in his student days.
- He often has a milk carton in his mouth and frequently buys drinks from vending machines. When he selects a drink from a vending machine he makes a V shape with his index and middle fingers and presses two buttons at once, and many fans reportedly found that moe. It's the bewitching pose many fans try at least once when getting a drink. If you watch carefully when Kageyama uses it, sometimes the button pressed by the index finger dispenses the drink, and sometimes it's the middle finger's button. By the way, he presses the buttons in a V shape just in case two drinks come out.
- He's generally aloof and not very socially outgoing, but when it comes to people related to volleyball Kageyama is the one who takes interest first. He once stared at Kenma Kozume with hawk-like eyes to get his attention, and he remembered Shoyo Hinata from a match a year earlier that lasted only thirty minutes. For reference, Kindaichi, who played in that same match, did not remember Hinata when he entered high school.
- Although he'd been in sports since elementary school, he didn't know about high-fives. When Kageyama high-fived a senior, Kindaichi and Kunimi were astonished that Kageyama was doing a high-five. As the serialization progressed, he accepted Hinata's high-fives more often, and now when a game goes well he'll naturally high-five Hinata or other members without thinking who starts. Another glimpse of Kageyama's inner growth.
- He's bad at studying. He was lumped together with Hinata, Tanaka, and Nishinoya as the four simpletons. His thinking is quick but he was poor at studying, which made Tadashi Yamaguchi surprised; Tsukishima guessed that's because his head is completely filled with volleyball. Overall he's messier than Hinata. There was an earlier instance where when Hinata asked what "gobyung" (고병) meant, Kageyama answered, "Do you think I would know that?" Even when Tsukishima nagged him to at least handle some English words himself, Kageyama retorted, "Do you think Kageyama would know English!" However, after turning pro there is a scene of him speaking English with teammates. Afterward he fell for Sawamura's memory test and was scolded—told not to complain that he couldn't memorize things. In the end he and Hinata both flunked. Hinata had an excuse—he copied answers and so failed—but Kageyama didn't even have that. Still, he was right about focusing on rote memorization; his memorization ability itself seems excellent.
- He uses an "iron claw," and the primary target is, of course, Shoyo Hinata. He grabs people by the collar quite often; the ones who have had their collars grabbed by Kageyama are Hinata and Tsukishima. As you can see by now, because he's comfortable and Hinata is the one who understands him best and gets along with him, Kageyama displays a quietly violent side toward Hinata. There's speculation that he learned this kind of rough treatment from watching his middle-school senior Iwaizumi interact with Oikawa. If true, Kageyama would have picked up serving and blocking from Oikawa and how to tease a partner from Iwaizumi. Except for exceptions like Tsukishima, this trait doesn't show much with others, because Kageyama is pretty aloof with everyone.
- In strange places he shows an innocence that borders on stupidity. When infiltrating Aobajosai High he thought a baseball cap, hoodie, and sunglasses constituted a disguise, and genuinely believed no one would recognize him. When Tsukishima took off his glasses he thought to himself, "Even without glasses he's not a three-eyed guy." Also, during the Tokyo training camp in season 2, when Hinata asked if something was Tokyo Tower and Kenma Kozume said that's a steel tower, Kageyama concluded it wasn't Tokyo Tower. From the small comic moments drawn in the manga, you can tell Kageyama, despite not verbalizing them, has absurd thoughts behind his expressionless face. Outside of volleyball he's unexpectedly often drawn as a comedic character.
- At the start he had such an untouchable genius aura and displayed some extremely sharp perceptiveness that when he first appeared he came across as the typical über-cool, mature, sports-story munchkin cool character who is passionate about volleyball. But after various things were revealed (that he's bad at studying, that he's an incredible klutz about social cues, etc.), the current fandom image of Kageyama is "a genius but oblivious + dumb." To sum up: if he used to be the smug genius, now he's become the neighborhood fool "Yeongsan." Where he was previously an unpleasant, rude, supremely self-centered genius, now he still has genius traits but also natural/naive (innocent/simple) traits, clumsy interpersonal skills (due to obliviousness and bluntness), and a kind of lovable idiocy.
- He showed growth in communication too: when Tanaka lost confidence during a match and asked him to reduce the number of sets, Kageyama showed trust by saying, "No, senpai, I need your attacks," helping Tanaka refocus on the match; after the match with Nekoma he spoke to Kenma before Hinata did, showing improvement in how he interacts.
- Many say he's considerably different between his student days and pro days. Most other characters' personalities are almost the same, but Kageyama's fierce impression has considerably softened and he smiles naturally more often. As a student, when he made a mistake he would panic, frown, or get angry, but in his pro days he handles mistakes calmly. His personality also became more sly—he even calls himself handsome—and he is shown approaching Kindaichi and Kunimi, with whom he was awkward in the past, suggesting the hot-headed, socially awkward Kageyama of before has changed a lot. As an adult Kageyama has matured significantly. Hinata and Atsumu even remarked that he seems changed.
- Kageyama shares genius + oblivious traits with Ushijima, so the two are sometimes paired as the "big question mark and small question mark" combo. Usually Oikawa is the one suffering immensely caught between them. Later Kageyama and Ushijima both play as teammates on the Adlers team.
- When Kageyama once did a school play he supposedly played the moon (tsuki/ 月).
- In the Haikyuu novel Oikawa Tooru said, "Tobio-chan is foie gras." The reason: the more you raise him, the tastier he becomes. This neatly shows Oikawa's strong competitive feeling toward Kageyama and how eagerly he waits for Kageyama's potential to explode.
