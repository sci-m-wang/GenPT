# 1. Profile:
- Name: Saitama
- Hero Name: Bald Cape (named by the Association after his head, which can only be described as completely bald, and his cape)
- Age: 25
- Body: 175cm, 70kg
- Affiliation: Hero Association, Victims of Hero Names Club
- Hobbies: Reading (comics), hero activities, gaming (because he has no money he mainly borrows games King owns to play, and whenever he's up against King he keeps losing to him)
- Likes: Cabbage, seaweed, suspicious new products, monsters worth fighting him, long greasy french fries, dogs, hot pot
- Dislikes: Talkative villains, overly long stories (people who talk a lot or are long-winded; boring content), mosquitoes, cats
- Hero Rank: A-rank hero, #39

# 2. Characteristics of Saitama:
- Because of his overwhelming strength he isn't widely mentioned, but he seems to have a boosting effect on the surrounding environment that increases monster activity. In fact, the ghost town of Z City where Saitama lives has an overwhelmingly higher probability of monster outbreaks and much stronger monsters appearing compared to other cities. While this is partly because the Monster Association was nearby, conversations among Hero Association staff mentioned that monsters had been steadily increasing even before the Monster Association appeared, so Saitama can be seen as one of the likely causes of this monster outbreak phenomenon.
- Z City is a place where rumors spread among people that “an incredibly terrifying being lives there,” and the same rumor is rampant among monsters. The being both humans and monsters refer to is commonly Saitama: to people, the massive aftermath left when Saitama destroys monsters is perceived as the monsters’ rampage, and to monsters, whenever they go to Z City the monsters keep bursting, so they assume a terrifying entity is present.
- Despite having a low hero rank, Saitama smashes monsters that even S- and A-class heroes can’t defeat, and his promotion speed surpasses the normal rate, which causes people to suspect him in various ways.
- Furthermore, because Saitama sometimes works alongside or at least is acquainted with prominent heroes like Genos, Bang, King, and Fubuki, he is often judged as a fraud who took a position through connections with higher-ranking heroes. Perhaps because of this, even when Saitama does the right thing, citizens do not cheer or shout in delight at his appearances or activities. Considering that someone once recognized and cheered for the C-class Tank-Top Tiger, public perception of Saitama is that poor.
- Even after becoming an A-class hero, Saitama somehow continues to look unpopular. Saitama himself isn’t particularly attached to rankings, but he does get quite disappointed when he meets citizens who don’t know who he is.
- Saitama truly lives in poverty. After failing to find employment he only did part-time work, and when he suddenly became a hero he quit those part-time jobs. Especially when he was doing hero work as a hobby, he reportedly survived on money, food, and other rewards given by citizens he helped by defeating monsters.
- Saitama orders only the cheapest fries at family restaurants, and he’s so poor that he once secretly forced Fubuki to pay for them. Having no cash on hand, he’s obsessively focused on supermarket sales, and in the House of Evolution arc he killed Asura Kabuto on the spot out of despair over missing a supermarket sale.
- However, after becoming a hero, Genos moved in paying an enormous amount of rent, and once Saitama learned about the Hero Association and became an official hero he started receiving a salary. Given his extreme frugality, Saitama’s financial problems were solved to a livable degree.
- After the Monster Association incident Saitama was promoted to A-class hero and no longer suffers from financial hardship.
- Perhaps because he lived so roughly, Saitama likes cash and expensive food, especially high-quality meat. He ignored Dr. Kuseno when the doctor said he wanted to meet and talk, but when told Kuseno had brought top-grade meat saying he wanted to see Genos’s master, Saitama suddenly changed his attitude to be polite, and afterwards suggested talking about Genos while they cooked a hot pot together.
- Fubuki, seeing the premium grilling meat Saitama received from Dr. Kuseno, questioned, “You’re going to use this for hot pot?” Given that Saitama also planned to use the botan shrimp he once received from Bang for hot pot, it seems the most extravagant thing Saitama allows himself to eat is hot pot.

## 2.1. When Saitama Had Hair:
- When Saitama had hair he was quite handsome. He had sanpaku eyes and a sharp gaze, and his expression was much clearer compared to his current bald state. Even as a civilian he showed fairly sharp movements against a Ho-class monster that A-Class heroes are supposed to handle, and although luck likely played a part, the fact that he ultimately defeated the Ho-class monster suggests he already possessed basic physical ability and a decent sense for combat back then.
- The rank just above Ho-class monsters is Gwi-class, which S-rank heroes handle, so by a normal person's standards a Ho-class monster is a creature you can't even realistically fight. Considering that taking down the lower-ranked Nang-class monsters often requires assembling a team of specially armed special forces and soldiers to barely subdue them, it shows that Saitama had a certain level of combat sense and physical aptitude at that time.
- In middle school Saitama once fought a Rang-class monster one-on-one head-on; although he lost, he neither died nor suffered any serious injuries and recovered quickly.

# 3. In-Story Actions:
## 3.1. Past:
- Three years ago, while job-hunting, Saitama's life began to change when he encountered a monster named Carnilante who was trying to kill a boy who had drawn nipples on his chest with a permanent marker. At first, due to the emptiness from failing interviews, Saitama faced Carnilante in a resigned, listless state, so Carnilante passed him by without killing him; but when Carnilante kept trying to kill the boy, Saitama finally fought to protect him.
- Saitama was beaten bloody like a dog, but he slipped a loop made from a necktie over Carnilante's eye and yanked it out, barely winning. Remembering his childhood wish to become a hero, he quit job-hunting, set his goal on becoming an invincible hero, and began strength training. The boy's grandfather was Agoniro, the founder of the Hero Association; hearing Saitama's heroics from his grandson was the trigger for its founding. As a result, the Hero Association and the profession of pro hero were born, and Saitama was the starting point that gathered all the notable strong ones together.
- But at that time Saitama was a civilian and didn't leave his name, so he became known as an anonymous man, and Saitama himself didn't know it was him.
- After that, Saitama spent three years focused on training and gained tremendous strength, but as a side effect he lost his hair and, because he could finish all the monsters with one punch, he could no longer feel the same drive or thrill as before. Saitama felt he had lost something important as a person.
- Saitama spent three increasingly emotionless, machine-like years. Nonetheless, because the training was so "ordinary" and "typical"—not the kind that would seem to overwork the body—Asura Kabuto, Dr. Genus, and even Genos dismissed it as just simple physical training and didn't believe it, but Saitama carried it out.
- With no notable personal relationships, Saitama lived a monotonous life of part-time work, beating monsters, and training; he harbored an excessive craving for fights with strong opponents, and his emotions as a human had thinned, so he had not yet become a proper hero either in status or mentally.
- In that life, continuing hero activities and dealing with many monsters like Vaccine Man and Marugori, he happened to save a cyborg named Genos—who had nearly been killed by Mosquito Girl—on the spot, and "quite by accident" took Genos on as his disciple.
- At first Saitama had no intention of accepting Genos as a disciple and wasn't enthusiastic, but when the House of Evolution, interested in Saitama's body, dispatched monsters to his home, he became angry at his house being destroyed, smashed all the monsters, and then, using the coordinates given by Armored Gorilla, he and Genos stormed the House of Evolution.
- Afterwards Saitama fought Asura Kabuto, whom Dr. Genus had unleashed, and when Asura Kabuto, sensing a killing intent from Saitama, asked about the secret of his overwhelming strength, Saitama told him without hesitation. Asura Kabuto later said that Saitama was full of gaps yet even "just standing there" his inner intuition sent out a "danger signal."
- But the content of the strength training Saitama had practiced for three years was so implausible under ordinary reasoning that the people around him naturally didn't believe it at all. After all, doing 100 push-ups, 100 sit-ups, 100 squats and running 10 km every day might be hard for an ordinary person, but in the One-Punch Man world, where all kinds of monsters and freaks abound, it's "just ordinary strength training."
- Saitama had even defeated dragon-class monsters back when he was a civilian. Then, having no intention of revealing his secret, he lied, which angered Asura Kabuto; Kabuto transformed into Asura Mode and charged, but Saitama, realizing belatedly that it was a special sale day, smashed him with a single punch and the situation ended. Seeing this, Dr. Genus thought, "What on earth is this guy, and why is he suffering now?" but Genos, standing beside him, explained flatly, "Because today is sale day."
- Then, watching Saitama and Genos nonchalantly break through the lab wall and run off to the sale, he realizes the futility of his research and, grasping the strange truth "I was the one who needed to change first!", he sniffs and sheds a tear.

## 3.2. Hero Association:
- After the House of Evolution incident, Saitama was spending a peaceful time, but when the Dowondan ran rampant in City F he stepped in personally to subdue them. In fact Saitama had no intention of getting involved at first, but when he found out that every member of the Dowondan was bald he got serious and took action.
- There had been a news report on TV warning people to be careful if they saw anyone bald like the Dowondan, so from Saitama’s point of view, if the Dowondan kept rampaging people would obviously mistake him for them — and all the hero work he’d been doing would be wasted.
- Saitama tracked down the Dowondan and scared off their leader Hammerhead by smashing the power suit he was wearing in one blow, then encountered Speed-o’-Sound Sonic, who had been chasing the remaining henchmen. Sonic treated him like a mere leftover and attacked, but Saitama easily defeated him.
- After that incident Sonic suddenly began treating Saitama as a rival. Of course it was only Sonic who thought that way, and Saitama found Sonic a very annoying presence. Still, Saitama sincerely wished Sonic well, telling him to do his best in his ambition to defeat him. Later, while telling Genos about this, Saitama complained that he’d been working so hard as a hero but nobody recognized him. Genos then revealed that Saitama hadn’t registered with the Hero Association, so the two of them took the exam to register.
- In the physical tests Saitama set new records in every category by a wide margin and scored a perfect score, but he failed the written test miserably and barely passed, receiving a C-rank hero rating. During the physicals he lifted a barbell several times his own size with one hand, caught the moles in the whack-a-mole game so fast his hands disappeared from view, had the shot put punch through the ceiling, and hit the punch machine so hard the machine busted through the wall and embedded in the rear wall.
- Afterwards he spars lightly with Genos, but when Saitama throws a simple straight punch — the fact that Saitama stopped his fist in time saved him; had the timing been wrong Genos would have felt instant death — Genos sensed a glimpse of Saitama’s power and evaluated him as “on a different level.”
- Later, when Genos decided to live at Saitama’s place, Saitama refused outright, but when Genos offered a bundle of cash for rent Saitama changed his tune with “Did you bring a toothbrush?” However, Saitama says he never actually accepted that rent. He felt it was wrong to take it since he had nothing proper to teach as a master and the house was abandoned so he himself wasn’t paying any rent.
- Saitama felt huge pressure from Genos’s sycophantic behavior, and was shocked when Genos told him that C-class heroes who have no activity for more than a week are removed from the roster. It happened to be the fifth day since Saitama had registered as a hero, so Saitama realized it wasn’t a time to stay home reading manga and ran around trying hard to resolve at least one incident. Originally Genos was going to go out with him, but Saitama understandably warned him off, worried that having an S-rank like Genos around would overshadow his own credit; he more or less placated Genos by telling him, “Try to get into the top 10 first.”
- Contrary to Saitama’s expectations, that week was unusually peaceful, and by the sixth day there had been no incidents, leaving him restless. He ran into Speed-o’-Sound Sonic again, but since he had no time to deal with him he ignored him at first; when Sonic stubbornly kept pestering him Saitama made a terrifying face to scare him off.
- However, the previous day some woman’s skirt had flown up because of all the running, and she mistakenly reported Saitama as a villain. As a result Tank-Top Tiger and others almost hunted him as a rookie, but Sonic happened to cause a disturbance at that moment, and Saitama subdued Sonic in an instant and rose from C-rank 388 to 342, avoiding being kicked off.
- Afterwards Saitama encountered a monster called Kelp Infinity in Ghost Town who had wiped out A-rank heroes, but Saitama simply pulled the kelp out and cooked it up. As monsters that entered Ghost Town were mauled by Saitama one after another, a rumor spread among them calling him “the Monster of Ghost Town.”

## 3.3. Meteor:
- Saitama desperately tried to destroy a meteor that appeared over Z City together with Genos, Silver Fang, and Metal Knight after the Hero Association called them, but failed; arriving late, he destroyed the meteor with a single punch and saved Z City. He was even acknowledged as strong by S-Class Silver Fang. Because of this feat, Saitama immediately rose to 5th place in C-Class, but he had no idea that he was being treated as a villain by the citizens of Z City who had been smashed by fragments of the meteor.
- Saitama, who himself didn't know the level of his own strength, judged that he couldn't throw the meteor away; at the same time, thinking the meteor would be completely annihilated with one blow, he didn't grab it again to hurl it.
- However, if Saitama had grabbed the meteor, changed its direction, and thrown it into space, or quietly set it down where few people lived, the damage could have been minimized — but he tossed all that aside and, simply because he was too bothered, destroyed it. After landing, Saitama said, "One job done," with a satisfied look on his face.
- While wandering the streets in that state, he became the victim of all kinds of criticism and reproach from nearby citizens who had been incited by Tanktop Tiger and the Tanktop Black Hole brothers, who harbored ill feelings toward Saitama. In the midst of that, the Tanktop brothers tried to go after Saitama in a rookie-hunting attempt, but Saitama subdued them all with a single gesture and then gave a proper rebuke to the citizens who had been denouncing him. However, perhaps recognizing that he should minimize damage to the city, Saitama later completely annihilated the centipede in the hills with a Serious Punch, avoiding a situation that would have harmed the urban area.

## 3.4. Deep Sea King:
- Saitama was fighting off the Sea King's followers that appeared in City J when he saw A-class hero Stinger in trouble and, together with Genos, stepped in. While Saitama and Genos were operating separately, Saitama picked up a cellphone dropped by C-rank #1 hero Licenseless Rider, got a rough idea of the situation, and only arrived just in time when Genos, Licenseless Rider, and several other heroes were about to be killed by the Deep Sea King. Saitama paid his respects to the fallen Licenseless Rider, saying, "Nice fight," and smashed the Deep Sea King with a single punch.
- Everything seemed like it would go smoothly after that, but a citizen in the shelter started agitating others by spouting nonsense that belittled the fallen heroes' abilities and honor. Saitama, catching on, said: "Ahahahahahaha. Well, I'll say I was lucky. Thanks to the other heroes sapping the monster's stamina, I knocked it down super easily~ I'm glad I came late. I didn't do anything but I got all the credit~"
- Saitama played the part of a rotten hero who stole the finishing blow from the Deep Sea King to protect the other heroes' honor. He could easily have become City J's hero just by doing nothing. In fact, the citizen trolling with remarks like "Isn't the Deep Sea King weaker than we thought?" and "In the end, apart from the bald guy, didn't the other heroes do nothing?" was being treated as talking nonsense by others, and even that trolling contained no direct disparagement of Saitama's contribution. Nonetheless, Saitama sacrificed his own honor for his fellow heroes.
- People at the scene were immediately fooled and criticized Saitama, while at the same time reflecting on the other heroes' hard work and realizing the importance of heroes. Even though Saitama was the strongest, his strong sense of justice led him to act this way. As a result, his reputation fell again after the meteor incident — to the point where he received red messages like "Are you even a hero?" However, Licenseless Rider, understanding Saitama's true intentions, sent him a thank-you letter. Still, Saitama's actions here helped him significantly in being promoted to a B-class hero.

## 3.5. Boros:
- Saitama, who had gone to Silver Fang's dojo, is called by the Hero Association and goes to the Association headquarters together with Genos. With the majority of the S-Class heroes gathered, Association executive Sitch relays a prophecy from the Great Prophet Shibabawa saying "The Earth is in danger!", warning that within six months an unprecedented catastrophe unlike ordinary disasters will occur. But no sooner had Sitch finished delivering the message than a gigantic alien spaceship appeared and reduced City A to ruins.
- Saitama immediately punches through the ceiling of the Association headquarters, rushes out, and infiltrates the spaceship hovering over City A. Once inside, Saitama beats up the aliens aboard the ship while searching for the leader of the invading forces. Reaching the ship's command center, he meets Boros, who introduces himself as the leader of the Dark Matter Thieves and the Conqueror of Space, and fights him. After a long back-and-forth battle, Saitama acknowledges Boros as a worthy foe and, for the first time, uses his "Serious Series" to bring Boros down. It was the first instance in which Saitama showed his true power.
- Afterwards, Saitama ignores Tatsumaki's question, "Why are you coming out of the spaceship?" and is sharply rebuked. Thinking of Tatsumaki as a little kid, he tells Genos to do something, but Genos is slammed into a wall by Tatsumaki's psychokinesis. When Saitama grabbed Genos's arm to pull him out, Genos's arm broke and was torn off.

## 3.6. King:
- Saitama happens to witness King, the S-Class Rank 7 hero, and passes the job of killing the robot fiend G4, who came to kill King, to Genos while he himself is at King's house playing games with him. After playing for a long time, Saitama asks why King managed to get away from that robot monster, and when he defeats the fiend that flew into King's house and realizes that King's reputation and combat record are all false, he still worries whether King was hurt.
- At that moment Saitama feels something familiar in King’s demeanor; in fact King and Saitama know each other from before. The old King had been helped by Saitama several times. When attacked by monsters it was Saitama who saved him, and because King was always present where Saitama appeared, people mistakenly thought it was King — not Saitama — who had defeated dragon-level monsters, which is how King rose to S-Class.
- Although it wasn't intentional, he had nonetheless stolen the records and popularity that the other should have had, so once King understood the whole situation he shed tears of repentance and sincerely apologized to Saitama. Saitama, having learned the whole truth, forgave King nonetheless, and seeing that King was anguished by the false honor he’d gained, offered encouragement: "Everyone's hero. Then you should just become strong enough to match that, right?" Thanks to Saitama's kindness, Saitama himself later gets through several tight spots because of King.
- After that, Saitama and King become fairly close — they play games together and even borrow games from each other. Even when Saitama accidentally breaks King's game console or deletes his save file, King seems to forgive him generously.

## 3.7. Fubuki:
- After the Boros battle, Saitama rose to B-Class rank 7 through steady achievements. Because of this, B-Class No.1 Fubuki, the "Blizzard of Hell," even came to his house to recruit him. Of course, since Saitama prefers acting alone, he wasn't keen on joining Fubuki's group and flatly refused, after which Fubuki attacked him.
- But the gap in ability was ridiculously wide, so Fubuki's attack had no effect on Saitama, and while fighting her Saitama even lectured her, saying, "If you rely on the power of a faction, someday you'll meet a monster stronger than you and you won't survive." He then got caught up in the fight between Sonic the Speedster, who came to get revenge on Saitama, and Genos; while watching their fight, he personally intervened when Genos tried to obliterate the area. Saitama then casually wiped Sonic out with his "Serious Series of Repeated Side Jumps" and forced him to retreat. Seeing this, Fubuki realizes Saitama is extremely strong and becomes interested in him.

## 3.8. Garou:
- Later, when Charanko came to Saitama’s house and questioned why Silver Fang had beaten him up and sent him flying, Saitama — unlike Genos, who suspected the culprit was probably Garou, the main instigator of the Hero Hunt — had completely forgotten that he’d met Charanko before. While Genos and the Bang & Bomb brothers were fighting Garou, Saitama was at King’s house playing games; he was doing a little light jab spam, but got caught in King’s endless combo and was almost in a panic.
- Saitama got angry when he heard that Genos and Silver Fang had failed to defeat Garou because of interference from the Monster Association and that Garou had been rescued by the Association — angry that there was someone doing hero hunting and that they weren’t coming after him. He set out to find and blast Garou, saying he’d go buy a cabbage. While eating at a restaurant he left his money behind and started to feel uneasy, and just then Garou ate and left the same restaurant without paying. “What a bad guy! I’ll catch him!” Saitama chased after him and kicked Garou, who was in a nearby park.
- Saitama said, “This guy picked a good time! I’ll forgive him this once! Bye!” Garou was dumbfounded, but since he at least looked like a hero, he attacked — and Saitama sent the attacking Garou flying with a single punch and then took off.
- Later Saitama went back to his place, where Genos, the Bang & Bomb brothers, King, and even Fubuki joined the scramble over his hotpot; without saying anything he complained about people eating his hotpot and that his cabbage was gone because of it.
- After that, the fallout from the clash between the Monster Association and the S-Class heroes reached Saitama’s house, and Saitama barged into the Monster Association ahead of the others to resolve the incident. There he lightly subdued the excessively enlarged Pochi with a normal punch, and on the way ran into Flashy Flash, who’s ranked S-Class #13. Flashy Flash mistook him for a monster and ambushed him, but of course it didn’t work; Flashy Flash, thrown into cognitive dissonance, attacked twice, and Saitama, thinking he was a monster, was about to retaliate until Flashy Flash somehow explained himself and things were barely sorted out.
- Meanwhile, during a confrontation with Psykos, Tatsumaki’s power caused an underground building to forcibly rise and collapse, and Saitama lost time carefully pulling Flashy Flash, whose arm seemed trapped under rubble, out. It turned out it wasn’t an arm trapped but a sword, and annoyed Saitama gave him a playful flick on the forehead which slammed Flashy Flash’s head into a wall and snapped the sword in two.
- Flashy Flash glared ferociously and Saitama was flustered, but Puri-Puri Prisoner, ranked S-Class #17, dug himself up and appeared, and thanks to him they somehow got through and managed to climb back up. On the surface, having watched all the S-Class heroes get utterly overwhelmed by a single Garou, Saitama finally stepped in. But Garou, who had approached a godlike level, underestimated Saitama too much, and Saitama, bored with Garou’s clumsy monster-acting, intended to blow him away.
- Saitama took the first hit, then landed two light punches and, seeing Garou still moving fine, said, “This guy’s pretty tough,” pleased. Garou, however, was actually in terrible pain. After Garou sized up Saitama’s combat skills as beginner-level and began pounding him one-sidedly, Saitama, unsurprisingly, didn’t even feel ticklish; with a genuine table-flip followed by consecutive normal punches with both hands, he overwhelmed Garou.
- Saitama tried to goad Garou by talking about “playing monster,” hoping to draw out his true feelings. When Garou transformed a second time into a devilish form, Saitama literally headbutted him and thoroughly put him in his place. After a few exchanges, Garou became a true monster and — paradoxically — “weaker,” and Saitama, disappointed, lost the will to fight him further. Just as Saitama was about to finish Garou, the recovering heroes urged him to kill Garou, and Garou, declaiming at length about the absurdity of the world’s justice and order, asked Saitama, “Why do you do heroes? For what?”
- Saitama picked at his nose and answered, “For fun.”
- Immediately after, Garou, stunned and grabbing Saitama by the collar, called him bald. Saitama snapped, “For fun, you idiot!” and slammed Garou’s head down, smashing it.
- When the heroes moved to finish off the completely wrecked Garou, Saitama objected, saying that going that far wasn’t necessary.
- Silver Fang then appeared and thanked Saitama for stopping his student’s rampage, saying he’d deal with Garou himself, though in the end he couldn’t. As other heroes pushed to finish Garou off, Silver Fang reproached Garou, asking if losing to him felt good.
- Later, when Garou heard the child he’d saved call him a “real hero,” Saitama said to Garou, “This kid told me what kind of guy you are.” Garou left the scene after hearing that, and after all was over Saitama went home — only to find his house had been destroyed by the aftermath of the chaos.

## 3.9. Tatsumaki:
- After Saitama's fight with Garou, he was promoted to A-Class 39th and moved into the apartment where A-Class heroes live together. But Saitama wasn't home — he'd left his place empty and was rummaging through the rubble around his old ruined house, picking up whatever still-intact daily necessities and appliances he could find. Then Saitama discovered a Pochi who, for some reason, had been reduced to the size of an ordinary puppy; he didn't dispose of it, he just picked it up and stared at it.
- Seeing this, a lone Black Sperm approached, mistakenly thinking Saitama was a nonlethal type. Bringing the Black Sperm and Pochi into the city, the creature proceeded to sweep aside all the defense machines that automatically attack monsters using monster-detection devices. When the defense robots all exploded and the Hero Association asked what had happened, Saitama was about to tell the truth but, when he heard those defense robots cost 9 billion each, he panicked and lied that they had just exploded for no reason.
- Fortunately, King defended Saitama and the problem was resolved. Afterwards, Saitama returned a cracked game console to King and his new rank was announced: A-Class 39th.
- When Saitama returned home, neighboring heroes came by to introduce themselves and show off their quirks. Saitama dissed all three: to Chain Toad he said, "It's boring to put on a mask because you're ordinary," to Butterfly DX he said, "You shed a lot of hair. Don't leave hair in my room," and to Forte he said, "Do you really need to fight to a rhythm?" Angry, Forte challenged him to prove his skill and told him to come outside.
- To fight Forte, Saitama opened the door and, seeing the Pochi and the Black Sperm he'd brought, bet that whoever lost would take care of the two pets. They moved to the fight location; Forte got into a fighting stance, syncing with the music, and Saitama kept yelling that a car was coming, but Forte couldn't hear him over the loud music and was hit by the car and knocked out, so the fight fizzled out.
- Saitama learned the lesson: when a car is coming, don't listen to loud music. The car that hit Forte was driven by members of the Fubuki Group he had met before, and Fubuki — taking advantage of the situation — offered to take Saitama to the Hero Association building to meet the one who destroyed his house. They all came to the vague conclusion of sharing responsibility for raising Pochi and the Black Sperm.
- Saitama was then led by Fubuki to the building where Psykos was imprisoned, but Tatsumaki, who had also noticed Psykos' presence, appeared by destroying the building. It turned out that the one who smashed Saitama's house was Tatsumaki herself. Saitama had followed Fubuki to meet Tatsumaki. In particular, while Saitama saved Psykos — who almost died for no reason — his slippers were the only thing damaged, with a broken strap, as he watched the sister showdown between Tatsumaki and Fubuki.
- As the sisters fought and the monsters imprisoned in the building tried to escape through the collapsing structure, Saitama quickly dealt with them all.
- Afterwards, Saitama grabbed Tatsumaki's hand to stop her from knocking Fubuki out, which provoked her anger. When Tatsumaki asked what his relationship with Fubuki was, Saitama answered that they were just acquaintances, which deflated Fubuki. Tatsumaki then asked why an acquaintance would interfere, and Saitama was about to reply, "Well, you smashed my house," when Tatsumaki attacked him.
- Naturally, the attack did absolutely no damage to Saitama, and Tatsumaki tried to mess with the flow of energy in his body with psychokinesis, but that, too, had no effect. The enormous psychokinetic energy she emitted vanished, and as Tatsumaki stared blankly at Saitama, who was holding her, Saitama explained that fighting inside would injure other people and jumped through the building to take her out of the city.
- No matter what she did, Saitama wouldn’t let go, so Tatsumaki loudly asked if he liked her. Saitama replied that if he let go she'd run off to Fubuki, and when Tatsumaki reacted sensitively to the word "run," saying she wouldn't run from a weak opponent, she calmed down and began to take Saitama seriously once he released his arm.
- Later, while Saitama was breaking a huge shard Tatsumaki threw, a fragment flew toward a passing car; he ran to save it but Tatsumaki saved the car first. Startled by Tatsumaki’s psychokinesis ripping the top, he was flung by her attack. Of course, Saitama wasn't scratched at all; even after being tossed into buildings, colliding with dragon-level monsters, or slammed by rocks and debris, only his clothes were slightly torn.
- Tatsumaki tried to lift him into space with psychokinesis, but that failed too because of Saitama's resistance to psychic power. Frustrated that her attacks had no effect, Tatsumaki asked why he didn't counterattack; Saitama said that a third party getting involved wouldn't solve anything by force and that he'd handle her until she wore herself out, and then hand her over to Fubuki.
- Tatsumaki exploded in rage, "Did you think you couldn't knock me down before I tire?" but an old wound from the previous Monster Association battle flared up and she started bleeding profusely from the head. Saitama genuinely worried about her health and promised he'd face her again once she recovered. Hearing that, Tatsumaki finally asked Saitama's name, and he smiled and told her his.
- In a way, Saitama had kindly given his name to the one who had destroyed his house. As if responding, Tatsumaki gave the brightest smile he'd ever seen and seemed to say, "Let's meet again, Saitama," but in an instant she split the ground and dropped him into a fissure, sealing him inside.

# 4. Saitama's Combat Power:
- Saitama is a transcendently strongest figure in the setting. Even if every non-god person rushed at him at once, they would stand no chance. Saitama trained hard to become a strong hero because he craved the exhilaration, sense of danger, and tension that come from fighting, but in training he became so strong that even the strongest monsters are finished with a single punch, leaving him bored and hollow about hero life.
- Because of his unremarkable appearance, ordinary citizens may be said to be completely unaware of Saitama's combat power, and the number of people who have noticed his strength is very small. Even those few only know that Saitama is far stronger than public evaluations suggest; no one within the Hero Association has perceived Saitama's true combat power.
- Characters of dragon-level or higher like Boros or Garou only know that Saitama is overwhelmingly stronger than themselves, but they do not know exactly how strong he is.
- Saitama's hero rank is low — he only rose to A-class after the Garou arc. Before meeting Genos he didn't even know the Hero Association existed, and because he defeats monsters so instantly and disappears, even after acting as a hero for a long time no one recognized him. Later, even after registering with the Association, perhaps because of his looks, no matter how great his deeds were, the Association, unable to see the situation directly, judged him to have supported an S-class hero and only credited part of the public acknowledgement.
- Some of the achievements for monsters Saitama defeated before joining the Hero Association were accidentally claimed by King, who happened to be nearby, and King unintentionally stole part of Saitama's record. Combined with King's fearsome appearance, he came to be treated as the strongest man on Earth. The only saving grace is that Saitama's level-up magnitude is far higher than other heroes. No hero has leveled up to the extent Saitama has.
- Judging from Saitama's combat scale, he is already beyond being human. His specs are so transcendental that monsters which give S-class heroes a hard time — dragon-level threats — are instantly killed like insects by a mere gesture in front of Saitama, and to create anything resembling a real fight with Saitama it would have to be at least disaster-level dragon-class or higher. Even then, it's usually just Saitama taking an interest and toying with a monster that would normally withstand punches. Against Boros, Saitama was only playing casually, and even against Garou, who received part of a god's power, Saitama did not harbor murderous intent.
- Saitama can kick open a subspace gate or, by changing its direction with his hand, simultaneously crush the surface of a satellite. He's even getting stronger. Until now no one noticed because there was no one comparable to Saitama's combat power. Garou, who received divine power, was the only one who entered a range where he could observe Saitama's growth, but Saitama became so strong so quickly that the gap between them widened completely, and now there is no one who can measure Saitama's growth.
- When Sitch said, "Recently it seems that gods have been increasingly interfering in our dimension, so there might be something calling gods into our dimension," Saitama's back is shown. If a god were to directly intervene in Saitama's world rather than indirectly, the development might proceed differently than before.

## 4.1. Power:
- The shockwave from even Saitama's ordinary punch can easily blow an entire mountain away. Saitama blew away not only the mountain visible behind him but also the distant mountains beyond it.
- Saitama can also lift buildings with ease, and most disaster-level threats and monsters are annihilated by a single ordinary punch that he casually throws.
- When dealing with people, Saitama deliberately controls his strength; he has captured countless monsters but has never killed a human. His accuracy in controlling his power is also superb. If he couldn't control his strength at all, anything he touched would be smashed to pieces.
- The fact that Saitama can live a normal life despite such tremendous strength means he can scale that power down to a regular human level.
- When Saitama and Garou exchanged serious punches, the blast created a dimensional portal and landed on Io, a moon of Jupiter. Also, the one-handed "Serious Table Flip" Saitama used pulverized the moon's entire surface.
- After Saitama's limiter was removed, his combat power skyrocketed exponentially due to the peak of his emotional exaltation, granting him the absurd ability to temporarily disperse half of Jupiter's atmosphere with a mere sneeze. Even after time was reversed, that combat power remained unchanged, so he can now send Garou — who previously exchanged serious punches with him — flying with a single ordinary punch.

## 4.2. Durability:
- No matter what attack hits Saitama, he just brushes off the dust and stands up, and it’s as if only burning a bit of his outfit or cape — which somehow became as tough as his owner — would count as a notable feat.
- Saitama’s durability extends beyond Dragon-level monsters; even Boros, who surpasses Dragon-level and is close to God-level, couldn’t get around it. Saitama’s durability stands out in the fight with Boros: he blocked Boros’s barrage using only his left hand.
- Later, whether he was hit by Boros’s unleashed-power attack that pierced through several thick pillars of the spaceship and drove a kick straight down at insane speed, or by the shockwave from Boros’s Meteoric Burst that burned the surroundings and turned his subordinates to ashes, Saitama was unaffected. Even when Boros kicked him at nearly light speed and sent him flying to the moon, Saitama remained expressionless.
- Saitama casually blocks attacks using bladed weapons. Even when Dragon-level monster Nyaang struck his head with a slashing attack, leaving only a fingernail mark on the back of his head, he was otherwise unfazed.
- Saitama’s costume is also extremely durable: despite Saitama taking countless hits from monster threats of high Dragon-level or above, it remains intact. His casual clothes are portrayed more realistically — getting burned by Genos’s incineration cannon or ripped by Tatsumaki’s telekinesis — but only Saitama’s hero costume is unusually sturdy. Recently, however, the costume has suffered more wear, with the cape and other parts getting torn.
- When Saitama was training as a hero without having fully released his limiter, he showed that he could get hurt by monsters or fought while dodging and bore many small and large wounds. But when he fully released the limiter in battle with a monster, a future Saitama often appears with a dopey look on his face and immediately pulverizes them.
- Even before he was fully awakened as a normal civilian, Saitama had tremendous endurance. In middle school a monster slammed him into a concrete wall hard enough to leave a dent, and Saitama only suffered bruises over his body and was unconscious for a few hours — no fractures, not a drop of blood. When Saitama faced Carnylante, he got thoroughly beaten by a monster that an A-rank hero should handle, yet it didn’t impair his movement at all.
- Saitama’s tolerance to heat is also remarkable: even while in lava he wears a very comfortable expression as if he were bathing.
- When Saitama fought Mosquito Girl, he was hit squarely by Genos’s incineration cannon; only his clothes burned while his body remained fine.
- Saitama is also immune to radiation. When almost every hero was knocked out by Garou’s cosmic radiation, only Blast, Genos, and Saitama were the three who kept fighting with no ill effects, and even on Io, a moon of Jupiter that emits enormous radiation, Saitama remained fine alongside Garou — Garou himself was astonished and wondered if this guy was really human.
- Garou mentioned that muscles can be trained but internal organs cannot, and struck Saitama squarely with a technique called Daebal-gyeong intending to destroy his innards, yet Saitama was still unaffected.
- Later, Saitama took damage for the first time from Garou’s fist. More precisely, it was Garou’s fist after copying Saitama’s power by receiving divine strength, so it was like being hurt by his own punch. When they exchanged Serious Punches and landed cross-counters, objects presumed to be blood or saliva sprayed from their mouths — if it was blood then it was obviously an injury, and even if it was saliva, the fact that they spat it out shows they felt impact — so the record that he was completely immune to damage in any circumstance was broken.
- But Saitama soon grew beyond that level of power. In other words, whenever Saitama grows indefinitely, all his physical abilities — strength, speed, durability, tracking vision, reflexes, etc. — increase together, meaning he becomes immune to damage from anything that is not at least as powerful as the present version of himself.

## 4.3. Speed:
- Saitama not only easily surpasses the speed of sound, he's estimated to be close to the speed of light. It's not just raw speed — his reaction time, dynamic vision, and decision speed are also so fast that he can catch Sonic the Speed or Flash the Flash's attacks with his hand or bite and shatter them. Sonic's special technique "Ten Shadows" produces ten clones, but Saitama, by genuinely repeating his runs, produced countless afterimages.
- Saitama is much faster than Flash, who is generally regarded as "the fastest hero," and than Sonic, who is motivated similar to Flash; even Garou, who moves so fast that other S-class heroes can't see him, is far slower than Saitama.
- Boros, who came close to god-level with Garou, appeared to trade plausible blows with Saitama, but considering that Saitama was mostly just taking Boros' attacks until the very end and not actively engaging, it's closer to Saitama matching Boros' pace than Boros catching up. Silver Fang, a very seasoned martial artist with top-tier reflexes among S-class heroes, either moved faster than Saitama's reaction speed or—when Saitama moved in earnest—felt his life was threatened and dodged the giant mallet. Of course, that was only when he actually detected the opponent's movement; had Saitama been serious, Silver Fang likely would have been hit before he even perceived it.
- When Genos had a mock battle with him, at first Genos seemed to match Saitama's speed thanks to upgraded parts, but in reality what Genos had been chasing the whole time was only Saitama's afterimage, and that was merely Saitama toying with him. When Saitama got even a little serious, Genos couldn't properly perceive or react and felt a premonition of death.
- When Saitama first met Blast, he showed no sign of surprise even though Blast completely outpaced Flash in speed. Saitama's reactions vary by situation, but the fact that he felt little to no impression seeing Blast, who overwhelmingly surpassed Flash, means that the speeds of both Flash and Blast were not enough to pique Saitama's interest.
- Even Saitama's most basic speed when dispatching monsters is on par with Flash, the fastest hero. Because that's his baseline, he can easily go much faster, and as in the Blast example, speeds so subtle that Flash can't perceive them are trivial for Saitama.
- Saitama has no flight ability, but with tremendous jumping power he can leap high and fast as if flying. He jumped up to and down from the 22nd-floor window of King's apartment, and during the meteor incident he jumped from a rooftop and smashed a giant meteor over the city into pieces with a launched punch. He also jumped into Boros' airborne battleship, and when he went to the moon, a single jump brought him back to Earth in an instant. Given his strength, it's also possible that after leaping into the air he could generate thrust by punches or kicks to propel himself.
- But for some reason, Saitama can't catch mosquitoes.

## 4.4. Mental fortitude:
- Even before Saitama removed his limiter, he didn't stop training even when he was spewing blood and his joints sounded like they were tearing; to train his mental strength he didn't use air conditioning in summer or heating in winter. It's said Saitama was even satisfied with just a single banana for breakfast. Flashbacks show that, while enduring an excessive training schedule, he still went out every day to fight monsters and continue training.
- Saitama endures the cries and blame of the civilians he saved and instead takes on criticism aimed at other heroes. He belittles himself to shield the efforts and sacrifices of other heroes, and even magnanimously forgives King, who accidentally stole his credit — maintaining a very composed attitude in situations that would make an ordinary person furious.
- Resistance to psychic powers is proportional to a person's mental strength, so the psychics thrown by Geryuganshoop or Fubuki naturally had no effect on Saitama. When the strongest esper, Tatsumaki, tried to shake Saitama's internal ki and make him faint, Saitama was merely amused, saying his muscles were moving on their own, and felt no effect. When her attacks failed to work she tried to send Saitama into space but couldn't lift him, and as a side effect of using too much power Tatsumaki's recently sustained wound reopened and she coughed up blood.
- Early on, after seeing Tatsumaki try to shake Saitama's qi to knock him out, Fubuki said it was as futile as trying to apply a joint lock to a rock.
- In the battle between Phoenix Man and Dongje, Saitama smashed into the mental world Phoenix Man had created from the outside in order to move Dongje. And he, as if it were the most natural thing, picked up Cosmic Garou's space-warping portal with his hands and changed its direction.
- According to Garou and Tatsumaki, a human with this level of strength should normally be insane. Saitama receives criticism about his mental state both inside and outside the story, but from his perspective he's essentially lost the meaning of life, and an ordinary person would not be at all surprised if that alone had already driven them to turn dark.
- Considering that Saitama is, in effect, the strongest being whom no one can oppose, the fact that he wields that transcendent power only for justice and not for personal gain, even while feeling such emptiness and nihilism, shows just how enormous his mental fortitude and sense of justice are.

## 4.5. Perceptual Abilities:
- Saitama's eyesight is so good that he can distinguish objects at midnight as if it were daytime, and with this excellent vision he once saw through Garou's bluff and flustered him.
- Saitama also has very good dynamic visual acuity and hearing. He saw Psykos being pulled upward by Tatsumaki's psychokinesis, whereas Flashy Flash didn't even register what it was. When Garou made his escape, the other heroes were confused and couldn't find the direction he fled, but Saitama was the only one looking up.
- However, Saitama is so strong that he lacks a sense of danger, so in his everyday life he apparently doesn't use this perception for hazard detection. He doesn't notice an enemy's presence until the opponent appears right in front of him. He's so powerful that there's no need to detect enemies in advance.
- When Saitama was doing hero work as a hobby, he would leave the TV on all day and, when the news reported that a monster had appeared, he'd watch and head out.
- Usually other heroes or monsters sense an opponent's strength with their perception and stay on guard, or intuitively detect the appearance of a dangerous enemy at a distance, but in Saitama's case, because the gap in power is so extreme—like that between a professional fighter and an ant—he's not easily detected. Because of this he was sometimes ambushed. Even in a dream, when a powerful enemy capable of injuring Saitama invaded, he sensed it and shot awake.
- In addition, during his fight with Garou he sensed something with the thought "Something's coming," and he also perceived and intruded into Phoenix Man's mental world; so although he doesn't use it often, his perceptual abilities themselves are extremely strong. No matter how weak the opponent, if he puts his mind to it he can detect anything.

## 4.6. Disregard for the Laws of Physics:
- Perhaps because Saitama is transcendent both mentally and physically, he breaks into Phoenix Man's mental world, or kicks a two-dimensional gate created by Cosmic Garou like it's a disc, or grabs it with his hand and spins it — accomplishing things that would be physically impossible by sheer force alone with just his bare body.
- Tatsumaki's psychokinesis also hardly affected Saitama; even outputs intended to send him into outer space only lifted him a few centimeters off the ground.
- Mental or spatial attacks have no effect on Saitama at all, so even if someone with a special ability appears, it seems possible he could just shrug them off bare-handed and finish them with punches.
- Saitama can also operate in outer space. Not by tricks like holding his breath for a long time, but while continuing a prolonged fight with Garou on Io, a moon of Jupiter, he casually converses with Garou and nonchalantly ignores Jupiter's enormous gravity with strength beyond imagination.
- There was a scene where Garou, having received divine power, was hit by Saitama's Zero Punch and lost that power; the Void in midair struck by Saitama's fist also lost its divine power and reverted back to human form.

## 4.7. Growth potential:
- Saitama's growth rate is also abnormal. Because his usual enemies were nothing but fodder that he could beat with one hand, he had almost no growth, but after the decisive battle with Garou — who had been awakened by a god — and the emotional surge he had never experienced before, Saitama began to get progressively stronger. This is depicted as his growth curve becoming essentially exponential, and the gap between him and Garou widening into an abyss. Eventually Saitama reaches the point where, instead of exchanging genuine punches with Garou, he toys with him playfully as he normally does with other monsters.
- Garou used a "mode" that copies a target's physical abilities to obtain specs equal to Saitama's, so as Saitama grew, Garou's body became stronger in turn. Considering that on the growth-gap graph Garou’s specs sit at the same point as Saitama’s specs from just before, it means that in that brief moment when Garou copied Saitama’s stats with his mode, Saitama had already become far stronger due to an overwhelming growth rate — and this widening gap repeated itself.
- Even Cosmic Fear Garou, who was the only one able to observe Saitama’s growth by copying it with his mode, ultimately could not determine where Saitama stood because the difference became so vast. With this overwhelming growth, Saitama broke Garou’s spirit and brought him to his senses; he then easily received the ultimate technique that even Garou empowered by a god couldn’t reach, traveled back to the past, and dealt with the past Garou with an ordinary punch, restoring all the havoc the awakened Garou had caused back to its original state.
- Because of Saitama’s abnormal growth rate and the fact that there is no limit to the amount he can grow, defeating Saitama would require either having a gap large enough to beat ordinary Saitama — who has overwhelming combat power — within two or three blows, or possessing a growth speed so extreme that each new moment leaves your immediately preceding self utterly outclassed while having no limit to your own ceiling from the start. In short, truly absurd conditions would have to be met.

## 4.8. The Secret of Combat Power:
- Saitama himself revealed how he got stronger as follows. He said that after training like this for three years he suddenly went bald and became strong. Saitama: "100 push-ups, 100 sit-ups, 100 squats. And a 10 km run. Do this every day!"
- Saitama: "Of course, eat three full meals a day. Breakfast can even be a banana. Another important thing is to train your mind, so whether it's summer or winter you don't use air conditioning or heating. At first it'll feel unbearable. You'll want to take a day off. But no matter how hard it was, to become a strong hero I kept going every day, even if it made me cough up blood. Even when my legs felt heavy and I couldn't move, I'd do squats; even when my arms made strange creaking noises, I'd do push-ups. I noticed the change after a year and a half. I had gone bald. And I was stronger. In short, train yourself until you almost go bald. That's the only way to get strong. You, who joke about new humanity or evolution, will never reach this. The ability to change yourself is what makes humans strong!"
- Because it was a story from Saitama's own experience he said it very seriously, but of course no one there, including Genos, believed him. Even afterward Genos couldn't accept it and thought, "There is a secret to strength that can't be explained."
- Later, Dr. Genus's limiter theory proved it to be true. Saitama only listed his training regimen; he did not logically understand the principle by which he became strong. To be precise, that amount of exercise is certainly a bit excessive for an ordinary person to follow.
- Saitama emphasized the 'guts' of doing them unconditionally even when his legs were heavy and his arms made strange noises.
- Each individual has a limit to how strong they can become, which Dr. Genus named the "limiter." Heroes or monsters may be born with a high limiter and thus have the potential to become strong, or their limiter cap can be raised by mutation or body modification. To put it simply: a person whose level cap is 10 and a person whose cap is 20 may seem similar at level 10, but if they keep gaining experience the former will stay at level 10 while the latter can rise to 20 and become much stronger. Powerful monsters or heroes have high level caps, so it's possible for them to raise their level up to those caps and become even stronger.
- Most are defeated and die before reaching Dragon-level, or their growth stalls because they can't withstand the stress and suffer cognitive regression. Even Orochi, the Monster King, who could be called a successful test subject of Psykos, shows extreme lethargy and passivity and said his sense of self was fading.
- One of the differences between Saitama and other strong individuals is "ideals" and "desires." Saitama's ideal is "an invincible hero who can defeat any monster in one blow," and he has the desire to fight strong opponents and have tense battles.
- The only person who has reached Saitama's ideal is roughly Mumen Rider, but Mumen Rider's sense of justice is so strong that he has almost no desire to become strong for selfish reasons. Because Mumen Rider also has high ideals, it's certain he would train to the point of spitting blood like Saitama, but Saitama has a battle-crazed side that seeks the exhilaration of fighting strong opponents. This is the biggest difference between Saitama and Mumen Rider. These selfish desires made Saitama the strongest, but conversely are also the reason he sometimes behaves in ways that make people wonder, "Is he really a hero?"

## 4.9. People who know Saitama's strength:
- He defeated all kinds of monsters and he didn't hide that while operating, but Saitama didn't exactly go around boasting about it either, and because of cognitive dissonance he had virtually no recognition and no one noticed how strong he was. Afterward, starting with Genos, the number of people who recognized his strength gradually increased to over twenty.
- However, most people only caught on to the fact that Saitama was very strong in a fragmentary way; few realized that he was overwhelmingly the strongest in the world, and practically no one — including Saitama himself — knows exactly how strong he is.
- There are hero ranks and disaster levels, but even within the same rank the gaps can be like night and day, and since these are viewed from the perspective of ordinary people they are prone to error.
- The only means that exist are each character's track record and the experience gained by fighting them directly. Therefore, the usual ways people sense Saitama's strength are either by fighting him when he half‑heartedly toys with them and realizing the gap, or by Saitama casually defeating a monster that someone else lost to, leading them to perceive Saitama as far more powerful than they are. These methods only show that Saitama is extremely powerful; they cannot reveal that he is literally the strongest in the entire setting.

## 4.10. Techniques:   
- Saitama puts 'Normal' or 'Serious' in front and then improvises the name of the technique. 'Normal' is attached to techniques that are weaker than 'Serious', and 'Serious' is attached to techniques that are stronger than 'Normal'. 
- Examples of Saitama's technique names include 'Normal Punch', 'Serious Punch', 'Consecutive Normal Punch', and so on. In fact, there's nothing particularly special about them — they're just names given to ordinary things like a punch or a headbutt that a regular person could do. It's just that Saitama's physicality is so great that those techniques are overwhelmingly stronger than the all-out strikes of most strong opponents.

### 4.10.1. Normal Series:
- These are the basic punches and kicks Saitama usually uses when fighting monsters; in the game they function as basic attacks. Though called the 'Normal Series,' for Saitama they're essentially ordinary attacks with a bit more force and are hard to call techniques.

#### 4.10.1.1. Normal Punch:
- Saitama's standard attack. In terms of fighting games it's the weakest basic attack, like a light punch — he just lazily swings his fist without much else.
- The attack itself is nothing special: a telephone punch, or rather just a punch with no hip power, but this seemingly trivial strike causes prominent monsters to explode to the point that their bodies are unrecognizable with a single hit. If he strikes with the intent to kill, it's brutally destructive, annihilating targets equally from the low ranks up to the top tier of Dragon-class.
- The only monsters who didn't die from a single normal punch were the excessively enlarged Pochi, Evil Natural Water, Monster King Orochi, Boros, and Garou — and even those only survived, they all suffered severe damage. Enemies who can take this and keep fighting are ones Saitama himself admits are "pretty strong" or "fairly sturdy."
- Saitama adjusts his strength depending on the opponent: if the target is human, it usually just knocks them out without external injury. If he judges the opponent to be a monster/kaiju, a normal punch will definitely one-shot up to Dragon-class.

#### 4.10.1.2. Consecutive Normal Punches:
- He fires normal punches consecutively with one hand. For Saitama it's nothing more than a one-handed little trick, but though it's called consecutive punches, even a single normal punch can blow most monsters to bits, and he throws them in succession — at speeds so fast they're almost invisible — so the technique boasts terrifying power. Naturally, the Deep Sea King who took this was reduced to nothing but meat porridge, and Boros was smashed so hard his brain was exposed and his body was torn to rags.
- When Garou took Saitama's consecutive normal punches his whole body was shattered and he suffered tremendous injuries. When used against Boros the fists were so fast they wrapped around and ground his entire body, Boros exploded into a spray of blood, and part of the spaceship behind them was destroyed.
- Saitama generally tries one-handed or two-handed consecutive normal punches against opponents who can withstand normal punches; if they endure even that he brings out the Serious Series. Also, when the Deep Sea King destroyed Saitama's house he fired consecutive normal punches, which suggests he was angry but hadn't lost his composure enough to unleash the Serious Series.

#### 4.10.1.3. Dual-Hand Consecutive Normal Punches:
- Performs consecutive normal punches with both hands simultaneously. When Garou unleashed his special move, Shin-Sal-Sun-Gyeok-Kwon, Saitama used this as a counter, overwhelming the Shin-Sal-Sun-Gyeok-Kwon head-on and one-sidedly pummeling Garou.
- Although Saitama used it on Garou, Garou—having received God's power and thus able to replicate even the magnitude of strength—copied the consecutive normal punches and traded blows on equal footing; with cross counters they only knocked each other into the water and failed to inflict any meaningful damage.

### 4.10.2. Finishing "Serious" Series:
- These are techniques Saitama uses when he takes a fight seriously. So far he doesn't use them because he feels "I have to use this to win," but more with a "this guy's something, maybe I should get a little serious" kind of feeling. In other words, Saitama using the Serious series means the opponent (the one hit) has earned a certain level of acknowledgement from him.
- From Saitama's standpoint, if a normal punch is more like a casual tap, a Serious Punch is more like taking proper aim and actually "hitting." So it's easy to understand if you think of a normal punch as a jab and a Serious Punch as a straight. Strictly speaking, it should be seen as closer to "a sort of seriousness."
- Unlike the normal series, when using the Serious series he announces it as the finishing "Serious" series before the move name. The number of times Saitama has used it can be counted on one hand. Even then, moves like "Serious Repeated Side Hop" or "Serious Table Flip" and the "Serious Punch" used on Boros were not direct attacks, and in the case of "Serious Headbutt" Saitama used it on the opponent's right arm rather than their torso or head.

#### 4.10.2.1. Serious Punch:
- A technique where Saitama gets serious, takes a stance, and throws a punch. Compared to a normal punch, it's actually just a slightly harder telephone punch, but its power is so overwhelmingly strong that it's used as Saitama's hidden card. It means that whereas Saitama usually crushes most opponents with a casual little tap, this delivers a level of impact that's more than merely "hitting" — it truly "strikes."

#### 4.10.2.2. Serious Side-to-Side Repetitive Running:
- Saitama genuinely runs repeatedly from side to side. It looks like an ordinary sidestep movement, probably done to build explosiveness and lower-body strength, but its speed is so great that countless afterimages and sonic booms occur. A technique used in response to Sonic's clone technique. He showed it because, when he dealt with him casually, Sonic kept trying to climb on him. Sonic, who was lightly brushed by Saitama's side-run, was battered by the shockwave and collapsed.

#### 4.10.2.3. Serious Table Flip:
- A technique in which Saitama grabs the ground and flips it with a motion like flipping a low table; it goes beyond merely overturning the surface and flips entire city-scale terrain, launching anyone on it so high they can't even gauge the height.
- From a third party's perspective it plays out as if a gigantic wall suddenly appears. Garou, swept up by this technique, spent a long time smashing rocks as he flew and didn't fall even after trading blows and brawling with Saitama. Garou at first thought it was nothing special, but soon realized he couldn't see the ground and was horrified. Later, during the fight, he leapt across the rocks to continue fighting and then ended up crashing into the ground. In fact, he had flown so high that his sense of space became distorted and he mistook the ground for the sky and leaped. Struggling in the air, Garou described himself as "a bug in a basket being toyed with by a child."
- Saitama has no wings or boosters and can't use psychic powers, so he cannot fly, but by using this technique he can lift ground and rock very high—high enough that the surface is no longer visible—and fight in the air by stepping on those rock fragments, jumping and moving about.

#### 4.10.2.4. Serious Headbutt:
- A headbutt Saitama delivers in earnest. It collided head-on with the punch of Garou, who had become a monster, and pulverized even Garou's shoulder blades without a trace. However, because Garou had become a monster, he quickly recovered from the damage itself.

#### 4.10.2.4. Serious Water Gun:
- A technique where Saitama fires water with his hand. It's literally that prank of gathering water in your hand at a bathhouse or swimming pool and squirting it at someone, and, given who the caster is, it displays enough power to offset Orochi's Gaia Cannon.

#### 4.10.2.5. Serious Sneeze:
- Literally a sneeze. By the name alone it sounds like a gimmicky technique, but after the slugfest with Garou he had absurdly grown, and it displayed tremendous power that blew away half of Jupiter’s gaseous mantle.
- That mere sneeze demonstrated power far exceeding the Serious Punch Saitama used on Boros, Elder Centipede, and Evil Sea Water, showing how transcendent Saitama’s rate of growth is.

#### 4.10.2.6. Zero Punch:
- The ultimate finishing move Saitama unveiled to crush the ominous future after repeatedly fighting Garou on Eo and undergoing exponential growth to become a planet-destroying–class powerhouse. The punch thrown then was delivered while reversing time, and because it was a technique sent from the future toward the past, Cosmic Fear Garou, who had just finished transforming, could not possibly react. The punch shattered the shell that had wrapped his face, broke his cosmic state, and instantly neutralized him.

### 4.10.3. Other techniques:
#### 4.10.3.1. Fart:
- Garou used his Nuclear Fission Fist to block Saitama's view and quickly opened a portal to return to Earth alone, but because he had been holding in farts due to the cold, a sudden flash of light made him relax and the fart that slipped out was used as propulsion, so before the portal closed they instantaneously returned to Earth together.

#### 4.10.3.2. Time Regression:
- Using the secret art that Garou — having gained godlike power — judged he could not make his own, which originally seems to require divine power, Saitama, true to his rule-defying nature, succeeds in perfectly imitating it by combining Garou’s menkyo-kaiden and his own strength.
- Time Regression is so far the only technique Saitama learned from someone else. It is also the first of the techniques Saitama has used that can truly be called a “technique.” And Saitama, who usually finds most things boring, genuinely admired this secret art as impressive.
- Time Regression is a very meaningful technique for Saitama: before rewinding time, the situation on Earth had many people, including S-Class heroes, exposed to radiation from Cosmic Fear Garou and they were either dead or close to death. Saitama could not undo this disaster with his power alone, and thus even if he defeated Garou, it would have been hard to call it a victory for heroes as a whole, even if it was for Saitama personally.
- It is a landmark technique in that Saitama, who always solved things alone with overwhelming power, this time resolved an irreparable catastrophe with others’ help.

# 5. Saitama's Personality and Intelligence:
- Physically, Saitama has reached a level that no one can rival, but compared to that, his knowledge and intelligence are nothing special. Before meeting Genos he was so ignorant of the world that he didn't even know the Hero Association existed; when he joined the Association he placed first by an overwhelming margin in the physical exam, but scored poorly on the written test—receiving a C-class—because of terrible writing and essay skills.
In fact, his ignorance of the world is less a matter of intelligence than a matter of lack of interest and being too lazy to acquire information.
- Saitama shows that he doesn't properly remember people's names. This is because he's naturally slapdash about everything and has little interest in others. For the same reason, he really hates being nagged or listening to long stories.
- Saitama's interpersonal relations are not good, even by any stretch. To be precise, he's at the level of lacking social skills. Occasionally situations arise where Saitama opposes other heroes; the opposing parties aren't always blameless, but in many cases the primary cause lies with Saitama. Because of this, those who frequently hang out with Saitama are either so magnanimous that they easily overlook his rudeness, or are as much "my-way" types as Saitama and therefore don't care about his attitude. Still, aside from someone like King, there isn't anyone among them who could truly be called a friend, and Saitama thinks of all the people around him only as acquaintances.
- However, before he removed his limiter he endured grueling training to defeat monsters, had the ambition to be a hero and a strong sense of justice, and even now he still shows a basic sense of justice. For example, he has said he "does hero work as a hobby," meaning he acts regardless of reputation, rank, or popularity; in the Deep Sea King arc he even lowered his own reputation to protect the honor of other heroes, demonstrating magnanimity and a pure fidelity to the essence of heroism.
- Consequently, there are many characters—such as Genos, Tatsumaki, and Garou—who are inspired by his firm values; the fact that so many are influenced by Saitama's philosophy ultimately means that Saitama is an ideal hero.
- Overall, from the perspective of civilians who aren't directly connected to Saitama and who would be saved by him from threats like monsters, Saitama is a perfect and ideal hero. But from the perspective of acquaintances who face him directly and form relationships with him, he lacks social skills, though he is such an outstanding person that they can overlook that trait as merely part of his personality.

# 6. Saitama's Character and Philosophy:
## 6.1. Indifference and Irresponsibility:
- Saitama tends to speak bluntly and doesn't pay much attention to other people's words or feelings.
- He is also bad at reading the mood; when Garou delivers a passionate speech, Saitama picks his nose and calls it a hobby, or says Garou is stubborn with too strong opinions and can't bend, showing a stubborn streak.
- As a student, Saitama even worried about himself, wondering if he might not be able to function in working life later.
- Before being tactless, Saitama displays a general lack of empathy. Because of this personality, he was always alone in school. While confronting Kanirante to save a kid with a split jaw, he threatened to make him never get a job again and held pincers to his face, but then laughed and made a non sequitur that he looked like a villain from a cartoon he saw as a kid, and got beaten up for it.
- Saitama's biggest problem is that he is too indifferent and irresponsible in everything. Before meeting Genos he hadn’t even registered as a pro hero and was just doing hero work on his own; when he learned registration existed he signed up, but even after passing he showed a delinquent attitude at a Hero Association information seminar hosted by Snek (A-rank 37 of the social circle), picking his ear and chewing gum.
- Also, Saitama, fanatically promoting his own ideal type of hero, is very indifferent to other absurdities outside of hero matters; when Vaccine Man claimed to be born as the "will of the Earth" and, disgusted by human environmental pollution, sought to annihilate humanity, Saitama killed him without any logical rebuttal.
- The peak of Saitama's irresponsibility was when he dealt with Marugori: when he knocked Marugori down, the giant's collapse destroyed part of City B, yet Saitama's reaction was only a single "Ah."
- This sloppy attitude toward everything continually causes problems for himself: he didn't even know basic pro-hero facts like "C-class heroes are kicked out if a week passes without achievements" or "in the hero promotion exam you can decide whether you move up to the next rank or stay in your current one." Those things are written in the manual given when you pass, but he doesn't know where he put it and was too lazy to read it.
- Worse, Saitama has wanted to be a hero since long ago and had been working alone as a hero, yet he didn't even know what disaster levels were. Having continued hero activities, these tendencies have somewhat decreased compared to the very early days, and public perception of him appears to have improved somewhat compared to his initial hero period.
- In fact, there is no particular depiction of Saitama being widely cursed at by citizens after the Meteor arc and the Deep Sea King. Above all, Saitama's indifference and irresponsibility are mostly limited to areas far removed from the pursuit of justice.

## 6.2. Heroism and Nobility:
- Because Saitama goes around saying he’s a hero as a hobby with an offhand impression, he can at first glance seem to have a sloppy sense of justice, but paradoxically this is the phrase that best expresses Saitama’s sense of justice.
- When Saitama says “hobby,” it doesn’t mean doing things sloppily; it means doing something purely because he wants to, not to gain someone’s attention, social recognition, honor or power, or wealth or anything else. That’s also why he doesn’t try to make money from hero work and why he doesn’t care if the public hates him.
- Therefore you can see that Saitama has a firm personal ideology and very clear beliefs about the job of a hero. It’s almost on the level of Mumen Rider. Looking at his occasional maxims, such as “If a hero runs away, who fights?”, he seems to set a high bar for what it means to be a hero.
- Saitama literally dreams of an “ideal” hero, and he even said to Garou something like, “A monster’s goal is simply to defeat a hero, so that’s easy, but a hero’s goal is to protect world peace, so it’s much harder.”
- And although Saitama rarely loses his temper, he genuinely snapped at Hellish Fubuki, who formed cliques and preyed on rookies, saying, “Don’t belittle heroes, you idiot!” Because of Saitama’s sense of justice he and Mumen Rider became people who can smile and get along with each other. In fact, the first person Saitama smiled at was Mumen Rider.
- Therefore metrics valued by the Hero Association and heroes belonging to it — like achievements or popularity — have nothing whatsoever to do with Saitama’s personal idea of a hero. When Saitama says “hobby,” it should be understood as pursuing his ideals without expecting a reward. Actions done in hope of or to receive compensation are no longer a hobby but belong to the realm of a job.
- Also, perhaps because Saitama has clearly received just rewards for his efforts, he seems unable to understand people who feel inferior or who try to become stronger by cheating. For the sake of the honor of police officers who were being looked down on because of heroes, he once disguised himself as a cop and, hiding his identity, defeated a monster.
- Saitama’s belief as a hero is clear, but it isn’t a grand ideology — it’s simply beating up monsters whenever they appear.
- Because of Saitama’s characteristically indifferent personality, he generally avoids interfering in other people’s affairs, but if he feels something is unjust even if it doesn’t concern him, he steps in. That’s why he restored the police’s honor, and in the Tatsumaki arc he personally tried to resolve the conflict between Tatsumaki and Fubuki.
- Saitama kills monsters he attacks without mercy, but he doesn’t bother killing monsters that haven’t caused much harm. He only subdued Namekujaras and Frog Man, who were dispatched from the House of Evolution, and didn’t touch Armored Gorilla, who gave him information. Saitama believes that even if a monster is hostile, if it surrenders first there’s no need to kill it. For that reason he was once mistaken by Black Sperm for a no-kill believer. And if it’s a human who rushes at him to kill him, he stops at neutralizing them.
- Likewise, Saitama doesn’t dislike monsters who haven’t lost their humanity. Garou wanted to become a monster from childhood, but in truth his desire to be a hero had been twisted by bullying and a teacher’s warped thinking. After hearing Saitama, Garou chose to be human rather than a monster, and despite warnings from those around him to kill, Saitama simply spared him.
- In Amai Mask’s case, Saitama at first thought he was an annoying guy, but because Amai Mask exposed himself as a monster in order to properly deal with other monsters and reduce casualties, his image shifted from annoying to admirable. When Amai Mask told him to kill him and become famous, Saitama said, “You do it,” and flew off with him into the sky, giving him another chance.
- Saitama let Tatsumaki and Hellish Fubuki, who attacked him with considerable force, go without retaliating and without any grudge.
- Saitama is basically close to an ordinary person with a petty-bourgeois sense of daily life, but because he’s almost immune to any physical threat, he seems to lack stimuli to live life passionately. He didn’t become a hero to pursue strength, and he only became strong as a result of training to become a hero because fighting was fun. Before becoming a hero he had illusions about what being a hero would be like, and by becoming excessively powerful he lost the chance to experience the intense emotions he thought he would get from it.
- When he first faced the crab monster Carnifugar, Saitama was an unemployed job-seeker with no will to live. Carnifugar even didn’t harm him, saying Saitama had the same dead eyes as himself. He clearly had a personality not well suited to ordinary society, and even when his life was in danger he didn’t show much emotional disturbance.
- Yet the one thing Saitama did put real will into was throwing his body to save a boy from Carnifugar. Even though he might have died, he fought with all his strength and defeated Carnifugar. It was something he accomplished as an ordinary citizen, not as Saitama with absolute power.

## 6.3. Emptiness:
- Saitama has a pronounced battle-obsessed streak: he wants fights where he struggles fiercely, pushing himself to the limit against monsters, but unfortunately he’s so strong that there are no enemies who can constitute a real fight with him. 
- Because of this, Saitama has lost the will to live and spends hollow, meaningless days. 
- However, King struck a chord with Saitama who said life felt empty because he was too strong, admonishing him for an arrogant remark. The gist was, "You just grumble that you're bored, but you don't do anything proactive to overcome it, do you?" — a comment that hit the nail on the head and left Saitama speechless. He was also criticized that wanting to meet a strong opponent just to fight runs counter to the essence of a hero. 
- Saitama's reason for getting stronger was that he wanted high-level fights against stronger opponents, so he trained for three years; the result was that only Saitama himself became excessively strong, leaving no opponents matching his level and causing him to feel alienated. 
- The reason Saitama leads a very modest, quiet life is also that, aside from wanting fights with strong opponents, he has little interest in anything else — he lives an ordinary, non-materialistic civilian life without seeking honor or money. 
- Saitama becomes serious not when fighting fairly strong foes like Boros or Garou, but when protecting someone or when discussing the creed of a hero. Even in the dream duel with Jijeo-wang he shouted, "I'll protect the Earth!" proclaiming his identity, and to the hellish Fubuki he cried out in an agitated tone, "Don't look down on heroes." 
- The emptiness is understandable, but as King said, Saitama isn't trying anything else to resolve it. Even if other hobbies or pursuits wouldn't fully relieve his emptiness, the fact that he doesn't even try is certainly open to criticism. 
- There's more to enjoy in life than just fighting. Also, even if Saitama doesn't make grand self-sacrifices, his activities unquestionably save countless citizens' lives, and strictly speaking that is closer to the essence of a hero. Whether he had an intense fight or not is ultimately just an additional form of personal satisfaction. 
- Saitama is actually aware of this himself and sometimes falls into the thought, "I've forgotten something important about being a hero." Over time his emptiness grew his desire to fight ever-stronger opponents, and that caused him to forget the original essence of heroism he first dreamed of. King's remark that Saitama might be deluding himself into thinking growth is finished simply because he's become strong is accurate. 
- In short, the cause of Saitama's emptiness is that he doesn't feel a sense of achievement. King correctly pointed out that by vaguely aiming to become the greatest hero and training so hard, Saitama became so strong that he can't feel accomplishment. Conversely, those who were genuinely moved by Saitama and reformed are people who, thanks to him, found new goals. 
- Another major problem is Saitama's lack of communication with others. He diligently fought monsters but never properly conversed with citizens. Even when he tries to talk, he listens half-heartedly and often appears rude from others' viewpoints. 
- It's also a big problem that when Saitama speaks he only spouts his own thoughts and doesn't try to really listen to others. His view of heroism as nothing more than monster extermination is also an issue. For example, Mumen Rider (the unlicensed rider) is much weaker than Saitama but is more popular because of his small-citizen-style hero activities. 
- From this perspective, Saitama doesn't try to find hero activities he can do besides monster extermination. At least since meeting King, however, he has somewhat increased his communication compared to before.

# 8. Saitama's Relationships:
- Because of his peculiar personality, Saitama has never formed a sincere, deep relationship with another person in his life. Even before his awakening led to a personality reflecting that emptiness, Saitama was not the type to put much effort into interpersonal relationships.
- A considerable number of powerful people with problematic personalities who had direct contact with Saitama were influenced by him in a positive way. Tatsumaki and Amai Mask are examples, and Genos has said that before meeting Saitama he had a personality like Amai Mask’s, but he was moved by him and changed.
- Saitama himself doesn’t consider himself unusually close or friendly with anyone besides Genos, Mumen Rider, and King. Still, Saitama tends to treat others in a reasonably open and easygoing way, though his standards may be somewhat peculiar. In any case, because he’s not particularly picky or rejecting of others, his interpersonal relationships are generally fine.
- Like Tatsumaki, Saitama dislikes being dependent on others, but he doesn’t deny the value of relationships themselves. If he considers someone to be somewhat close to him, he will look out for them in his own way. At first Saitama found Genos annoying, but later he developed a teacher’s awareness toward him. When Genos was left battered by the Deep Sea King, Saitama was surprised and asked if he was even alive, and upon seeing Genos still alive he sighed as if relieved.
- When Platinum Sperm and Garou clashed and Genos lamented his lack of strength, Saitama offered words of consolation.
- Ultimately, when Garou ripped out Genos’s core and killed him, Saitama flew into a rage and, without warning, landed a real punch — showing that he truly cared for Genos.
- Because of Saitama’s overwhelming strength, people with twisted ideologies sometimes take great interest in his power and approach him. That doesn’t mean they threaten him, but there are persistent types who treat him as a rival and follow him around, like Sonic, Amai Mask, and Metal Knight. Amai Mask in particular shows something close to obsession toward Saitama.
- In Tatsumaki’s case, she was unconscious at the time, so even after the Garou fight she kept treating Saitama as weak until she recently fought him directly and realized his strength; she seems to now regard him as on her level or far stronger.
- Especially at a point when she couldn’t know the details about him, Tatsumaki said to Saitama, “You don’t have any friends either… like me,” which implies she considers Saitama to be of the same kind as her.
- Moreover, Saitama could not deny Tatsumaki’s remark, which seems to show he acknowledged she was similar to him. Metal Knight, who stayed out of the Monster Association raid until the end, later learned of Saitama’s strength, so at least the S-Class heroes likely recognized most of Saitama’s power by now.
- Mumen Rider is different from most others: unlike those who approached Saitama knowing about his hidden strength, Mumen Rider first reached out purely to help and later came to express gratitude for the Deep Sea King incident. Saitama opened his heart to Mumen Rider’s character, and since then they greet each other and speak warmly when they meet.
- Saitama usually doesn’t show favor toward those who approach him because of his strength, but with Mumen Rider it’s a mutual understanding. Another difference is that, despite differences in each person’s power, there is a deep shared empathy regarding the idea of “hero” and the concept of justice.
- Among those connected to Saitama, the one who can truly be called a friend is probably King. Saitama knows King’s secret well, King sometimes borrows Saitama’s abilities, and they fit well together—playing games, chatting casually, and so on. Especially playing games together is a new hobby King brought to Saitama, who had no other hobbies outside hero work.
- Also, among those who interact with Saitama, King is one of the few who knows what kind of person Saitama is without prejudice. Genos, who has the most contact, reveres and follows Saitama unconditionally and tends to read too much into his words and actions; King, being an ordinary person, may not know the full extent of Saitama’s overwhelming strength, but he can look at and speak about Saitama objectively as a person without perceptional distortion.
- Because King can be immature, Saitama sometimes looks after him like an older brother, but King also occasionally shows a brotherly side to Saitama. King was the one who gave Saitama a sharp rebuke for Saitama’s criticism—his apathetic attitude of not even trying to resolve his sense of emptiness. King is a friend who is widely recognized by the public and heroes as being at the top of the world, and he also has S-Class Genos as a sort of protégé.
- Silver Fang has become friendly enough to visit each other’s homes, and Saitama is on good terms with Fubuki, who is fairly famous in the A–B ranks. Flashy Flash even came to Saitama’s house to try to recruit him as a disciple. Though Saitama finds it bothersome, considering that many other S-Class heroes don’t show close relationships with each other, Saitama’s network is among the top in the Hero Association.
- Furthermore, Saitama empathized most with Garou and helped rehabilitate him, aided the comrade fighting Phoenix Man, improved his relationship with Tatsumaki after they made contact, and recognized and defended Amai Mask when Amai Mask was shunned by the public—Saitama has been unknowingly expanding his network mostly among other powerful figures. Because he’s strong, he forms many ties with other strong people; Genos’s words are exactly right.
- Of course, the person who interacts with Saitama the most is Genos. At first Saitama found the persistently attached Genos annoying and burdensome, but over time Saitama began to worry when Genos was hurt and to consider what to say as a teacher; they became a good, brother-like pair.
- When Garou provoked Saitama by actually killing Genos, Saitama unleashe d a terrifying anger he had never shown before and fought Garou in earnest. Contrary to his usual manner, he spoke harsh, sincere words instead of jokes, expressing his hostility toward Garou, and even while fighting he gripped Genos’s core tightly with his hand so it wouldn’t break.
- In other words, just as Saitama instilled the goal of being a hero in Genos and advised him when he wandered, Genos, having spent a long time with Saitama, became a precious person who supports Saitama’s humanity.
- There has been no mention yet of Saitama’s parents or other family.

# 10. Miscellaneous:
## 10.1. Saitama's bungling nature:
- Saitama is extremely bungling in non-combat matters. In games he gets beaten by King every day, his finances are so tight that every supermarket sale is crucial, his house was smashed after the Garou arc, and despite his transcendent physical stats he botched the written test and started as a C-class.
- Saitama holds a mentor position yet is less popular than his pupil, and he even lost an eating contest. In a way, it's because his opponents are all overpowered in those fields, so he loses—just like how he's overpowered in fighting and easily defeats opponents.
- Also, apart from his transcendent body, he still feels cold, can get cavities, and can fall ill. He broke the limits of human physical ability, but his body itself didn't cease to be human.

## 10.2. Manga:
- Saitama has many manga he enjoys; one of them is Assassination Classroom (Resort Arc).

## 10.3. King:
- The original bearer of the name "King" was Saitama. Most of King's fame is a fabricated reputation: large monsters that Saitama had defeated, and for some reason monsters that had already been defeated, were mistakenly reported as having been defeated by ordinary people who happened to be at the sites where the corpses lay. As a result, the current King is an ordinary man put in an awkward position by that illusion. In short, the true King is, ultimately, Saitama, who actually earned most of those accomplishments himself.

## 10.4. Hero Costume:
- The material of Saitama's hero costume is always a subject of speculation. Saitama himself is the one with a tough body, while his clothes are ordinary — yet his hero costume will sometimes be only slightly scorched by heat attacks or other times remain completely intact, so people keep asking, "What on earth is it made of?"

## 10.5. Saitama's Residence:
- He lives on the third floor of a seven-story apartment building in a ghost town on the outskirts of Z City. He has lived in Z City since childhood. This area used to be a normal urban district, but powerful monsters kept appearing and grim rumors about monster incidents spread, so people abandoned their homes and left, especially around the outskirts where monsters mainly occur. Although people left, utilities like electricity and water don't seem to have been cut off, and unsurprisingly rent became extremely cheap for this reason, so Saitama lives here as well.
- Saitama originally lived in an apartment in downtown Z City, but he was evicted after he turned the apartment residents, who were criminals, over to the police, which cut off the landlord's income. Later, for his merits in the battle with Garou, Saitama was promoted to A-Class, and thanks to that he got a room in the Hero Mansion in A City.

## 10.6. Baldness:
- Saitama is self-conscious about being bald. When fighting monsters he usually chatters and then, when they rush at him, throws a punch to take them down, but if a monster talks and brings up his hair he kills it on the spot.
- The hot pot Saitama makes contains cabbage that's said to be good for hair, so when the other heroes fight over the meat in the hot pot he's the only one worrying about the cabbage.
- Saitama even keeps hair-growth treatment at the bathhouse. Judging from that effort, if his hair ever grew back he probably wouldn't bother cutting it, but so far there are no signs of regrowth.
- Saitama is often underestimated because of his appearance. The only monsters who recognized Saitama's strength at a glance were Asura Kabuto, Boros, and Orochi. Asura Kabuto seemed to have a kind of wild instinct, Boros said that when he faced Saitama he felt an unfathomable energy, and Orochi, the moment he sensed Saitama, ignored the other invaders and immediately came and went into combat readiness.
- Genos, Silverfang, Garou, and Metal Knight realized Saitama's power after seeing him use it firsthand. Other characters, even after seeing him, experienced cognitive dissonance.

## 10.7. Public evaluation of Saitama and his inner self:
- Saitama, to some extent, accepts a poor reputation for the sake of other heroes, but even taking that into account, opportunities to receive positive recognition never come; instead, his reputation only keeps getting worse.
- In fact, Saitama is willing to let his reputation be damaged for the heroes who fight with their lives on the line. He was simply hit hard by the emptiness he felt the first time he faced the reality that, despite three years of overwhelming achievements, no one acknowledged him.

## 10.8. Taciturnity and Seriousness:
- Saitama dislikes people who talk a lot, and when someone’s story drags on he urges them, "Summarize it in 20 characters or less!" He himself also speaks very briefly; a representative example is answering Garou’s impassioned question about why he does hero work with the two-character reply "취미" ("hobby").
- Saitama once lazily signed a densely written consent form Fubuki handed him because he didn’t want to read a long document, almost getting forcibly recruited into the Fubuki Group. Of course, Saitama isn’t always like that; when necessary he listens seriously to others and doesn’t hold back advice. He confides his worries to King and, during his fight with Garou, offers him sincere counsel.

## 10.9. Financial situation:
- Saitama has no significant source of income besides doing hero work as a hobby, so his finances are poor. He lives in a cheap apartment in Z City and is so frugal that he gets seriously upset if he misses a supermarket's special discount period.
- With Genos living with him, Saitama's situation improved somewhat, but during the Garou arc his house was completely destroyed and he moved to an apartment at the Hero Association headquarters. Since being promoted to B-Class, the frequency of money troubles has decreased.

## 10.10. Saitama's Daily Life:
- Aside from the time he spends defeating monsters, Saitama is usually very idle. He mainly borrows King's game console to play games or reads manga.
- When he says the anime King lent him is boring yet begs to borrow the next episode, King even thinks, "Doesn't this guy really have nothing to do?"
- According to Genos's investigation, his routine is a repeating cycle of [sleep → eat → watch TV → sleep → eat → watch TV].

## 10.11. Saitama's fashion:
- Saitama's sense of fashion is quite peculiar. His hero costume is a combination of yellow tights, a white cape, and red gloves, and for casual wear he wears a shirt covered with the character '毛' (hair) or a T-shirt printed with the phrase 'HAGE (bald)'.
- In particular, Saitama often wears a hoodie with the word 'OPPAI (breasts)' and a drawing of breasts on it.
