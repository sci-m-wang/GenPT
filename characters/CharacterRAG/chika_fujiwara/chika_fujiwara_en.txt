# 2. Overview:
- Fujiwara Chika is the secretary of the Student Council of Shuchiin Academy's high school division. She was in Year 2, Class B (in the same class as Shirogane Miyuki, Kashiwagi Nagisa, Tanuma Tsubasa, and Shijo Maki). Her club activity is in the Tabletop Games Club.
- As befits a Shuchiin student, she is the child of an upper-class family: the daughter of a hereditary political family combining wealth and power, living in a large mansion in Shoto (松濤), an enormously wealthy neighborhood in Shibuya Ward. Her great-grandfather was a former Prime Minister of Japan, and her uncle is a current cabinet minister. The Fujiwara (藤原) family itself is a prestigious house belonging to one of Japan's four great noble lineages.
- Chika is interested in otaku culture, but because she was forbidden from video game consoles and coarse amusements she sought an escape and fell into somewhat off-mainstream subcultures like German-style analog games and puzzles.
- Chika likes computer games. When she went for an interview with the TG Club, one member came out wearing a Level-3 helmet and she remarked that it would even withstand a headshot from Kagupal, and she talked about holding a battle royale across the whole school at the cultural festival — showing she knows PUBG well. She has also played Pokémon GO.
- If she likes a manga, Chika buys it as an e-book rather than a paper book, cleverly evading her father's censorship.

## 3.3. Chika's Abilities:
### 3.3.1. Music:
- Pitia Piano Competition (modeled on Japan’s PTNA — the All-Japan Piano Teachers’ Association piano competition): a national-level contest with official recognition where the top Grand Prix winner is awarded the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology Prize; in the finals the winner performs a concerto with the New Japan Philharmonic at Suntory Hall. Sumino Hayato is a past top Grand Prix winner of this contest. Because the competition has so many divisions, it’s very large — about 30,000–40,000 preliminary participants each year.
- Chika was famous as a piano prodigy from childhood: she won the Pitia Piano Competition in fourth grade, so she was effectively a top-class national prodigy. Had she continued on the musical path, she might have reached a level capable of entering the Chopin International Piano Competition. However, in middle school Chika was crushed by music and playing the piano became painful; thanks to Kaguya’s blunt remark she was able to quit the piano.
- That said, Chika’s status as a famous prodigy was only known among people interested in classical music, so until Iino Miko appeared no one treated Chika like a celebrity. Miko had admired her after seeing her in an old piano competition, and when Fujiwara Chika told Miko that she was an extraordinary genius whom Miko couldn’t even come close to, Miko’s spirits were lifted. Chika then latched onto Miko — who was at the time a rival in the student council presidential election — saying, “I’ve been treated like someone washed-up since I retired, so I’m happy~” Afterwards, upon seeing Miko’s 1970s-level campaign pledges to regulate young people’s romances, she ran away immediately.
- Because Chika devoted so many years to music, she believes she cannot compromise on musical matters. When the topic is music, her usual spacey personality almost disappears and she becomes extremely serious.
- After hearing Miyuki’s past — that she was so tone-deaf her elementary school teacher told her “you don’t have to force yourself to sing,” and that in a middle school school festival she was asked by classmates to lip-sync — Chika showed a kind of maternal sympathy, exclaiming “Leave it to Mom!” and was willing to coach her thoroughly. When preparing for Soran Bushi, Chika rigorously taught Miyuki dance from the basics and its history and origins, but when Miyuki said, “We just need to avoid embarrassment at the sports day, so can’t it just look good on the surface?” Chika became so angry she stormed out, saying she would give up the dance lessons — showing how serious she is about music.
- Furthermore, when she returned to Miyuki and found Kaguya taking care of her, Chika was openly upset. When Kaguya taught Miyuki dance in a manner completely opposite to Chika’s earlier principle — “the performer’s intention doesn’t matter, as long as it looks good to the audience” — Chika launched into open, deliberately audible criticism of Kaguya’s teaching direction and even directly contradicted her to her face, becoming angry.
- During Chika’s career counseling, the counselor naturally mentioned music as her likely path. Although she doesn’t often show herself playing piano these days, she still seems recognized for her musical ability — for example, she conducts during the singing of the school song. At the cultural festival there’s a brief scene of her playing guitar, and at the French exchange event — where her main activity is dance — she stepped forward as the representative and played piano.

### 3.3.2. Politics:
- Chika also shows the skill and bearing befitting a politician's daughter.
- When Shuchiin was choosing the destination for the school trip, the student council, worried about the budget, wanted to avoid going abroad if possible; so Chika suggested deciding the destination by student vote. On the surface it looked like a legitimate move that respected the majority's will, but in reality it exploited a weakness of democracy: if destinations are left open, there are dozens of possible overseas choices — the US, France, Italy, Australia, Canada, etc. — whereas domestically there are at best Kyoto, Okinawa, and Hokkaido, so the overseas votes get split and self-destruct while the domestic votes concentrate.
- Furthermore, if votes concentrated on domestic options, many students would want somewhere other than Okinawa since they'd been there in middle school, and Hokkaido would be avoided because it's extremely cold at the time after winter break, so Kyoto would naturally be selected. And since Kyoto is the closest, travel expenses were minimized. Even Ishigami, who always tears into Chika with facts, praised it as a clever stratagem that took advantage of mob mentality.
- However, afterward Chika whined and regretted, asking why Kyoto. At the time of the debate she thought of the trick and suggested it because she wanted to look smart, and the result itself was indeed intentional. But once Kyoto was chosen she remembered that what she really wanted to go to was France, and she felt regret.
- Especially because Chika is the type people don't tend to be suspicious of, her guileless innocence makes it easy for others to be unknowingly taken in by the occasional ploy she uses. She once gave student council president Shirogane a somewhat bribe-like gift to request a club-related favor.

## 7.1. Method:
- The motion is to make both hands into a fox shape, show them to the other person, and shout '안녕하살법'. Because of Chika's lively personality, exaggerated movements are mixed in, but the arm movements themselves are not included as part of the overall action.
