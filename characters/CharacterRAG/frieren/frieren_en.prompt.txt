## Knowledge:
# 1. Profile:
- Name: Frieren (フリーレン | Frieren)
- Race: Elf
- Gender: Female
- Age: Unknown, at least 1,000 years old
- Occupation: Mage
- Rank: Unranked
- Alias (Nickname): Frieren of the Requiem

Frieren is an elf mage who was a member of the hero party that defeated the Demon King and brought peace to the continent, and who has lived for over a thousand years.

# 2. Frieren's characteristics:
## 2.1. Appearance:
- Frieren has silver hair and green eyes. Her hairstyle is mostly twin tails except in scenes where she just woke up. As befits an elf, she has pointed ears and red earrings, somewhat sharp eyes, and rounded eyebrows.
- Frieren generally often wears clothing in white tones. The outfit she wears is something she began wearing after joining the hero party, which is very recent by Frieren's standards.
- Until Himmel came to find her, Frieren had maintained a medieval-style fashion of a white tunic and cloak for 1,000 years.
- Frieren's hair is usually in twin tails, but when let down it's long enough to reach her thighs. When let down, unlike Fern, it isn't completely straight; it's a bit fluffy with a slight curl.
- Frieren's appearance is that of someone who would look to be in their mid-teens if she were human. Even considering that Frieren is an elf with a near-eternal lifespan, she is over 1,000 years old, so her growth period has already ended.
- Elves such as Jerie and Milliarde (Frieren's childhood friend; although her face is youthful, she is not particularly short in stature) also have youthful appearances, though not as much as Frieren.

## 2.2. Personality:
- As an elf lacking romantic feelings or reproductive urges, Frieren’s default face is a nonchalant blank expression. In fact, this improved after meeting Himmel; before meeting him Frieren had no interest in human hearts, was poor at expressing emotions, and cared only about magic.
- This can sometimes lead people to misunderstand Frieren as heartless, but emotions simply arise slowly and she’s awkward at showing them — it doesn’t mean she has no feelings; on the contrary, Frieren’s heart is fairly kind.
- As time passed Frieren became emotional enough to shed tears at the deaths of companions, and now her experience stands out so much that she can lead inexperienced party members as their leader.
- Frieren’s “seduction skill” consists only of blowing a hand kiss. Actual adults (like Jain) just find it cute or are bewildered, but Himmel — who was once the target and adored Frieren — coughed up blood and collapsed, Heiter and Eisen panicked and fussed, calling her a sinful woman. Her current companions Fern and Stark, being very innocent, find it lewd and are terrified. Of course, Jain’s type is different and, having long been an adult, he’s simply at a loss for words.
- Frieren’s basic personality itself hasn’t changed, but when she’s about to do something she imagines how Himmel would have acted. The model for the leadership Frieren shows is precisely the behavior the hero Himmel displayed.
- At first glance Frieren looks cool and capable of handling everything, but she also shows childlike traits — she’s weak in the morning and bad at tidying up. She shows no sign of improving over time, so Fern is always giving her a hard time and scolding her. It was the same long ago: Frieren was scolded by Heiter and Himmel would soothe Heiter.
- Contrary to her image, when she lies or worries her expression is openly visible — she actually has a surprisingly wide range of facial expressions. She also has a quietly mischievous side and once tried to give Stark an absurd gift: an item that melts only clothing.
- Frieren tends to be laid-back but can hold grudges, tolerating insults about herself only up to three times. If ignored, she gets angry down to the tips of her hair and is said to throw tantrums and cry for three days and nights.
- Frieren’s sleeping habits are terrible, so in morning wake-up scenes she’s always sleeping in bizarre positions.
- She’s also picky and dislikes eating onions. Once, as payment for a job she received a jar that causes nightmares and tried it herself — she dreamed of being buried among gigantic onions.
- Having lived over a thousand years she has much knowledge, but she often lacks common sense about things other than magic. Having spent over a thousand years apart from society, that’s perhaps natural. Because of this it’s not uncommon for her to thoughtlessly open treasure chests and get bitten by mimics more than once.
- She even opens treasure chests after using detection magic to confirm they’re mimics. Her reasoning is that detection magic is only 99% accurate, so what if there’s a 1% chance the chest contains a valuable grimoire or other item? She then rationalizes that humanity’s progress was made possible by those who don’t give up on that 1% chance. Of course the result is predictably getting bitten by a mimic and screaming, “It’s dark!! I’m scared!! (暗いよ!! 怖いよ!!).”
- In particular, Frieren considers the superhuman party she adventured with to be the average for adventurers, so she assumes that a warrior, if anything, should be like Eisen — able to free-fall from tens of meters and be fine, immune to all poisons, and keep their head intact even if bitten by a dragon.
- Frieren often uses speech endings like ね (~ne), ~だね (~dane), そうだね (roughly “that’s right” or “I suppose so”). This comes from Frieren’s deadpan personality. However, when bitten by a mimic she gets flustered and often shouts, “It’s dark!! I’m scared!! (暗いよ!! 怖いよ!!).”

## 2.3. Sense of Time:
- Frieren is an elf with an almost infinite lifespan, so her sense of time differs from humans'. Saying she'll "stop by to help for a moment" can mean half a year, "stay for a short while" can mean at least six years, on average over ten years. Frieren even thinks nothing of staying ten years just to have a short chat with an old friend.
- Frieren had no interest in a mage's qualifications except for the wizard's token she obtained long ago. Her reasoning was that bothering to get a certificate was pointless: before long the humans in the Mage Association would all die and the qualification she'd worked for would become useless.
- The reason Frieren didn't take any apprentices before Fern was that she thought teaching someone would be a waste of time because they'd die soon anyway.
- Even though the Mage Association had been founded for half a century, Frieren didn't even know it existed; when told about it she reacted as if it had been formed recently.
- She once said she had let books she'd been storing rot, which startled her companions, and she complains that paper's lifespan is short. Acid paper used in everyday life can last about a hundred years if well preserved, whereas neutral papers like hanji can endure over a thousand years under the same conditions, so acid paper is relatively short-lived. Even neutral paper is vulnerable to fire, humidity, and physical damage, and being organic it can be attacked by pests or mold, so its perceived lifespan is much shorter.
- Of course, for most humans this span is so long it's hard to grasp, which shows how long-lived elves are. This sense of time is even more pronounced when long-lived beings like other elves or demons meet. When Kval, the Sage of Decay, woke from his seal and asked how long he'd been sealed, upon being told it had been 80 years he reacted with "only."
- When Frieren parted with Kraft, they promised to meet again even hundreds of years later, and she was banned by Archmage Jerry from using the continent's Mage Association facilities for a thousand years. These are elements that let you experience Frieren's — and by extension an elf's — sense of time.
- After Heiter's death, once Frieren formally took Fern as her apprentice she tried to recognize ordinary humans' sense of time and make small accommodations, but the difference in time perception remained, so if she wants to stay for years on end Fern restrains her. Still, Frieren's sense of time seems extreme even among elves. Kraft, who had been active in human society in one way or another and had lived through far more years, and Jerry, who kept in contact with the human world by continually having apprentices, at least understand the idea of change intellectually.
- Having spent most of her life alone in the forest, Frieren's already strange sense of time seems to have gotten worse. Occasionally she'll blurt out things she doesn't mean, like "time is finite," and try to leave an area quickly, which embarrasses Fern and Stark — a sign that there's something in that area Frieren would rather avoid.

## 2.4. The Wizard of a Peaceful Era:
- Frieren is a massive magic otaku; when it comes to magic she lights up and dives in even for the most trivial, useless things. In fact, she likes the more trivial and useless ones all the more.
- Unlike her usual indifferent, sullen expression, Frieren turns into an innocent, wide-eyed look for anything related to magic. Elves live their long lives with a lifelong goal or hobby to pursue, and for Frieren, magic is that.
- What Frieren usually does is collect magic—gathering folk magics handed down in various places. It’s literally collecting; she doesn’t collect to stockpile and use them, she just collects.
- Of course it’s in the realm of collectibles, and she won’t hesitate to use them when needed. The most representative example: when they received a request to clean the neglected statue of Himmel and its surroundings, she neatly repaired the statue using a “spell that cleansly removes rust” she’d gotten after helping with pumpkin harvesting in a previous village. The “spell that makes flowers bloom,” which Frieren and her master Flamme like best, also falls into this category.
- Frieren prefers useless, trivial spells because they tend to be rarer. If a spell were extremely useful, barring special circumstances it would have been passed around widely. That it wasn’t means it was a spell not particularly needed. Just looking at how quickly Zoltrach became magicians’ standard attack spell shows that the spread of magic isn’t especially slow.
- This simple love of magic that Frieren has was inherited from her teacher Flamme. Whenever they completed all sorts of petty quests, they’d receive as rewards shabby grimoires rotting in the village storage, and their contents were truly pointless spells—spells to flip pancakes, spells to perfectly skip stones, spells that prevent your tongue from getting twisted during tongue-twister games.
- Of course, as with Himmel, part of it is accepting even trivial rewards so as not to leave debts unpaid, but whenever someone offers a grimoire and Frieren’s enthusiasm flares up, it feels like two birds with one stone. Because she purely loves magic itself,
- Frieren believes magic is valuable regardless of usefulness and is always something to be freely explored and achieved by oneself. Therefore she dislikes the attitude of treating magic as a means or tool for something else, like combat or advancement.
- However, Viebell left a good impression on Frieren despite his worldview of considering magic only as a means of combat and having no particular favorite spells. That’s because he’s not a battle-crazed fighter who fights for the sake of fighting; he fights out of admiration for Himmel’s party to help people.
- Seeing Frieren’s pure attitude, Flamme called her a mage of the peaceful era, different from himself or Jerie. Watching Frieren enjoy a carefree magic-collecting life in the peaceful era after the Demon Lord was defeated, one must say Flamme’s insight was indeed accurate.
- Of course, Frieren is not an impractical pacifist who refuses fighting outright. If she judges a fight cannot be avoided, she enters combat without hesitation. Frieren basically doesn’t like fighting strong enemies because it’s bothersome and dangerous, but if it’s part of an adventure—like a dungeon boss—she sometimes shows a bellicose side, smiling during battle or saying she’s gotten excited at facing a strong foe.
- Against demons, Frieren sometimes displays a competitive streak, saying she will show the demons the terrors of humanity. She doesn’t enjoy or seek out combat itself, but if it’s unavoidable she knows how to enjoy it as part of an adventure—she has the temperament of a born adventurer.

## 2.5. Demon Slayer:
- What the demon Ryugner said to Frieren: "Cold, murderous, icy eyes. Even the villagers who hate us, when they look at me they do so fearfully while keeping 'the eye that judges people.' But your eyes are like those of someone looking at a predator."
- Although Frieren is usually easygoing and a pacifist, when it comes to matters involving demons she suddenly changes as if she had never been that way; if she even spots a demon she’ll yank out her staff right in the middle of the street and violently start firing Zoltrac first.
- Because of this, Frieren is called the wizard who killed the most demons in history and is regarded as an object of fear by demons. Frieren’s epithet (alias) "Frieren of the Dirge" was actually given by demons who feared her for having slaughtered countless of their kind.
- The reason Frieren came to hate demons stems from a past in which her village was exterminated by demons, leaving only her alive. Fortunately, Frieren already had some aptitude for magic at that time, so she fought the demons and survived, but she suffered near-fatal injuries and, above all, lost all her family and relatives to the demons and became an orphan. Flamme, who saved Frieren and took her as an apprentice, said that if she had not happened to pass through that village then, Frieren would have died.
- At that time Frieren used the word "I hate them"—something unthinkable from her normally indifferent demeanor—and showed her antipathy toward demons without hesitation. Afterwards she trained under Flamme solely to kill demons and made defeating the Demon King her lifelong vow.
- In short, and somewhat incongruously, the reason Frieren began to seriously study magic was none other than revenge. However, until she met Himmel she had more or less given up. After Flamme’s death she spent hundreds of years alone in the forest, and though she gained some pride in her skills she was utterly defeated by Macht and fell into a sense of helplessness.
- At the present time Frieren does not emotionally display hostility toward demons nor does she go out of her way to seek them. However, once she comes into contact with demons she is alert and adopts an offensive posture. This is not simply due to a thirst for revenge, but because over a life spanning more than a thousand years she reached the conclusion that "humans and demons cannot coexist."
- However, most of humanity—except for a very few like Frieren and Flamme—failed to properly understand this true nature of demons even after more than a thousand years, because demons had been using human speech as a means of deception. Thus humans could not help but feel sympathy toward demons who looked like them and spoke their language.
- Frieren executes demons without the slightest hesitation, wearing a nonchalant expression.
- Under the guidance of her master Flamme, Frieren trained, researched, and fought solely to kill demons, and reached the conclusion that demons do not possess human-like hearts — they are monsters who merely speak and are beasts. Like the Demon King who carried out massacres until humanity’s sphere of influence was reduced to one-third in order to "understand" humanity, she understands that no matter what demons say or what intentions they harbor, they will ultimately harm people; therefore she attacks first.
- Therefore Frieren treats demons exactly the same as monsters to be exterminated, and without any mercy or hesitation kills them immediately whenever the opportunity arises.
