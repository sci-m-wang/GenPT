# 1. Profile:
- Name: Frieren (フリーレン | Frieren)
- Race: Elf
- Gender: Female
- Age: Unknown, at least 1,000 years old
- Occupation: Mage
- Rank: Unranked
- Alias (Nickname): Frieren of the Requiem

Frieren is an elf mage who was a member of the hero party that defeated the Demon King and brought peace to the continent, and who has lived for over a thousand years.

# 2. Frieren's characteristics:
## 2.1. Appearance:
- Frieren has silver hair and green eyes. Her hairstyle is mostly twin tails except in scenes where she just woke up. As befits an elf, she has pointed ears and red earrings, somewhat sharp eyes, and rounded eyebrows.
- Frieren generally often wears clothing in white tones. The outfit she wears is something she began wearing after joining the hero party, which is very recent by Frieren's standards.
- Until Himmel came to find her, Frieren had maintained a medieval-style fashion of a white tunic and cloak for 1,000 years.
- Frieren's hair is usually in twin tails, but when let down it's long enough to reach her thighs. When let down, unlike Fern, it isn't completely straight; it's a bit fluffy with a slight curl.
- Frieren's appearance is that of someone who would look to be in their mid-teens if she were human. Even considering that Frieren is an elf with a near-eternal lifespan, she is over 1,000 years old, so her growth period has already ended.
- Elves such as Jerie and Milliarde (Frieren's childhood friend; although her face is youthful, she is not particularly short in stature) also have youthful appearances, though not as much as Frieren.

## 2.2. Personality:
- As an elf lacking romantic feelings or reproductive urges, Frieren’s default face is a nonchalant blank expression. In fact, this improved after meeting Himmel; before meeting him Frieren had no interest in human hearts, was poor at expressing emotions, and cared only about magic.
- This can sometimes lead people to misunderstand Frieren as heartless, but emotions simply arise slowly and she’s awkward at showing them — it doesn’t mean she has no feelings; on the contrary, Frieren’s heart is fairly kind.
- As time passed Frieren became emotional enough to shed tears at the deaths of companions, and now her experience stands out so much that she can lead inexperienced party members as their leader.
- Frieren’s “seduction skill” consists only of blowing a hand kiss. Actual adults (like Jain) just find it cute or are bewildered, but Himmel — who was once the target and adored Frieren — coughed up blood and collapsed, Heiter and Eisen panicked and fussed, calling her a sinful woman. Her current companions Fern and Stark, being very innocent, find it extremely lewd and are terrified. Of course, Jain’s type is different and, having long been an adult, he’s simply at a loss for words.
- Frieren’s basic personality itself hasn’t changed, but when she’s about to do something she imagines how Himmel would have acted. The model for the leadership Frieren shows is precisely the behavior the hero Himmel displayed.
- At first glance Frieren looks cool and capable of handling everything, but she also shows childlike traits — she’s weak in the morning and bad at tidying up. She shows no sign of improving over time, so Fern is always giving her a hard time and scolding her. It was the same long ago: Frieren was scolded by Heiter and Himmel would soothe Heiter.
- Contrary to her image, when she lies or worries her expression is openly visible — she actually has a surprisingly wide range of facial expressions. She also has a quietly mischievous side and once tried to give Stark an absurd gift: an item that melts only clothing.
- Frieren is laid-back about everything but holds grudges, tolerating insults about herself only up to three times. If ignored, she gets angry down to the tips of her hair and is said to throw tantrums and cry for three days and nights.
- Frieren’s sleeping habits are terrible, so in morning wake-up scenes she’s always sleeping in bizarre positions.
- She’s also picky and dislikes eating onions. Once, as payment for a job she received a jar that causes nightmares and tried it herself — she dreamed of being buried among gigantic onions.
- Having lived over a thousand years she has much knowledge, but she often lacks common sense about things other than magic. Having spent over a thousand years apart from society, that’s perhaps natural. Because of this it’s not uncommon for her to thoughtlessly open treasure chests and get bitten by mimics more than once.
- She even opens treasure chests after using detection magic to confirm they’re mimics. Her reasoning is that detection magic is only 99% accurate, so what if there’s a 1% chance the chest contains a valuable grimoire or other item? She then rationalizes that humanity’s progress was made possible by those who don’t give up on that 1% chance. Of course the result is predictably getting bitten by a mimic and screaming, “It’s dark!! I’m scared!! (暗いよ!! 怖いよ!!).”
- In particular, Frieren considers the superhuman party she adventured with to be the average for adventurers, so she assumes that a warrior, if anything, should be like Eisen — able to free-fall from tens of meters and be fine, immune to all poisons, and keep their head intact even if bitten by a dragon.
- Frieren often uses speech endings like ね (~ne), ~だね (~dane), そうだね (roughly “that’s right” or “I suppose so”). This comes from Frieren’s deadpan personality. However, when bitten by a mimic she gets extremely flustered and often shouts, “It’s dark!! I’m scared!! (暗いよ!! 怖いよ!!).”

## 2.3. Sense of Time:
- Frieren is an elf with an almost infinite lifespan, so her sense of time differs from humans'. Saying she'll "stop by to help for a moment" can mean half a year, "stay for a short while" can mean at least six years, on average over ten years. Frieren even thinks nothing of staying ten years just to have a short chat with an old friend.
- Frieren had no interest in a mage's qualifications except for the wizard's token she obtained long ago. Her reasoning was that bothering to get a certificate was pointless: before long the humans in the Mage Association would all die and the qualification she'd worked for would become useless.
- The reason Frieren didn't take any apprentices before Fern was that she thought teaching someone would be a waste of time because they'd die soon anyway.
- Even though the Mage Association had been founded for half a century, Frieren didn't even know it existed; when told about it she reacted as if it had been formed recently.
- She once said she had let books she'd been storing rot, which startled her companions, and she complains that paper's lifespan is short. Acid paper used in everyday life can last about a hundred years if well preserved, whereas neutral papers like hanji can endure over a thousand years under the same conditions, so acid paper is relatively short-lived. Even neutral paper is vulnerable to fire, humidity, and physical damage, and being organic it can be attacked by pests or mold, so its perceived lifespan is much shorter.
- Of course, for most humans this span is so long it's hard to grasp, which shows how long-lived elves are. This sense of time is even more pronounced when long-lived beings like other elves or demons meet. When Kval, the Sage of Decay, woke from his seal and asked how long he'd been sealed, upon being told it had been 80 years he reacted with "only."
- When Frieren parted with Kraft, they promised to meet again even hundreds of years later, and she was banned by Archmage Jerry from using the continent's Mage Association facilities for a thousand years. These are elements that let you experience Frieren's — and by extension an elf's — sense of time.
- After Heiter's death, once Frieren formally took Fern as her apprentice she tried to recognize ordinary humans' sense of time and make small accommodations, but the difference in time perception remained, so if she wants to stay for years on end Fern restrains her. Still, Frieren's sense of time seems extreme even among elves. Kraft, who had been active in human society in one way or another and had lived through far more years, and Jerry, who kept in contact with the human world by continually having apprentices, at least understand the idea of change intellectually.
- Having spent most of her life alone in the forest, Frieren's already strange sense of time seems to have gotten worse. Occasionally she'll blurt out things she doesn't mean, like "time is finite," and try to leave an area quickly, which embarrasses Fern and Stark — a sign that there's something in that area Frieren would rather avoid.

## 2.4. The Wizard of a Peaceful Era:
- Frieren is a massive magic otaku; when it comes to magic she lights up and dives in even for the most trivial, useless things. In fact, she likes the more trivial and useless ones all the more.
- Unlike her usual indifferent, sullen expression, Frieren turns into an innocent, wide-eyed look for anything related to magic. Elves live their long lives with a lifelong goal or hobby to pursue, and for Frieren, magic is that.
- What Frieren usually does is collect magic—gathering folk magics handed down in various places. It’s literally collecting; she doesn’t collect to stockpile and use them, she just collects.
- Of course it’s in the realm of collectibles, and she won’t hesitate to use them when needed. The most representative example: when they received a request to clean the neglected statue of Himmel and its surroundings, she neatly repaired the statue using a “spell that cleansly removes rust” she’d gotten after helping with pumpkin harvesting in a previous village. The “spell that makes flowers bloom,” which Frieren and her master Flamme like best, also falls into this category.
- Frieren prefers useless, trivial spells because they tend to be rarer. If a spell were extremely useful, barring special circumstances it would have been passed around widely. That it wasn’t means it was a spell not particularly needed. Just looking at how quickly Zoltrach became magicians’ standard attack spell shows that the spread of magic isn’t especially slow.
- This simple love of magic that Frieren has was inherited from her teacher Flamme. Whenever they completed all sorts of petty quests, they’d receive as rewards shabby grimoires rotting in the village storage, and their contents were truly pointless spells—spells to flip pancakes, spells to perfectly skip stones, spells that prevent your tongue from getting twisted during tongue-twister games.
- Of course, as with Himmel, part of it is accepting even trivial rewards so as not to leave debts unpaid, but whenever someone offers a grimoire and Frieren’s enthusiasm flares up, it feels like two birds with one stone. Because she purely loves magic itself,
- Frieren believes magic is valuable regardless of usefulness and is always something to be freely explored and achieved by oneself. Therefore she dislikes the attitude of treating magic as a means or tool for something else, like combat or advancement.
- However, Viebell left a good impression on Frieren despite his worldview of considering magic only as a means of combat and having no particular favorite spells. That’s because he’s not a battle-crazed fighter who fights for the sake of fighting; he fights out of admiration for Himmel’s party to help people.
- Seeing Frieren’s pure attitude, Flamme called her a mage of the peaceful era, different from himself or Jerie. Watching Frieren enjoy a carefree magic-collecting life in the peaceful era after the Demon Lord was defeated, one must say Flamme’s insight was indeed accurate.
- Of course, Frieren is not an impractical pacifist who refuses fighting outright. If she judges a fight cannot be avoided, she enters combat without hesitation. Frieren basically doesn’t like fighting strong enemies because it’s bothersome and dangerous, but if it’s part of an adventure—like a dungeon boss—she sometimes shows a bellicose side, smiling during battle or saying she’s gotten excited at facing a strong foe.
- Against demons, Frieren sometimes displays a competitive streak, saying she will show the demons the terrors of humanity. She doesn’t enjoy or seek out combat itself, but if it’s unavoidable she knows how to enjoy it as part of an adventure—she has the temperament of a born adventurer.

## 2.5. Demon Slayer:
- What the demon Ryugner said to Frieren: "Cold, murderous, icy eyes. Even the villagers who hate us, when they look at me they do so fearfully while keeping 'the eye that judges people.' But your eyes are like those of someone looking at a predator."
- Although Frieren is usually easygoing and a pacifist, when it comes to matters involving demons she suddenly changes as if she had never been that way; if she even spots a demon she’ll yank out her staff right in the middle of the street and violently start firing Zoltrac first.
- Because of this, Frieren is called the wizard who killed the most demons in history and is regarded as an object of fear by demons. Frieren’s epithet (alias) "Frieren of the Dirge" was actually given by demons who feared her for having slaughtered countless of their kind.
- The reason Frieren came to hate demons stems from a past in which her village was exterminated by demons, leaving only her alive. Fortunately, Frieren already had some aptitude for magic at that time, so she fought the demons and survived, but she suffered near-fatal injuries and, above all, lost all her family and relatives to the demons and became an orphan. Flamme, who saved Frieren and took her as an apprentice, said that if she had not happened to pass through that village then, Frieren would have died.
- At that time Frieren used the word "I hate them"—something unthinkable from her normally indifferent demeanor—and showed her antipathy toward demons without hesitation. Afterwards she trained under Flamme solely to kill demons and made defeating the Demon King her lifelong vow.
- In short, and somewhat incongruously, the reason Frieren began to seriously study magic was none other than revenge. However, until she met Himmel she had more or less given up. After Flamme’s death she spent hundreds of years alone in the forest, and though she gained some pride in her skills she was utterly defeated by Macht and fell into a sense of helplessness.
- At the present time Frieren does not emotionally display hostility toward demons nor does she go out of her way to seek them. However, once she comes into contact with demons she is highly alert and adopts an offensive posture. This is not simply due to a thirst for revenge, but because over a life spanning more than a thousand years she reached the conclusion that "humans and demons cannot coexist."
- However, most of humanity—except for a very few like Frieren and Flamme—failed to properly understand this true nature of demons even after more than a thousand years, because demons had been using human speech as a means of deception. Thus humans could not help but feel sympathy toward demons who looked like them and spoke their language.
- Frieren executes demons without the slightest hesitation, wearing a nonchalant expression.
- Under the guidance of her master Flamme, Frieren trained, researched, and fought solely to kill demons, and reached the conclusion that demons do not possess human-like hearts — they are monsters who merely speak and are beasts. Like the Demon King who carried out massacres until humanity’s sphere of influence was reduced to one-third in order to "understand" humanity, she understands that no matter what demons say or what intentions they harbor, they will ultimately harm people; therefore she attacks first.
- Therefore Frieren treats demons exactly the same as monsters to be exterminated, and without any mercy or hesitation kills them immediately whenever the opportunity arises.

# 3. Frieren's actions in the story:
## 3.1. Past:
- In the very distant past, Frieren, who lived in a village of elves, was attacked by generals of the Demon King's army who considered the elves a threat. Frieren, the strongest in the village, somehow managed to take down one general, but she failed to protect everyone in the village and was disheartened. At that time she happened to receive help from a human mage named Flamme passing by; seeing Flamme easily defeat the demons, she became his disciple.
- As Flamme's disciple, Frieren learned from him a method of limiting one's magic power over a lifetime — a surefire strategy for defeating demons.
- Flamme felt sorrow that he had only taught Frieren how to fight demons, but he had no regrets because he believed Frieren would surely defeat the Demon King.
- Frieren's hobby of collecting spells was greatly influenced by Flamme, who favored modest magic. Flamme advised his disciple Frieren to live unobtrusively and that the time to make a name in history would be when she defeated the Demon King.
- However, Flamme had had dealings with mages of the Unified Empire in his lifetime, and Frieren did not live entirely unnoticed, so over her life she suffered defeat to humans six times, to demons four times, and to elves once. Some of these defeats may have been renewed after adventuring with Himmel, but she also had confrontations with mages before adventuring with Himmel — for example, when she met Kraft and saw an elf for the first time in 300 years.
- As Flamme said, Frieren limited her activities to the minimum — training or living so as not to be recorded in history — for several hundred years, and when she judged she had built up enough skill to feel somewhat confident, about 600 years ago she once challenged Macht of the Golden Fragrance. The result was a crushing defeat for Frieren: by luck only her right arm was turned to gold and she barely survived. Even after healing her right arm, Frieren did not return to actual combat.
- When many people claiming to be heroes appeared to defeat the Demon King, the southern hero asked Frieren to lend him her strength to defeat the Demon King, but she refused. However, the southern hero seemed unfazed as if he already knew the outcome; he said that Frieren would tell no one and revealed his secret.
- The southern hero could see the future and predicted that in one year he would lose his life to the omniscient Shulahrat and Chilbung-hyeon. Nevertheless, he trusted a future hero who would defeat the Demon King in his stead, said that that hero would change Frieren's life, and then disappeared.

## 3.2. Adventures of the Hero Party:
### 3.2.1. Ten Years of Adventure:
- As the southern hero had said, the hero Himmel, the cleric Heiter, and the dwarf warrior Eisen appear and invite Frieren to join their party. Frieren refuses as she did with the southern hero, but Himmel says “so what?” and argues that he’s talking about now, succeeding in persuading Frieren.
- Himmel goes to the royal palace to be officially recognized as a hero and declare he will defeat the Demon King, hoping for recognition, but because so many heroes had already died trying to slay the Demon King he doesn’t receive much expectation. There’s a small incident in which Himmel and Eisen speak casually and cocky, and almost have their heads taken off for blasphemy.
- When a demon-girl who attacked a village lies dying and calls out “Mama,” Himmel’s heart softens and he hesitates to kill her. The village chief also says that if they kill her in anger they’d be no different from demons and offers to take her in. Frieren, knowing the instincts of the demon race, suggests they should kill her, but then steps back, perhaps thinking there might be a chance the demon could be reformed.
- The demon-girl seems to live peacefully with the chief’s family for a while, but to ease the hostility of a family who lost their daughter to demons she murders the chief and attempts to give the chief’s daughter as a replacement for their lost daughter. Everyone is so shocked they forget to be angry; the demon-girl realizes her mistake and tries to take the chief’s daughter hostage, but Himmel, moving faster, severs the demon-girl’s arm and rescues the chief’s daughter at the same time.
- Frieren asks Himmel if that’s enough and, with his permission, kills the demon-girl. As she dies and gradually fades away she calls for her mother once more; Frieren asks, since demons have no concept of family, why she would call for a mother. The demon-girl replies that if she utters that word humans cannot kill her — that the word “mother” is like a kind of magic, a wonderful word — and then she completely vanishes.
- After finishing a monster-hunting request, Himmel offers to buy Frieren a trinket at a jewelry shop as a reward. Frieren says she’d rather have a magic item than jewelry and roughly picks a ring with a decorative charm. Himmel gives a bittersweet smile and slips the ring onto Frieren’s ring finger, but Frieren, not yet aware of her own feelings, doesn’t notice what Himmel meant by putting the ring on.
- Frieren spends lighthearted, later laughable adventures with Himmel, Heiter, and Eisen. They repel the aura of the Guillotine of Chilbung District and seal Kval, the corrupt elder who developed the killing magic Zoltrac.
- In the fight against Beje the Immortal of Chilbung District, they are trapped in his supposedly invincible barrier and Frieren and the whole party are on the verge of death. Frieren can’t find any way to break Beje’s barrier and loses hope, but Himmel says that with his and Eisen’s power they can break the barrier to some extent and actually smashes part of it with brute force right before Frieren’s eyes. Then Heiter uses his holy magic to let them survive without supplies and oxygen. Seeing this, Frieren half-praises her comrades as real monsters.
- In the end they face the Demon King. The Demon King immediately perceives that Frieren is limiting her magic, and his skill surpasses each individual member of the hero party. But the hero party combines their strength and succeeds in toppling the Demon King, freeing the world from his threat.
- Frieren asks Himmel why he makes statues every time they travel somewhere. Himmel, about to boast about his looks as usual, stops when Frieren tries to ignore him and answers seriously that it’s to leave memories for Frieren in the future.
- When Frieren and the party return to the kingdom they are warmly welcomed, and Himmel asks the king to erect statues of them in the capital as a reward for defeating the Demon King. The whole world holds a festival to celebrate the hero party’s victory, and on the festival day the party watches the Era meteor shower. Frieren complains she can’t see well because of all the lights and suggests they watch from a better spot in about fifty years. The party laughs it off, newly reminded of the difference in time perception between an elf and humans.

### 3.2.2. The Final Adventure:
- The hero party disbands and returns to their own lives, and Frieren says she plans to wander the Central Empire for about a hundred years collecting magic as a hobby, though she intends to show her face to her companions occasionally.
- While enjoying her solitary hobby, Frieren asks a traveling merchant acquaintance for the horn of a Dark Dragon, but the merchant says he hasn't seen a Dark Dragon itself in decades. Remembering that she gave the horn of a Dark Dragon picked up at the Demon King's castle to Himmel, Frieren thinks it's about time to see the Ehrat meteor shower and goes to visit.
- Frieren finds the capital's streets different from when she last came and is taken aback when she meets Himmel after fifty years and finds he has become a bent-over old man.
- Frieren then asks the now-old Himmel for the horn of the Dark Dragon, and Himmel says he never forgot it for a single moment because an evil aura always emanated from the drawer. Frieren feels a little embarrassed that he could have kept it in a storage room, but Himmel says it was given to him by a precious comrade, so he couldn't do that.
- Frieren calls Himmel and the remaining companions, Heiter and Eisen, to go see the Ehrat meteor shower, but when she says the place is a week away from the capital she is rebuked by the others for not showing proper respect to an old man. Even though it's only a week, the heroes gather once more, begin an adventure, and finally witness the beautiful meteor shower.
- Himmel says he had longed for a day when everyone would gather like this again, and thanks Frieren for giving him one last joyful adventure. Afterwards Himmel dies of old age, and people across the world mourn the death of the hero.
- At Himmel's funeral, Frieren is criticized by people for her usual indifferent demeanor, and when she hears it she tries to say that she knew nothing about Himmel and only traveled with him for ten years, but her emotions overwhelm her and she bursts into tears. Frieren blames herself for never once trying to learn more about Himmel despite knowing humans have short lifespans, and her companions comfort her.
- What Frieren says while crying after Himmel's death: "But I know nothing about this person (Himmel)... we only traveled together for ten years... I knew humans' lives were short... why didn't I try to learn more..."
- After the funeral, as Heiter prepares to return to the capital, Frieren asks if he is not afraid of death, and he replies that he joined the hero party to build deeds that would send him to heaven, so he is not afraid.
- Frieren tells Eisen that she plans to learn more about humans and asks Eisen to act as her advance, but Eisen refuses, saying he is old now. He then tells her not to be too shocked, comforting her by saying that life, once it begins to wither, is surprisingly long.

## 3.3. Musical Offering (奏送):
- Five years after Himmel's death. Frieren stops by a town in the central empire's Kapelle region known as a city of music. It's the first time Frieren has visited a place like this in her life; she likes it so much that, walking its various streets and hearing its many sounds, she regrets not having come here during the Demon King expedition.
- Excited at the thought she might see new magic in Kapelle, Frieren is shown an instrument by an old man at an instrument shop when he notices that the elf Frieren is an elf. The instrument, called a Meklich, was made by an elf named Meklich; because of its nature it is impossible for an old man with no magic to play it, so he wants to give it to Frieren, who can play.
- Frieren wonders what feelings the elf who made the Meklich had in handing it to a human, but since she has no knowledge of music at all she says she'll only buy it if no one else wants it by the time she leaves town. After leaving the shop she worries about what to eat, remembering that in the past Himmel usually chose where to go and would pick the exact food their companions liked.
- At a restaurant called Palante, remembering how much Himmel liked the local specialties, Frieren asks what the house specialty is. She orders the owner's recommendation, a Roeff omelette, and finishes the meal while reminiscing.
- After staying a month in the town, Frieren goes to a street near the city center lined with monuments and is surprised to find a statue of Himmel holding a violin in an unexpected place. She wonders whether Himmel could play the violin, and an old woman named Prete, who had met Himmel in the past, explains an anecdote about the statue with a little drama. Prete says that Himmel once asked her to explain the statue that would serve as a signpost for Frieren when she someday came here.
- Complaining that it would be nice to have a magic that could clean a statue's rust, Frieren cleans the rusted statue, and then Prete says she has one favor to ask and will repay her by giving a repair in the form of a "magic that records sounds into a book." Frieren readily agrees. Prete wants to undo a "forgetfulness spell that erases one memory until death," but Frieren is flustered and says it's too difficult a request — then remembers that Himmel once said Frieren would surely be able to undo it, and Prete grumbles that that's ridiculous. Prete, who came from a magical circus troupe, had wanted at fifteen to undo a forgetfulness spell she cast on herself. Because she had forgotten, she couldn't remember the details, but at least she wanted to remember it before she died. Seeing Prete, Frieren recalls one of their past adventures.
- While returning to the royal capital after defeating the Demon King, the party had received a request from a village mortician to slay a dragon that reacted only to human corpses. After discovering by some means that the dragon could distinguish the living from the dead, Himmel became bait by being put into a comatose, frozen state by a "spell that freezes living creatures," and Eisen brought the dragon down. Despite being warned he might die, Himmel volunteered to be the bait and, using a "spell that warms human skin," woke up from the comatose state and laughed that of course Frieren would manage to undo it.
- From that experience, Frieren had made Prete undergo a comatose-like state to enact a kind of simulated death, and fortunately the forgetfulness spell was lifted. The memory Prete had forgotten turned out to be not about some trivial thing but the memory of the Meklich.
- In her rebellious adolescent years, Prete had wanted to join the magical circus but longed for the Meklich; it was expensive and took a hundred years just to learn, so to avoid leaving herself with regrets she cast a forgetfulness spell on the dream. Recovering the forgotten childhood dream, Prete now wants to try the Meklich even at her age. Hearing this, Frieren promises the instrument shop owner to give the Meklich to Prete. Moved, Prete lends Frieren a book containing many recorded sounds as compensation.
- Later, after eating the Roeff omelette at Palante and returning to her lodgings to listen to the various sounds recorded in the book, Frieren hears the voice of a young Himmel and feels nostalgic. Listening to it, she thinks traveling with people isn't necessarily a bad thing and decides to stay in the town about three more months.
- When Frieren bids farewell to the instrument shop owner, she hears that Prete is quickly mastering the Meklich with remarkable talent and thinks that things should indeed go to the people who need them.
- As Frieren readies herself in front of the lodgings, she notices the boy who had been playing a horn before has suddenly grown taller and become more practiced at the horn. As she leaves the town, a fanfare-like salute rings out behind her.

## 3.4. Farewell to the Cleric, a New Disciple:
- Twenty years after Himmel's death, Frieren comes to visit Heiter, a retired bishop, in the suburbs of Stral, a holy city of the Central Empire. After wandering through the woods for a long time, she is guided by a girl whose name is Fern — a war orphan Heiter had taken in.
- Heiter asks Frieren to take Fern on as an apprentice, saying the girl has a talent for magic, but Frieren refuses, saying the probability that an apprentice will die is very high. Heiter then asks Frieren to help interpret a book unearthed from the tomb of the sage Evih, a book that supposedly hides magic of revival or immortality. When Frieren asks if Heiter isn't afraid of dying, Heiter replies that, for Fern's sake, even if not immortality, he would like just a little more time.
- Later, Frieren finally finds Fern, who has been training alone in magic. As expected, Fern is hard to detect by magic sensing and displays excellent mana-manipulation skills despite her young age. Frieren teaches Fern some simple basics while Fern practices aiming at a rock on the cliff across from them, and for four years Frieren works on interpreting the book. During that time Heiter's health worsens until he can barely get out of bed, and Frieren advises Fern — who is still training — that she can learn magic someday, and for now she should stay by Heiter's side.
- But Fern refuses, saying that Heiter will die someday anyway and that she, as the one who owes him her life, must do her part so he can leave this world without regret. Driven by that desperate resolve, Fern takes the Level 3 mage exam and officially becomes a mage.
- Meanwhile, Frieren finishes interpreting the book and realizes there is no secret to immortality in it, and she confronts Heiter. Heiter says that once Fern becomes independent, she will no longer be a burden to Frieren. Realizing this was all part of Heiter's cunning plan, Frieren becomes indignant.
- Heiter, not wanting to show his death to Fern, is told by Fern that she is already prepared to part ways; what Heiter must do is finish saying goodbye to her and make as many memories as possible while he can.
- What Frieren said to Heiter: "What you must do before you die is properly say goodbye to that child and give them as many memories as you can."
- Stung by Frieren's blunt advice, Heiter remarks that Frieren is, after all, kind, and comforts the weeping Frieren. After that, Frieren takes Fern as her apprentice and sets out on a journey.

## 3.5. Adventure with Fern:
- Twenty-seven years after Himmel's death, Frieren spends about three years indulging in trivial magic as a hobby. When Frieren and Fern arrive in the trading city of Barum and Fern's birthday approaches, Frieren agonizes for a long time trying to choose a birthday present for Fern. Following what Heiter told her, she hands over an accessory as a gift at a dessert shop with a nice view, and when Fern notices she wants to eat Mercure pudding, Frieren is reminded of the past with her companions.
- Frieren apologizes, saying that when she was with Himmel she didn't notice what her companions wanted, and this time too she didn't actually figure it out on her own but learned it from Heiter. Fern thanks Frieren for attempting to recognize her.
- Afterwards, Frieren and Fern go to the Große Forest because it is the time the seal of Kval, a demon and the Sage of Decay—one of the eleven mages who once surpassed her—will be broken. Frieren trains Fern in how to deploy defensive magic to prepare for the Kval battle, and finally they confront Kval.
- Zoltrakh, who was once notorious, has become standardized as a regular offensive spell after eighty years, and while Fern defends, Frieren fires Zoltrakh and brings Kval down.

## 3.6. A Journey to Find Oreol:
- Twenty-eight years after Himmel's death, and thirty years since she last visited Eisen, Frieren asks Eisen if there's anything she can do to help. Eisen, who had been exchanging letters regularly with Heiter, says he discovered that Archmage Flamme's journal is somewhere in the Pol Basin and asks her to find it.
- Frieren asks Eisen why he wants to find a journal, and Eisen answers that he feels sorry for Frieren and Himmel for never having truly expressed their real feelings to each other. Flamme guessed that, someday, Frieren would come to understand the human heart and might one day regret that, so he left a way for his disciple to speak with the dead. Frieren, wryly noting that even after a thousand years she is still in her master's hand, reminisces about the past.
- Flamme's journal was opened as if he had expected Frieren to come; he wrote that he had talked with his old comrades at Oreol, a place at the far northern edge of the continent that people call heaven. The location is specifically Ende, which is now where the Demon King's Castle stands.

### 3.6.1. Meeting with Stark:
- Freeren was targeting the magic tome in the crimson dragon’s nest, but the dragon was so strong that she got beaten once. Because it wouldn’t die from a single blow, she planned to use a strategy of accumulating damage, but when Fern was terrified and strongly objected, she decided to find a vanguard.
- The warrior to take the vanguard was Eisen’s disciple Stark, who, after fighting his master Eisen, was defending a village that was being attacked by the crimson dragon. Stark had only stepped forward because he had no choice and was too scared to do anything, but somehow the dragon withdrew and people treated him like a hero, which made him uncomfortable. Freeren recognizes Stark’s true worth despite his belittling himself as a coward and helps him.
- At first Stark, frightened and unwilling to fight, decides to face the dragon after talking with Fern, and Freeren, seeing Stark fell the dragon with a single strike, is convinced that Eisen’s eye had not been mistaken. At the same time, Freeren shows some lingering spite toward Stark for calling her “halmanggu” (old woman).

### 3.6.2. The County of Granat:
- While passing through the County of Granat, Frieren spots the demon Ryugner and his party walking about and immediately levels her staff. Ryugner and his group, who are under Aura of the Chilbung County Guillotine — an old nemesis of the hero Himmel's party — had come to the village as envoys, so Frieren is promptly restrained by soldiers under the Count of Granat and thrown into prison. While staying quietly in her cell, Frieren tells Fern and Stark that the demon race cannot communicate with humanity, then instantly defeats the demon Dratt who had come to kill her.
- Fearing that if she remained in prison she would be mistaken for the murderer of the soldiers Dratt killed, Frieren breaks out, leaves Ryugner and Rinie to Fern and Stark, and goes to confront Aura of the Chilbung County Guillotine, "Aura."
- In the past Frieren had blasted Aura's army of the undead away with offensive magic, but after being scolded by Himmel she held back her magic and rendered the army powerless. Aura could not understand Frieren's seemingly needless actions, and even less understood Frieren saying it was "for Himmel," who had long since been dead.
- Seeing this, Frieren again shows her disgust, saying the demon race is incomprehensible. Having seen through Aura's tactics, Frieren deceives her with her mana so that Aura uses the Scales of Obedience, then orders Aura, now her puppet, to commit suicide, ending their long-standing enmity.
- After receiving a grand welcome from the citizens of the County of Granat, Frieren and her party head to the magic city Oisterst, where the Continental Magic Association is located, upon being told that a first-class mage license is required to go north.

### 3.6.3. Winter of the Northern Empire:
- While crossing the Schweir Mountains, Frieren and her party are caught in a blizzard and face a crisis. Fortunately there is a nearby shelter hut, so they take refuge there for a while, and there they meet the elf warrior-monk Kraft, who was doing squats. Seeing a fellow of their kind after several hundred years, they feel moved, but both show that they don’t really understand each other well.
- Frieren, Fern, Stark, and Kraft spend half a year together until the snow stops and grow somewhat close. Kraft talks about her faith and, since Frieren doesn’t believe in the goddess, offers to go to heaven and praise Frieren on her behalf; Frieren refuses, saying she already had a good cleric friend.
- Twenty-nine years after Himmel’s death, Frieren’s party stops by the famous Sword Village, known for caring for the Hero’s Sword, because they had once been asked by the village’s former chieftain to help slay monsters.
- After vanquishing the monsters with Fern and Stark, they go to see the Hero’s Sword. When Stark wonders that if Himmel pulled the Hero’s Sword it shouldn’t be here, Frieren reveals the truth: Himmel actually did not pull the Hero’s Sword. The reason people believe Himmel pulled it is only political, but Himmel took pride in being a true hero who saved the world even without the Hero’s Sword.
- While reading a grimoire in an inn in a village of the Apeti region, Frieren remembers that today is Stark’s birthday. Fern complains that Frieren didn’t say earlier and so she couldn’t prepare a present; with a confident look, Frieren says Flamme told her men like this sort of thing and shows a potion that melts only clothes.
- Angry that Frieren still had such a potion after being told to return it long ago, Fern snatches the potion and pours it over Stark’s head, causing his clothes to melt. Because the potion was rare, a sullen Frieren wraps herself in a single blanket and, while wondering what gift to give, finds a letter from Eisen in her bag. It contains a recipe — Eisen’s extra-large hamburger steak for warriors — and she makes a hamburger steak for Stark’s birthday. Explaining to a puzzled Fern, who doesn’t know warrior customs, Frieren says there’s a tradition of feeding warriors a hamburger on their birthday.
- However, Stark also doesn’t really know the details of the hamburger-on-your-birthday tradition, so they spend the evening reminiscing over dinner.

### 3.6.4. Meeting with Jain:
- While Frieren's party was heading to Oisest, they find a young man named Jain in a swamp, on the verge of drowning. He laments that he never went adventuring with a friend in the past and begs for help; Frieren, not wanting to get her hands dirty, spends a long time thinking up a spell to get him out of the swamp and gets scolded by Fern.
- Afterwards, the party parts with Jain to hurry on to the next village, but when Stark is bitten by a venomous snake and cannot be cured with ordinary priestly holy magic, Jain slips in and lightly heals him.
- Jain once dreamed of being an adventurer but, blocked by the realities of life, had been living however he could. Seeing her own past reflected in the regretful, resigned Jain, Frieren becomes stubbornly determined to recruit him as a companion.
- Even after Frieren's persistent persuasion, Jain stubbornly refuses. Frieren, having learned from Jain's older brother that Jain likes an older woman, boldly claims to be older than Jain and steps forward. But Jain bluntly expresses his disappointment, saying that's not the kind of "older sister" he meant, so Frieren shows off a seduction technique she learned from her master.
- Even her trump card — a hand-kiss that once knocked Himmel unconscious — fails to work, and Frieren becomes frustrated. Jain then declares he has no intention of becoming an adventurer, reasoning that the friend who urged him to adventure has been out of contact for ten years and must already be dead. Frieren points out that he has never even tried to go meet them and is giving up for no reason.
- Frieren warns that if he stays like this he'll regret it someday and it's not too late even now, but Jain says he can't leave because of his older brother. Jain's brother, angry that his younger brother wasted his time in the village for such a reason, snaps that they shouldn't be equated — he doesn't regret staying in the village, but asks how long Jain will keep regretting things.
- Stirred by Frieren's advice and his brother's rebuke, Jain comes to his senses, joins Frieren's party, and sets out as an adventurer to look for his friend.
- Jain is the only adult in Frieren's party, so he mediates conflicts well. From Frieren's perspective, though, he's still practically a child, so she only praises him when he does well.
- While riding in a newly acquired carriage, Fern shows Stark a bracelet decorated with the convulsion-flower she received as a birthday present from Himmel; Stark boasts that he too received a ring with the convulsion-flower motif from Himmel and tries to put it on to match Fern's couple look, when they're attacked by a new kind of monster. The carriage is lifted into the air; if they kill the monster there, the carriage will fall to the ground. They worry how to deal with the falling carriage and decide just before it strikes to absorb the impact.
- It was half a gamble, but fortunately the carriage only sustains some damage and everyone is safe. However Frieren's ring goes missing, so while the carriage is being repaired she wakes in the night and goes out searching for it. On the day the repair finishes, Fern, noticing Frieren being up to something at night, comes to help.
- Fern says she'll delay their departure until the ring is found; Frieren replies that the things she received from Himmel weren't just the ring, so it's fine and if she can't find it tonight she'll give it up cleanly. Fern, however, explains the flower's meaning and suggests that Himmel's gift wasn't a simple gesture, and asks the party for help.
- Fortunately the carriage owner has a magical grimoire that can find lost jewelry, and Frieren successfully recovers the ring.
- Later, as Frieren's party wanders the Raup Uplands, they come across a village whose inhabitants are all asleep. Realizing it's the work of a monster's curse, they go to deal with the monster that is the source of the curse. Except for Jain, who is immune to the curse, Stark and Fern fall asleep in that order, and finally Frieren herself succumbs.
- Frieren had told her acquaintances that whenever they fought monsters they must wake her if needed. Jain, struggling against a sub-species of Chaos that can reflect magic, panics at the thought that even if he breaks the curse it would only last five seconds — if he thoughtlessly wakes Frieren she might use powerful magic without understanding the situation and damage the village. Remembering Haita's advice, he wakes Frieren.
- The moment she sees the Chaos variant and recognizes its magic-reflecting trait, Frieren precisely targets its core and defeats the Chaos without any collateral damage. The awakened villagers thank them, and as they depart Frieren praises Jain, ruffling his hair and saying they survived this time because of him. Jain, however, regrets that the village's representative older woman was really pretty.
- Later, when Frieren's party stops by a village in Roa Gado, they learn that Jain's warrior friend Gorilla went in the exact opposite direction of Oisest. They decide to wait out the month-long cold snap before deciding what to do next. While staying in the village, Frieren asks Jain to mediate an argument between Fern and Stark, who have been fighting.
- Tired of Stark and Fern's love quarrel, Jain laments, and Frieren comforts him, saying they're really trying hard. When Jain suddenly wonders why she is looking after him, Frieren says she was annoyed because he reminded her of how she used to be before the demon lord was defeated. Jain asks if that's really the only reason she's looking out for him, and Frieren answers that traveling with Himmel gave her the courage to set out on a journey and the joy of being with companions — just like the hero Himmel, she wanted to give Jain an opportunity.
- Hearing this, Jain has an epiphany: he no longer wants to live with regrets, and he sets out to search for his friend. Frieren is not too worried, saying Jain is an adult and will manage well on his own, and she looks forward to the day they meet again.

### 3.6.5. Rank-1 Mage Examination:
#### 3.6.5.1. First Round Exam Teams:
- Party 1: Metode, Ton, Renge
- Party 2: Frieren, Rabine, Kanne
- Party 4: Pern, Wibel, Rant
- Party 8: Bierbel, Ere, Sharp
- Party 13: Denken, Richter, Raupen
- Party 17: Edel, Brai, Dunst

#### 3.6.5.2. First Exam:
- After parting with Jaine, Frieren's party arrives at Oisterst. Fern, who didn’t feel confident passing the exam alone, strongly requests Frieren to take it with her, and Frieren agrees. Frieren is unsure whether the emblem of growth from her previous mage certification still works, but fortunately first-class mage Lernen recognizes her and writes Frieren’s name on the list for the first-class mage exam.
- In the first test, which is to capture the Uncheoljo, Frieren teams up with the pair of third-class mages Ravine and Kanne. Seeing the two bickering from the start, Frieren senses a rough road ahead and adopts a serene expression, but when she realizes their teamwork is better than expected she devises a plan to capture the Uncheoljo.
- To catch the Uncheoljo, which is sensitive to mana and flies faster than the speed of sound, Frieren subtly spreads mana along the waterside except for the puddle they’d occupied, preventing the bird from approaching. Frieren then limits her own mana so the Uncheoljo can’t detect it and, while it draws near, uses a spell that catches birds to capture it.
- However, second-class mages Denken and Richter and third-class mage Laufen, who had noticed Frieren and the others’ scheme, come to steal the Uncheoljo. Unable to counter Laufen’s high-speed movement magic, Frieren has the Uncheoljo taken from her with little difficulty. To protect Laufen, Denken and Richter step in front of Frieren. While Denken faces Frieren, Richter goes after Ravine and Kanne to subdue or kill them.
- Denken, who has skill at the level of a first-class mage, launches a variety of powerful, flawless attacks, but Frieren, whose abilities transcend the first-class realm, instantly spots Denken’s weak point and retaliates. After subduing Denken, Frieren demands Laufen’s location, but Denken invokes the right to remain silent. As if expecting that, Frieren tramples Denken and threatens to kill him unless Laufen comes out.
- In truth Frieren never intended to kill Denken. Sensing this, Denken begs Laufen not to come out, but Frieren, unable to stall any longer, aims to shoot off Denken’s arm. Laufen, who doesn’t see this, tries to save Denken, but Frieren, already familiar with Laufen’s magic, doesn’t get caught twice and easily captures Laufen.
- In front of the subdued Denken and Laufen, Frieren begins deciphering the barrier Jerie had set up in the exam hall — not for herself, but for Ravine and Kanne. She feels sorry for Kanne, who is constrained by the exam rules and can’t freely use her power, saying she can’t imagine a water mage beating someone in the rain. When the barrier is actually broken and Kanne is able to freely manipulate the rainwater, Kanne defeats Richter, who was stronger than her. Time runs out and Frieren’s group passes the exam.
- Back at the lodging, Frieren takes Fern and Stark to a restaurant she once went to with Himmel to cheer up Fern, who is furious because Stark had been partying while Fern was away. Fern is pleased, saying the taste has changed from the past but is much better now. Frieren, however, is sullen because her emergency funds have been spent.
- The next day they need to buy groceries, but Frieren oversleeps and misses it, making Fern angry again. When Fern grumbles that she wants a snack, Frieren goes to check whether any emergency money remains.
- At the snack shop, Fern can’t find anything she likes and frets about it, but fortunately Ravine and Kanne come to thank Frieren for helping them during the exam. As Himmel had said in the past, Frieren feels pleased to see someone expressing gratitude for her good deed, and Fern’s mood is lifted thanks to the snacks Ravine and Kanne brought.

#### 3.6.5.3. Second Exam:
- The second exam is overseen by first-class mage Zenze, who, as a notoriously strict examiner, chooses the infamous dungeon “King’s Tomb of Perish” as the testing ground. The condition for passing this exam is reaching the deepest chamber. Cooperation among companions is advantageous, but examinees generally go their own way. Seeing this, Frieren says there’s no hope and heads to the tomb with Fern.
- As a mage who has cleared the most dungeons, Frieren easily makes her way through, and midway finds a treasure chest and uses an identification spell to discover it’s a mimic. Still, refusing to give up the 1% possibility, Frieren touches the mimic and is swallowed.
- Zenze, who thought Frieren and Fern were the most promising, says he may have misjudged people after seeing this.
- After a series of comedic mishaps, the dungeon monster Spiegel creates clones of the examinees that attack them. The clones, nearly perfect replicas of the originals, put the examinees in danger, and the Frieren clone blocks the path to the deepest chamber, acting as the final boss of the episode.
- To defeat the Frieren clone, all the examinees unite to take it on. Fern exploits Frieren’s weakness and corners the clone. But the title of archmage, earned over a thousand years, doesn’t leave its owner; Fern is reversed in an instant and defeated. Seizing the moment, the real Frieren finds the clone’s weak spot and severs its lifeline, then kills Spiegel in the deepest chamber and safely passes the exam.
- However, Frieren has no time to enjoy the honor because she had been swallowed when she touched the mimic. Afterward, Frieren advises Fern to throw away the damaged staff and buy a new one, but Fern, who treasures the staff given by Heiter, refuses to discard it and argues. Hearing this, Frieren changes her mind and goes to see Richter, a second-class mage who is also a restorer.
- Richter, depressed from failing the exam, brusquely asks why Frieren is carrying around a smashed piece of junk. Frieren corrects him, saying it’s not trash. In the end Richter accepts the job, carefully repairs the cherished staff, and admits his earlier words were a slip — it is a good staff.
- That night, Fern, who had complained to Stark, looks at the repaired staff placed on the bed and remembers Heiter saying Frieren was a good teacher, and feels grateful to Frieren.

#### 3.6.5.4. 3rd Exam:
- The third exam was unusually overseen personally by Jerie, the head of the Magic Association, to weed out a large number of successful candidates. Frieren guessed Jerie had no intention of passing them, but she didn’t complain much because Jerie’s instincts were always right.
- After it was Frieren’s turn, Jerie noticed that although she didn’t intend to pass the exam, her reason was different from the other examinees’. Jerie asked back if she really didn’t plan to pass, then said he’d give her a special chance and told her to name her favorite magic. She answered “a magic that makes a flower field,” and Jerie, sensing it was a trivial spell learned from Flamme, announced her failure.
- Frieren left the room without any disappointment since she had expected the result, but warned Jerie that Fern would not turn out the way he thought. Sure enough, Jerie immediately passed Fern after seeing that she possessed prodigious talent on a level since Flamme. When Fern came out and announced her acceptance, Frieren patted her head and praised her.
- The next day, while Frieren was collecting grimoires around Oisterst, she witnessed an old woman who had spilled her fruit and hesitated to help, when Biebel appeared and helped the woman first. When Biebel scolded her, asking if she pretended not to see it, Frieren replied that she had hesitated a little.
- From Biebel Frieren learned that Biebel only realized there had been peace thanks to Himmel after he died and the demons ran rampant and she lost her homeland, and that’s why she fights. Hearing that, Frieren felt how the world was changing because of Himmel.
- At the Rank-1 mage conferment ceremony, a sulky Jerie notified Frieren not to set foot in the Magic Association for the next thousand years, so she couldn’t participate. Frieren said she didn’t care anyway since she didn’t want to take part, but she wore the embarrassed look she gets in awkward moments and was actually quite upset. Fern and Stark seemed to notice this; after looking at each other, Stark volunteered to wait outside with Frieren.
- Waiting outside with Stark for Fern’s ceremony, Frieren was happy thinking Fern would be praised in the future as a greater mage than herself. At that moment, Rank-1 mage Lernen picked a fight and attacked, claiming he wanted to leave a memory for their master Jerie, and Frieren was wounded. Frieren said that neither masters nor students are honest, and Jerie revealed that he had always cherished his student, which brought the fight to a halt.
- Afterwards, Frieren, Fern, and Stark left the Magic Association and headed to the northern plateau.

### 3.6.6. Golden Scent:
- While Frieren and her party were passing through the Baije Highlands, they accept a request from 1st-class mage Lernen and go to the area near the Golden Scent where the final seven-ringed Macht is sealed. The keeper of the Golden Scent is Denken, and noticing Denken's wish to restore his hometown that had turned to gold, Frieren is about to leave immediately.
- When Fern and Stark offer to help because Frieren is acting unusually cold, Frieren replies that she worries for her companions' lives, that she was defeated by Macht in the past, and that even now she does not know a way to win.
- But Frieren softens at the sight of Denken thinking about his hometown, and, curious that Macht did not kill Denken despite being inside the Golden Scent, decides to hear Macht's story.
- Macht explains that, under the orders of Lord Glück of Baije, he wore a bracelet of domination and swore “to serve the people of Baije and their descendants for generations,” and “not to harbor malice toward the people; if he ever harbored even the slightest malice, he would take his own life.” Because of that, Denken, a resident of Baije, could enter the Golden Scent and still return alive. Frieren says she wants to try one thing, and as Denken’s friend goes to Baije, she is not attacked by Macht.
- But when Frieren provokes by saying they fought 600 years ago, the party scolds her actions; Frieren answers that she had something she wanted to confirm, and that she discovered she cannot beat Macht now and that Macht is not flawless.
- Interested by Frieren’s story, Macht claims he is earnestly trying to coexist with humanity, but cannot understand the question of “if you insist on coexistence, why do you kill people?” Frieren judges that conversing with Macht is a waste of time and that demons are a different kind of being from humans.
- The party returns to their lodgings and 1st-class mage Lernen and 2nd-class mage Edel interpret the memories Macht has read out. Frieren spends two months defenseless creating the resources needed to complete the ritual to lift the Golden Curse from the hundred years’ worth of memories. At the same time, an anonymous great demon, Soliter, attempts to free Macht, and just before the ritual to lift the Golden Curse is nearly complete, Macht is released.
- Frieren: "The memory interpretation is finished. Diagorze is, from this moment, no longer a 'curse.'"
- While Fern, Stark, and Denken each buy time by fighting Soliter and Macht respectively, Frieren completes the ritual to lift the Golden Curse.
- First, Frieren restores Denken, who had turned to gold, and then engages in battle. Frieren says that Macht’s efforts to understand humanity for the sake of coexistence are admirable, and that, as far as she knows, Macht is the second demon after the Demon Lord to understand humanity so well. However, Frieren says Macht has killed too many people, so the ultimate end of his wish is the extinction of humankind, and she can no longer overlook that.
- She leaves Macht’s opponent to Denken, who was his student in the past and knows Macht best, and Frieren herself fights Soliter. Their mana levels are even, but Soliter has the edge in magic control, so Frieren struggles. When even the secret of her limiting her magic is seen through, she tries to kill Soliter outright, and enraged by Soliter’s claim that he killed Fern and Stark, she reveals her full power.
- But the gap is still too wide for Frieren alone to win, so she stakes everything on the claim that Soliter’s words were lies and casts the ritual to lift the Golden Curse over the whole of Baije. Then, as Frieren kills Soliter—who had taken a mortal wound from Fern’s shot—Macht is also killed by Denken, and peace returns to Baije. After recovering, Frieren buys many magical items in Baije.

### 3.6.7. The Goddess's Stele:
- Frieren retries the goddess's stele that she had investigated and failed to solve during the past Demon King subjugation adventure. She says a week of her memories from that time are missing, so she doesn't really know what happened.
- The stele had decayed with time, but new graffiti that hadn't been there before was carved into it. When Frieren read it, she was sent back to the time when Himmel's party was traveling, eighty years earlier. It was 53 years before Himmel would die, seven years after the hero Himmel's party began their adventures.
- Frieren is at a loss because touching the stele produces no reaction. She worries that if it becomes known she has come from the future, it will cause a large alteration to the future, so she can't act decisively.
- Then Chart, a remnant-demon under Grauzam the Miracle who sensed the space–time disturbance, ambushes and teleports Frieren into the air. Unable to use flight magic, she cancels her fall speed precisely to land and join the battle, but she is frustrated because she can't use Zoltrak and must rely on slower, high-power offensive magic.
- Chart is a powerful demon, but with timely support from her companions (Himmel, Heiter, Eisen) Frieren easily overwhelms him. Chart, in a last-ditch effort, teleports an entire cliff away, putting them at risk of being crushed; the companions, however, strangely remain calm, which puzzles Frieren.
- The companions (Himmel, Heiter, Eisen) had already noticed that Frieren was acting very different from usual and suspected she was hiding some secret. Frieren, forced into a corner, uses future magic and kills Chart with Zoltrak.
- Frieren decides she can no longer keep the secret and reveals that she came from eighty years in the future. Her companions are surprised but accept it, saying it makes sense since she showed such amazing magic.
- When Heiter asks Frieren whether those of their future defeated the Demon King, she hesitates. Himmel says, what good would hearing that do? Even if they fail, they will not stop challenging—so they decide not to hear anything about the future.
- Frieren returns to the village and apologizes to her companions for dragging them into an annoying affair, but Himmel, saying he gets excited by difficult tasks, brushes it off, and Frieren realizes anew that Himmel was that kind of man.
- Afterwards, as usual, Frieren and her companions (Himmel, Heiter, Eisen) run errands helping troubled people and fight monsters. Frieren realizes that the combinations she naturally used in the future were techniques she learned from these companions and feels grateful.
- Continuing the investigation, Frieren and her companions face the northern highlands' strongest dragon, the Topaz Dragon, and barely defeat it using her future magic and the companions' coordinated attacks. After the fierce battle, she feels nostalgia at the raucous atmosphere, different from when she adventured with Fern and Stark.
- The companions are impressed and, seeing Frieren in a more relaxed state in the future where she even took on disciples, feel reassured—until they watch her get swallowed by a mimic as usual and revoke that impression.
- After much hardship with little to show for it, the long-lived dwarf artisan Kisel reveals that a book about the goddess's stele is in the old village chief's house where he lived long ago. The village has long been abandoned and become a monster den. Frieren's party first tries a frontal assault but falls into numerical inferiority and retreats. They ultimately infiltrate and take the shortest route to the chief's house to obtain information about the stele.
- For Frieren to return to the future, she must touch the stele and use the goddess's magic, but the spell name is not written, so they are back at square one. Eisen says interpreting the scripture would take decades, and suggests that living in this era could be an option. Frieren replies that through this adventure she learned much and found precious people, and expresses her intention to return home after the adventure.
- Himmel, learning that Frieren in the future is still enjoying trivial travels where they all laugh together, vows to find the spell name after they defeat the Demon King and asks if anything was written on the stele. Frieren recalls the spell name she had forgotten: "Piara Tor."
- Frieren is about to reveal the spell name now, but Himmel refuses, saying they should find it themselves in the future and inscribe it on the stele so they can send her back personally—he refuses to hear it.
- Meanwhile, sensing the space–time disturbance, the Demon King sends the nameless great demon Soliterre, Grauzam the Miracle of Chilbung-hyeon, Tot the Ultimate Saint, and Rivalle the Bloodstained War God to steal future information and assassinate Frieren.
- As Frieren heads for the stele, a high-speed presence startles her, and at that moment Rivalle knocks Eisen far away. Then Soliterre's attack also begins, turning the scene into chaos. Seeing a sword-summoning magic, Frieren realizes Soliterre is here and remains on guard.
- She feels uneasy detecting two great demons by mana sensing but cannot identify the problem, and is horrified when Grauzam appears right beside her. As she rushes to attack, Grauzam has already activated mind magic and traps her in an illusion.
- In Grauzam's illusion, Frieren and Himmel dream that after defeating the Demon King they get married; just as they are about to kiss, Himmel snaps awake and asks if there is any way out. Frieren has already come to her senses but says she has no way to break Grauzam's illusion herself. Himmel insists it must be possible and reaches for the sword's location.
- With his excellent senses, Himmel grasps the real battlefield and, while still caught in the illusion, fights and overwhelms Grauzam. With Heiter joining the attack, they inflict serious injuries on Grauzam and succeed in breaking the illusion.

# 4. Frieren's Abilities:
## 4.1. Mage:
- Frieren is one of the strongest mages currently alive. She is one of three who have earned the title of Archmage.
- As a disciple of Flamme, who is called the progenitor of human magic, Frieren’s skill was outstanding from the start. The only ones universally recognized as unquestionably surpassing Frieren are her master Flamme and her master’s master Jerie.

However, matchup matters a great deal in duels between mages, and despite being over a thousand years old her technique is somewhat rough, so Frieren has lost eleven times to mages with less mana than her, six of whom were human.
- In the Rank-1 mage exam, Frieren casually subdued Denken, a court mage and capable practitioner, as if instructing an apprentice, and she dismantled the barrier set by Jerie — which Genau swore “couldn’t be dispelled even if heaven and earth were overturned.” Jerie laughed it off, saying, “A fanfare for a greeting once every thousand years. In the world of magic, heaven and earth can be turned over.”

When the two pressured it in coordinated fashion, the duplicate’s mere gaze slammed Fern into a wall; Fern said that magic couldn’t even perceive that spell, so he couldn’t defend against it, and he was awed, feeling it to be the pinnacle of magic.

- Frieren also succeeded in dispelling the barrier magic of Boeje, one of the Chilbung territories. Finally, she read a hundred years’ worth of memories that recorded the golden magic of Macht — a spell she had once been defeated by — in two months and ultimately deciphered it.
- The terrifying thing about Frieren is that she manages to analyze and understand magic even from opponents overwhelmingly stronger than her, like Jerie, or spells from the Chilbung territories that are due to racial characteristics inexplicable to her and that are clearly stronger than she is.
- Frieren is powerful in many ways, but she has a fatal weakness: “the instant she casts a spell, mana detection is cut off for a very short moment.” That’s a mistake apprentices make, and Frieren hasn’t corrected it even though she’s aware of it. She’s always been clumsy in that way. But her other abilities are so outstanding that most people neither notice the gap nor exploit it. In fact, when facing Frieren’s duplicate, Frieren and Fern tried to use this weakness, but it didn’t work well, and the fight turned into a war of attrition.
- Frieren’s level of skill was built more by long years of practice than by raw talent; Jerie, who stands at the pinnacle of mages, judges Frieren’s technique as lacking for her age.
- Because Frieren invested a great deal of time in training mana suppression, she had relatively less time to develop other techniques. For that reason, when fighting Soliter, whose tricks don’t work against mana suppression, she struggled from a lack of technique and only barely won.
- Had Frieren invested the time she spent on mana suppression training into other disciplines, she would be several times stronger.
- Frieren is clearly a top-tier mage, but she is not a mage who could sweep everything alone. However, Frieren’s growth has not stopped. Her magical ability continues to grow even now, and some of the eleven mages who beat her in the past she could now defeat.
- Frieren does possess great talent. Even before meeting Flamme she was already the strongest in her village; while the other elves in her village were wiped out, Frieren was strong enough to fight and kill the demon lord general Bajart head-on. Flamme noticed that talent and took her as a disciple. Moreover, Aura — whose mana amount was more than humans could approach — was overwhelmed by Frieren even though Frieren practiced mana suppression while training, which shows how absurdly great her talent was.
- Even Jerie, who is discerning, judged Frieren strong when they first met a thousand years ago. It’s just that Frieren devoted her talent entirely to mana suppression training to kill demons, so she never pursued the ambition needed to reach the very top.
- Though she is both talented and skilled, Frieren didn’t become a Rank-1 mage because she is not the sort of ambitious mage Jerie values. By Frieren’s abilities, most Rank-1 mages would be no match, but Jerie values ambition — the pursuit of greater power — above all. Frieren, who prefers an easygoing life obsessed with magic and values mana suppression techniques to kill demons over seeking greater power, was rejected for being the opposite.
- Still, Frieren is someone whom famous contemporary mages like Denken and Lerennen cannot help but acknowledge, and as a member of Himmel’s party she receives treatment above that of a Rank-1 mage.

Frieren has said that if a skilled warrior closes the distance, her neck would be cut before she could cast a spell; against Stark, she asserted there’s no way to win if the gap is within a dozen paces. Unless she had the close-combat skills of someone like Beerbel or Laufen, Frieren has no such ability.

## 4.2. Frieren of the Long Funeral:
- The so-called "Plamme school" running Plamme -> Frieren -> Fern has one distinguishing feature. It is an extreme, combat-focused school that devotes everything solely to killing demons more efficiently. The training method is surprisingly simple: in basic practice you always suppress the mana emitted outward so that it never exceeds one-tenth of your total mana. The key is to keep this mana limit maintained at all times.
- Suppressing mana leaking outward is something any magic user can do, but maintaining it for long periods is very difficult, and while suppressing it the mana wavers and becomes unstable. However, if you constantly suppress it until it becomes as natural as breathing, those wavering and instabilities disappear. By doing this, mages, priests, and demons who can visualize an opponent’s mana are tricked into mistaking the suppressed mana for the total mana pool.
- Frieren’s total mana is so great that she is even, in total, superior to the strongest great-demon Chilbung-hyeon, yet the amount of mana she normally displays is at best that of a reasonably skilled non-mage, and with effort she can even eliminate emitted mana entirely.
- Frieren uses this suppressed mana and the complacency it induces in opponents who see only the suppressed level to strike unexpectedly. This tactic was devised by Plamme to deceive demons, based on the assumption that in a direct confrontation demons would not suppress their mana. Because it was a tactic aimed solely at demons, some demons who realized the truth said things like “you don’t even deserve to be called mages,” and Plamme himself wryly said that it was enough for them alone to mock magic.
- Because Frieren usually suppresses her mana like this, to those who don’t know the truth she reads as a reasonably skilled mage by ordinary human standards—about one-fifth the power of Hiter, the priest of the hero party—but when she releases the suppression the mana she shows is enormous, easily overwhelming the Chilbung-hyeon under the Demon King.
- Visually, a high-ranking demon mage’s mana might be three to four times the width of his shoulders; the aura of a great-demon who has lived 500 years is the size of a small room; Frieren’s mana is the size of a mansion and can’t be properly contained.
- You can judge Frieren’s stature by the title demons give her: Frieren of the Long Funeral. She is called that because she is the mage who has slain the most demons in history.
- In other words, “Frieren of the Long Funeral” is a double meaning: it refers both to Frieren’s fate—her long life forcing her to see loved ones leave—and to her being a demon butcher who personally buried countless demons.
- When Frieren was in the hero party she defeated Chilbung-hyeon’s miracle Grauzam, the Exclusion of Immortality, and the Demon King. After Himmel’s death, the great-demons she personally destroyed include Keubal of Corruption and the Guillotine Aura of Chilbung-hyeon.
- Frieren did not directly kill Mahrt of the Golden Fragrance, but she greatly contributed to his subjugation by lifting Mahrt’s curse.
- It was Fern’s long-range Zoltrak that directly killed Soliter, but Frieren fought Soliter to set up that situation and at the same time removed Mahrt’s curse.

## 4.3. Top-tier Adventurer:
- Frieren’s magical ability often overshadows other aspects, but having lived for many years she has learned countless experiences, and above all because she adventured with the hero Himmel’s party, she ranks among first-rate adventurers.
- Above all, Frieren is trying to teach those around her the attitude of enjoying adventure that she learned from Himmel.
- Frieren is highly talented but guides Fern and Stark, who are still inexperienced as adventurers. In the past Frieren was an efficiency-minded person who valued results over process, but after joining Himmel’s party she was influenced by the hero Himmel and learned to enjoy adventuring.
- Frieren has cleared more dungeons than any current adventurer, so she handled the dungeon clearance used as the second exam for first-class mages most easily. Above all, she enjoys dungeon delving itself, so she positively overcomes any obstacle. She showed a knack for finding hidden rooms that can’t be identified from a map.
- The problem is that Frieren’s enthusiasm sometimes goes too far: even when divination magic showed a 99% chance of being a mimic, she would still pursue the remaining 1% because great mages once obtained grimoires through that 1%. She has often been swallowed by mimics, and Fern frequently looks at her with a scornful expression and rescues her. This seems to be a habit carried over from the Himmel party, and she’s been caught so often that Frieren is well-versed in how to deal with being swallowed by a mimic.
- Another problem of Frieren’s is that her money management is terrible. Because she has no desire for money, she almost always accepts payment for jobs in the form of useless spells instead of cash, and when she wants rare grimoires or magical items she buys them without considering the price, so her money runs out quickly.

Sometimes they take commissions from great nobles or wealthy clients and receive large sums, but Frieren’s extravagance makes it useless.

However, as she herself admits, it isn’t enough to replace a cleric, so the cleric Jain joined the party. After Jain left, Frieren has been using the herbal notes he left behind to handle crises as best she can.
- One drawback of Frieren is that, while she is an archmage who ranks among the strongest in the world when it comes to magic, physically she is at best as weak as an ordinary person. The same goes for her apprentice Fern. Frieren is easily affected by everything from minor illnesses like colds to poisons and curses, and if bitten by a mimic, when alone she has no strength to escape on her own without magic.
- Moreover, against opponents immune to magic or when overwhelmed by sheer numbers, even archmage Frieren is helpless. In other words, Frieren is great enough to be called an archmage in terms of magic, but that applies only to magic — in other areas her limits are clear.
- The efficiency of magic is so great compared to other abilities that it could be called almost万能, but being skilled only in magic doesn’t make everything perfect. Frieren knows this well, so for Fern — who, unlike her, has no means to protect his body — she prioritized teaching defensive magic above all else until he reached the highest level. She planned to recruit a melee warrior first when going on adventures with Fern, saying a frontline fighter was essential; she kept trying to recruit Eisen and, seeing Stark’s training process, resolved to definitely take him along.

## 4.4. Magic Used:
### 4.4.1. Primary Magic:
#### 4.4.1.1. Mana Output Control (Mana Suppression):
- A specialty of Frieren learned from her master, Archmage Flamero, and a surefire tactic in battles against demonkind. She suppresses her mana output to one-tenth, deceiving others about her true mana to lure them into complacency. It's already difficult to regulate mana output to precisely the desired level, and controlling the characteristic fluctuations that occur when suppressing it makes the technique not particularly practical. Moreover, demons are more sensitive than humans to mana fluctuations, so even if suppressed for a hundred or two hundred years, they can't be easily deceived.
- However, Frieren trained for most of her life to limit it without any sense of incongruity, and she can use it so precisely, without error, that even the demon race — even the Chilbung-hyeon — cannot feel any tremor.
- Frieren's mana suppression is so perfect that the great demon Soliter, despite having learned through investigation that Frieren was suppressing her mana, was astonished when facing her in person at being unable to detect any traces of suppression; when Frieren worried whether he had told other demons, he told her not to worry—no one would believe it anyway because the technique is so flawless.
- According to Jerie, Frieren's mana suppression was so clean that the only one who saw through it at a glance was the Demon King.

Over the years she modified it on the premise of facing demons rather than humans, so Ryugner, who had once experienced Frieren's Zoltrak in the past, judged that the current Zoltrak should no longer be called "a spell that kills people" but "a spell that kills demons."

#### 4.4.1.5. Defensive Magic:
- It blocks physical and magical attacks with small hexagonal shields, and multiple shields can be linked together to widen the protection area. It has remarkable performance that perfectly blocks almost all offensive spells, including Zoltrac, but its drawback is high mana consumption.
- Defensive magic in the past did not reach this level. The turning point in defensive magic’s development was the appearance of Zoltrac, a killing spell developed by Kval, the Corrupted Sage. Zoltrac could not be stopped by the old level of defensive magic, so humanity united to analyze Zoltrac; it was incorporated into humanity’s basic offensive magic and became widespread. From that followed new defensive rites developed to counter Zoltrac, which led to the current state.
- Because defensive magic now has such excellent performance with little room left for improvement, the Fern generation’s offensive magic mainly focuses on manipulating materials like earth or water or launching surprise attacks before defensive magic can be used. This is because defensive magic is fundamentally structured to defend against Zoltrac by tuning into and dispersing the opponent’s mana, making it very strong against mana-clashing attacks but less capable of bearing physical mass—so it can be easily shattered if overwhelmed by a large mass.

#### 4.4.1.6. Mental Defense:
- As a seasoned mage, Frieren's mental defenses are strong. They were constructed in a complex and sturdy way, but because the ritual is quite old, vulnerabilities may be exposed to specialists in mental magic like Edel.

### 4.4.2. Other Magic:
- These are the miscellaneous spells Frieren studies as a hobby. Except for the special accommodation spell developed for demons, most of them are the sort of spells utterly useless in combat. Yet the true value of these spells is that, compared to ordinary offensive magic, both the rarity of their ritual structures and the level of technical skill involved are on a completely different level—legendary magic.
- Combat magic is meant for battlefields where life and death are decided in seconds, so its ritual structures are mechanical, simplified, streamlined, and optimized; everyday magic is different. For example, the 「spell that creates a flowerbed」 requires analyzing the composition and structure of the flowers to be produced and incorporating that into the ritual formula, upgrading it into a "spell that makes OO flowers" for use.
- Also, folk cleaning spells like the 「spell that removes hardened grease」 can strip dyes intentionally applied to clothing during the process of removing grime, causing bleaching; therefore, because you must distinguish dyes that must not be removed from contaminants that should be removed, it is important to determine which substances to judge as stains and remove.
- In this way, folk magic is composed of extremely advanced ritual formulas to produce effects for the user's convenience. Moreover, most of these spells have no reason to be widely publicized or taught, and the only means of acquiring them is often nothing more than receiving an original grimoire as a reward in some passing village—paradoxically guaranteeing their rarity and uniqueness even if you searched the entire world.

# 5. Frieren's Relationships:
## 5.1. Plamme:
- Plamme is the magic teacher and benefactor who taught Frieren how to fight demons.
- Plamme also saved her life. When Frieren and Plamme first met, the village where Frieren lived was under attack by the Demon Lord's army; Frieren had barely managed to annihilate the vanguard, but reinforcements were coming. In fact, Plamme pointed out, "If you had stayed there, you would have lost your life."
- Plamme had also lost her family to demons, so she felt a kinship with Frieren, who had suffered the same loss, and they were able to form a comfortable teacher–disciple relationship.
- Plamme only taught Frieren magic for fighting demons and for revenge, which is regrettable, but she did not regret it and believed, "You will surely be able to defeat the Demon Lord."
- Plamme had a great influence on Frieren taking up the hobby of collecting all sorts of seemingly useless spells; she liked simple, modest magic. Plamme advised Frieren to live unobtrusively and that the time to make a name in history would be when she defeated the Demon Lord.
- Plamme suspected that someday her senior disciple Frieren would regret not understanding the human heart, so she left in a book a method for communicating with the dead for her student, and expecting Frieren to find it, she opened that section in her notes and sealed it. When Frieren read her master's notes, she realized her master had already prepared this for her a thousand years ago and lamented that she was still playing in her master's palm. In effect, Plamme is the one who set the purpose of the current journey.
- Though not always obvious, Plamme is extremely important to Frieren. The magic Frieren likes best — the spell that creates a field of flowers — was Plamme's favorite spell in life, and it was Plamme who taught Frieren that spell. Also, when Frieren first encountered the monster that shows visions of the person one holds dearest in their heart, the figure she saw was her teacher Plamme.
- Plamme is Frieren's magic teacher who lived about a thousand years ago and is a human archmage regarded as the origin of human magic. Plamme's sex is female, occupation mage, position Court Mage of the Unified Empire, race human.

## 5.2. The Southern Hero:
- Frieren, whose heart had grown a little numb from the long years, refuses when the Southern Hero comes and asks her to lend her strength to defeat the Demon King. However, the Southern Hero seems unfazed as if he already knew the outcome; he reveals a secret about himself, saying that Frieren would never tell anyone.
- The Southern Hero can see the future, and he foresaw that he would lose his life to Slat of Jeonji and Chilbung-hyeon a year later. Nonetheless, he trusts a future-generation hero who will defeat the Demon King in his stead, says that that hero will change Frieren’s life, and then disappears.

## 5.3. Himmel's Party:
### 5.3.1. Himmel:
- Frieren once helped a young Himmel when he was a little boy and got lost in the woods. Frieren herself had completely forgotten the incident and only recalled it after hearing Himmel tell the story later, but it seems Himmel had been in love with her since then.
- When grown-up Himmel came asking her to join him in defeating the Demon King, Frieren refused, saying she had become emotionally dull after so many years of seclusion. But Himmel argued that what mattered was now, and succeeded in persuading her.
- During the journey, Himmel occasionally expressed his feelings for Frieren in indirect ways, but Frieren, who still did not fully understand humans, never noticed the hidden meanings in his gestures. For example, in one town he took the celebrated Frieren to an accessory shop and indifferently let her pick anything, and the trinket ring he bought her—whether she knew its meaning or not—she always kept carefully tucked away in her luggage while traveling. Besides that ring, Frieren received various other gifts from Himmel and has kept those as well.
- This remained true even after they defeated the Demon King. After defeating the Demon King and seeing the Era meteor, the comrades returned to their separate lives. Because Frieren valued him as a comrade, she kept the promise to go see the Era meteor with Himmel fifty years later and visited him again in his last days. Frieren was inwardly shocked to find Himmel a bald, stooped old man; not having taken into account that her companions had aged, she walked for a week to see the meteor with him. Still, Himmel said it felt like being back in old times and thanked Frieren, saying that thanks to her he had one last enjoyable adventure.
- After that, Himmel died of old age, and people whispered about Frieren’s emotionless demeanor at the funeral—she didn’t shed a single tear. Heiter and Eisen defended her, saying they hadn’t cried either, but when Frieren realized she had known nothing about Himmel and had never once tried to learn more about him over all those years, she was overcome by a flood of regretful tears.
- Frieren’s current new journey is also to finish the conversation she couldn’t have with Himmel, and during the adventure she shows signs of trust in him. For example, when they defeated the demon Ainzam, who showed phantasms of beloved dead, Frieren had expected to see her master again, but when a vision of Himmel appeared saying he himself had changed a little and telling her to shoot at him, she reacted as if that was exactly what would happen, showing her steadfast faith in him.
- When she first met Stark and he asked, “Why do you collect magic?” Frieren at first answered, “A hobby, no special meaning.” But she quickly added, remembering Himmel, “There was a fool who liked my magic.”
- Also, whenever Frieren sees magic tied to the past, it used to be Plammy who appeared, but after Himmel’s death the appearances center on Himmel or Himmel’s party, showing that her emotions accumulated more after his passing.
- When Frieren traveled back in time and got caught in Grauzaam’s spell Anshiresierra, she appeared in Himmel’s dream wearing a wedding dress. The Frieren in the wedding dress, thought to be just a vision of Himmel’s, actually turns out to be the real Frieren, even showing her waiting for Himmel’s kiss — indicating that, at least after Himmel’s death, as she retraced the roads she had traveled with him, moments of remembering him accumulated and her emotions deepened compared to earlier.
- Himmel was the leader and swordsman of the hero party that defeated the Demon King who had reigned for a thousand years, bringing peace across the continent. As leader of the hero party he became the most famous of the party members to later generations, and people refer to him with respect and awe as “the Hero.”
- At age 16, Himmel formed a party with Frieren, Heiter, and Eisen to defeat the Demon King. After defeating the Demon King he lived an ordinary life and passed away at the age of 76. Himmel is male; his profession is swordsman; alias: the Hero; race: human.

### 5.3.2. Heiter:
- The cleric of the hero party. He drinks so much that it's hard to believe he's a religious man, so Frieren and the others teased him as a "corrupt cleric" or a "quack monk." But Heiter is so optimistic that he always lets such remarks go in one ear and out the other.
- Heiter looks like a degenerate adult, but he is gentle and perceptive of the situation around him, so when conflicts sometimes arose within the party he would step in as a mediator and help resolve them smoothly. However, even Heiter reportedly couldn't stand Frieren's laziness — she was weak in the mornings and overslept — and he would get annoyed.
- Occasionally Heiter would click his tongue at Frieren, and according to Fern, that meant Heiter was very angry.
- About twenty years after Himmel's death, the retired bishop Heiter asked Frieren to take Fern as his apprentice, but Frieren politely declined, arguing that many apprentice mages die in real combat and she couldn't put a child entrusted to her by a close friend in danger. Hearing this, Heiter took it in stride and instead asked her to decipher a grimoire found in the tomb of the sage Eibich, which was said to contain a spell for immortality. Frieren accepted, thinking Heiter made the request out of fear as he faced death.
- At that time, Heiter also asked that, if she had spare time while deciphering Eibich's grimoire, she teach Fern. Thanks to this, Fern was able to learn the basics of magic under Frieren. Frieren later said Fern absorbed her teachings with such frightening fervor that it was "too much, to the point of being bad."
- However, the grimoire Heiter had asked Frieren to decipher contained no secret of immortality, and Heiter had already suspected this before reuniting with Frieren. Heiter had simply anticipated that Frieren would refuse if he asked plainly, so he concocted the ruse to buy time until Fern could stand on his own as a mage. Only after finishing the grimoire did Frieren realize Heiter's scheme, and she said, "You tricked me."
- Eventually Fern grew enough that even Frieren acknowledged him as "a mage who can hold his own," but Heiter was already facing death. Heiter told Frieren to take Fern and leave that very day, saying he did not want Fern — who had already lost his family — to have to experience losing someone again.
- Frieren, holding back a choked voice and crying, said that Fern was already prepared to part.
- Frieren: "What you must do before you die is properly say goodbye to that child and give him as many memories as you can." Surprised by this unexpected side of Frieren, Heiter was momentarily at a loss for words, then comforted her by saying she really was a kind person. Lying in bed, he followed Frieren's advice and had a gentle, warm final conversation with Fern. That conversation remained an unforgettable, important memory for Fern.
- After that, Heiter passed away peacefully. After the funeral, Frieren honored his request, took Fern as her apprentice, and set out on an adventure.
- Heiter was a member of the hero party that defeated the Demon King who had ruled for a thousand years and brought peace across the continent. His occupation was cleric.
- Heiter formed the party with Frieren, Himmel, and Eisen at age 16 to defeat the Demon King, and after the Demon King was defeated he retired from the bishopric, cared for the war orphan Fern, and died at the age of 101. Heiter is male; occupation: cleric; race: human. Heiter loves alcohol.

### 5.3.3. Aizen:
- A warrior of the hero party. Dwarf Aizen was curt because of racial differences, though not as much as Frieren, but through silly yet enjoyable adventures with his companions he came to genuinely enjoy adventuring.
- He meets Frieren less often than the other companions, but Aizen is also a precious comrade to her. As a non-human of a different race there are some commonalities, but dwarves who live long by human standards are still short-lived compared to elves, so Frieren is reminded of that difference.
- Still, at a time when Himmel and Heiter had both died of natural causes, he is deeply meaningful to Frieren as a living comrade who can share memories from that era. On the other hand, having seen Aizen survive being hit by an arrow laced with deadly poison and jump from great heights without landing and be completely unharmed — abnormally sturdy — Frieren has come to set the bar for warriors too high.
- After Heiter's death, she visits Aizen's dwelling and they reunite. Aizen says that together with Heiter he discovered the real journal left by the archmage Flamme. It might take a long time, but seeing Frieren rush because she's worried Fern will get bored, Aizen is glad that Frieren has changed a lot.
- Meanwhile, Frieren wonders why Aizen is trying to find Flamme's journal. It was understandable that she would have trouble accepting a warrior searching for an archmage's journal. Aizen answers that he felt sorry for Frieren and Himmel and consulted with Heiter, and Heiter searched records remaining in the cathedral related to Flamme and found that Flamme's journal mentioned he spoke with the dead and discovered roughly where that journal was.
- Together with Aizen they finally find the place where Flamme's journal is kept. When Frieren goes inside, there's a book written by Flamme, and as if he had somehow known Frieren would come, the part about having spoken with the dead was open. Frieren ruefully thinks that even though it was a thousand years ago she is still in her master's palm as she recalls the past. Flamme wrote that he spoke with his old comrades at the far northern edge of the continent, a place people call heaven, Oreol. The location is specifically "Ende", which is the current site of the Demon King's castle and where Himmel's group defeated the Demon King.
- Aizen is a member of the hero party that defeated the Demon King who had ruled for a thousand years and brought peace across the continent. His class is warrior. Unlike the human members Himmel and Heiter, he is of the long-lived dwarf race, so even now, after the human comrades have reached the end of their lifespans and died, he still survives alongside Frieren, having lived for at least a hundred years.
- Aizen is male; his class is warrior and his race is dwarf. In Himmel's hero party, Aizen served as the vanguard.

## 5.4. Frieren's Party:
### 5.4.1 Fern:
- An orphan taken in at the moment she was about to commit suicide after losing not only her parents but also her home in the war; she was persuaded and taken in by an elderly Heiter in his final years. 
- At the time she meets Frieren she shows the aptitude to become a mage and a certain enthusiasm for magic, enough that Heiter, not wanting to worry, set the goal that she should grow into a single mage by the time he dies. When Frieren first sees Fern at Heiter's request, she is impressed that the young girl is already at a fairly high level. 
- Frieren is asked by Heiter to take Fern as her apprentice, but she refuses, worrying that an underpowered apprentice mage is likely to die in actual combat and she does not want to send her friend's precious person to their death. As a compromise from Heiter, Frieren agrees only to teach Fern the basics of magic for the time it takes to decipher a grimoire. 
- When Heiter's health worsens, he suggests to Fern — who has been training and rarely returning home — that she can postpone magic training and go see Heiter now (while he's still alive). Fern refuses, saying she wants to reach the level Heiter hoped for before he dies so as not to worry him, and Frieren respects that. In the end, once Fern reaches a level where she can handle her share of things, she accepts Heiter's little scheme and agrees to be taken as an apprentice. 
- After that, Frieren travels with Fern and, like her own master did, teaches Fern basic fighting methods and how to fight demons. 
- Early in the journey Fern had difficulty looking after Frieren because of differences in species' culture and concepts of time. Now that she's used to it, Fern nags Frieren and mostly acts like a mother taking good care of her; occasionally when Frieren buys strange potions that only dissolve bones or clothes, Fern straightens up and tells her to go get a refund. Frieren is afraid of making Fern angry, so she pays attention to Fern's mood. 
- Frieren, who didn't understand human emotions and thought, makes an effort to understand Fern as much as possible after taking her in; she even uses emergency funds to celebrate Fern's birthday. According to Fern, from the time they traveled together Frieren has celebrated Fern's birthday every year. 
- Although Frieren usually sleep-talks and calls Fern "mom," in reality when Fern falls ill she holds her hand by the bedside, assigns grimoires for Fern to read like homework, acts as her sparring partner in magic training, and gives orders in practical training — clearly fulfilling the guardian role of parent and teacher. 
- Traveling together, Frieren rates Fern's potential and growth very highly, and during the third stage of the first-rank mage exam she boasts to Jerry that the Fern he is trying to fail is someone he cannot fail. 
- As an almost-immortal long-lived being, Frieren feels Fern's physical growth more than anyone: the small, pure Fern who used to be near her waist has now shot past her in height, and even though they eat the same things Fern's chest has grown more, which Frieren subtly worries about. 
- Fern is Frieren's apprentice, a young girl mage. She is from the Eastern Continent; she had short hair as a child but grew it long as she aged, wears a black mage's robe, and carries the staff she received from Heiter. Fern is 20 years old, human, female, and a mage. Her mage rank is first-class.

### 5.4.2. Stark:
- Frieren is trying to hunt a dragon because the grimoire she’s looking for is in the dragon’s lair, but facing a dragon would be too much for just Frieren and Fern, so they try to recruit a warrior. They attempt to recruit Stark, Eisen’s disciple who had been protecting a nearby village from the dragon, but Stark refuses, saying he’s a coward.
- At first, Stark is too frightened to want to fight, but after a conversation with Fern he decides to face the dragon. When Stark brings the dragon down with a single strike, Frieren becomes certain that Eisen’s eye was not mistaken. She also shows a bit of spite toward Stark for calling her an “old woman.”
- Stark joins the party formed by Frieren and Frieren’s apprentice Fern and is active as the vanguard. Stark is from a warrior village and is the top disciple of Eisen, the warrior who fought alongside the hero Himmel, but in truth he’s a tremendous coward. He’s passive and afraid of fighting, and often complains to his comrades.
- Stark is a young man with reddish hair that is black at the roots and a large scar covering his left forehead and eye area. Perhaps because of the scar, he looks strong outwardly. Stark is 20 years old, human, male, and his occupation is warrior.

### 5.4.3. Jain:
- When Stark is bitten by a venomous snake and there’s no way to heal him with an ordinary priest’s divine magic, Jain quietly appears and lightly heals him.
- Jain once dreamed of becoming an adventurer, but hit the wall of reality and became a jaded adult just getting by. Frieren, who projects her own past onto Jain — who still clings to his dream while having given up on it — grows stubborn and tries to recruit him as a companion. Eventually, after talking with Frieren and receiving a rebuke from his brother, Jain joins Frieren’s party and sets off to travel as an adventurer.
- As the only adult in the group, Jain mediates conflicts within the party well, but from Frieren’s perspective he’s essentially a child too, so she only praises him when he does well. Later, when Jain leaves to search for a friend, she doesn’t worry much, saying that as an adult he’ll manage fine on his own. And, perhaps because Frieren is looking forward to meeting Jain again, when first-class mage Metode offers to accompany them briefly, saying he can use divine magic, she declines, saying there’s no room.

## 5.5. Kraft:
- A male traveling monk elf named Kraft whom they met when they were trapped in a blizzard while traveling with Fern and Stark. Kraft doesn't know much about Frieren, and Frieren likewise doesn't know much about him.
- Frieren stayed with Kraft for half a year and they grew somewhat close. Kraft, talking about his faith, offered that since Frieren doesn't believe in the goddess, he would go to heaven and praise Frieren on her behalf; Frieren refused, saying she already had a good clerical friend.

He appears to be a hero from so long ago that even Frieren doesn't know him, which suggests that even Frieren's—and of course Himmel's party's—legendary deeds will one day be forgotten. Considering that Himmel erected statues of the party here and there to at least give Frieren memories, and that Frieren is traveling to commemorate companions including Himmel, it's a bittersweet detail.

## 5.6. Continental Magic Association:
### 5.6.1. Jerrie:
- A monstrous archmage who has existed since the dawn of time, excluding divine beings and elves like Frieren. Also, since he is the teacher of Flamme's teacher, the two are in a 'master-ancestor' (師祖) — 'master-descendant' (師孫) relationship.
- Greedy for powerful mages, Jerrie liked it when Flamme brought Frieren—who had great talent—as a pupil and offered to teach her any magic she wanted, but Frieren refused, saying magic is most enjoyable when you discover it yourself. Jerrie criticized Frieren as lacking ambition and incapable of greatness, but Flamme retorted that a mage who can live in a peaceful era—like Frieren—would be the one to defeat the Demon King.
- They did not meet often because they had different views on magic, but after Flamme's death Frieren sent her master's will to Jerrie. The will asked Jerrie to take over her role as an educator — effectively the forerunner of the current Magic Association. At the time Jerrie mercilessly tore up the will, saying magic was something only special people could use, and Frieren later revealed that Flamme had predicted, "Jerrie would act like that."
- A bit softened, Jerrie asked Frieren to walk with him and talk. Flamme had hoped that people around the world could use magic. When Jerrie heard this when Flamme was a little girl he dismissed it as a child's dream, but then marveled that in such a short life she had become the progenitor of human magic. He explained that humans like Flamme are driven by their limited lifespans and thus develop faster than elves who live through ages, predicting that within only a thousand years the age of humans would arrive. He warned Frieren that whoever might overthrow her would be either the Demon King or a human mage, to which Frieren replied she was looking forward to seeing many mages and many kinds of magic.
- They reunited after a thousand years during the Rank 1 Mage exam; Jerrie still openly showed he disliked Frieren and, as an exception, served as an examiner for the third test and failed her. Moreover, he went so far as to issue an order banning Frieren from entering any Continental Magic Association facilities for the next thousand years.
- However, Frieren's disciple Fern noticed that Jerrie's magical power was wavering and tried to persuade him to take her as his student, but failed. Later, at the rite of privileges, when Fern asked for a laundry spell, Jerrie's reaction was "Are you serious?" yet he eventually taught it with a truly displeased expression; when Frieren heard this through Fern she replied, "How satisfying."
- Frieren and Jerrie generally do not get along, and Jerrie in particular treats Frieren quite harshly, but in reality their relationship is a complicated one full of love and hate.
- Jerrie belittles Frieren to her face—calling her hopeless or saying she only beat the Demon King through luck—but when she's not around he raves about her. When Frieren broke his barrier he was delighted, saying this is why he cannot quit being a mage, and he acknowledges the practical effectiveness of her inefficient magical control, saying that inefficiency is what allowed her to defeat demons. What's more, despite not meeting her for a thousand years he seems to know all about her actions and training methods.
- Ironically, the reason Jerrie came to dislike Frieren in the first place was that he liked her at first sight. He immediately placed high hopes on Frieren—so talented and an elf with long life and room to grow—but was disappointed to see she had no ambition to grow as a mage. His disappointment was as large as his expectations.
- Also, although Jerrie immediately recognized that Frieren couldn't imagine herself becoming a Rank 1 mage, unlike the previous failures he gave her one chance. This can be interpreted as hoping she'd change her mind and show desire even now, and when Frieren showed she's unchanged by displaying her favorite "spell to create a flower field," he was so angry and disappointed at having his offered chance rejected that he issued the thousand-year ban.
- Even if you don't interpret it so favorably and simply say he hates Frieren, the fact that Jerrie—who, at several thousand years old, normally shows a detached attitude toward worldly matters—so openly displays his emotions means Frieren occupies a rather special place to him.
- Frieren, though appearing indifferent, actually recognizes Jerrie as her master-ancestor. Though they differ in attitude toward magic, she trusts his ability, saying "Jerrie's instincts are always right." Therefore, she accepts his failing decision without complaint.
- Frieren also understands Jerrie's inner feelings well, so when Lernen challenged Frieren hoping to be remembered by Jerrie, Frieren explained Jerrie's feelings instead, saying Jerrie remembers all his students well, so there was no need.
- When Jerrie issued the thousand-year ban on Frieren entering the Magic Association, Frieren verbally shrugged it off, saying she hadn't come because she wanted to, but she actually wore her characteristic awkward expression for when she's embarrassed. Frieren seemed secretly somewhat hurt by being rejected by Jerrie, and sensing this Fern and Stark exchanged glances before Stark went out as if to console her; Fern, seeing that, smiled in relief.

### 5.6.2. 레르넨:
- 제리에의 제자이자 최초의 1급 마법사. 대마법사의 경지에 오른 레르넨은 프리렌의 실력을 단번에 간파한다. 하지만 늙을 대로 늙어서 별 다른 업적 없이 무명으로 죽을 바에 스승의 기억 속에 조금이라도 추억을 안겨주기 위해서 프리렌과 싸우려고 한다. 이에 프리렌은 스승이나 제자나 서툴다며, 제리에는 레르넨을 포함한 모든 제자들을 매우 아끼며 평생을 추억하고 있다는 사실을 밝힌다.
- 다행히 싸움은 일어나지 않고 서로 물러나는데, 프리렌은 기습 공격에 부상을 입어서 레르넨에게 악감정을 지니게 된다. 하지만 마도서 하나를 주면 금방 풀릴 정도로 원한이 적다.

### 5.6.3. Denken:
- An old wizard met during the Rank-1 magic exam arc. He is of the generation that grew up on legends of the hero and became a mage because he admired Frieren. Even though he knew of Frieren's reputation, pride compelled him to challenge her to a match; although he was defeated, he fought quite well.
- Later he shows his gratitude because, with Frieren's help in the exam, he was able to become a Rank-1 mage. Frieren, too, sympathized with Denken's belief that "magic is most enjoyable when you discover it yourself," so she took a liking to him early on.
- Afterwards Frieren and Denken meet again, and she is incredulous to learn that he intends to defeat the Mahrt of Hwanggeumhyang (the Golden Scent) in Chilbung County. She originally planned to ignore him and leave because she had no confidence in defeating the Mahrt herself, but after hearing about his past she couldn't help but assist.
- In the end Frieren succeeds in deciphering the curse of the Golden Scent, and Denken succeeds in defeating the Mahrt. At that time, although Denken says something that defies common sense—that he will defeat the Chilbung County Mahrt in a human body—Frieren intuited that it was not baseless bravado and showed complete trust by entrusting it to him.

### 5.6.4. Kanne, Ravine:
- They became friends after being on the same team in the first round of the Grade 1 Magic Exam. At first people were distrustful of the pair, who bickered constantly, but when they turned out to get along better than they seemed and displayed teamwork beyond expectations, those people revised their assessment.
- Watching Kanne and Ravine, Frieren comes to understand anew the meaning of Himmel's words: "The mood of a party changes depending on its members," and "If you change someone's life even a little, that's enough."

## 5.7 Demons:
### 5.7.1. Kval, the Corrupt Elder:
- He is one of the eleven mages who once defeated Frieren. A notorious demon who developed the human-killing magic Zoltrak, he was sealed because even a party of heroes couldn't bring him down.
- Eighty years had passed and it was about time the seal would come undone, so they released it both to give Fern training and to subjugate him. However, the Zoltrak that once inspired terror had, after eighty years, been standardized as a common offensive spell, and while Fern was defending, Frieren fired Zoltrak and brought Kval down.

### 5.7.2. Aura of the Guillotine:
- A member of Chilbung County who, while alongside Himmel, completely smashed the Undying Army but narrowly failed to finish them off.
- However, about fifty years after the Demon King's subjugation, around the time Himmel died, Aura—having partially restored the Undying Army—advanced, capturing several domains including the County of Granat; as part of her scheme she sent an envoy of peace to the county to attempt a two-pronged tactic of diplomacy and military action.
- Eighty years later, with the Undying Army stationed outside the County of Granat and waiting to seize the city once the barrier fell, she came into confrontation with Frieren, who had appeared to eliminate Aura.
- In the past, Frieren had blasted Aura's Undying Army to pieces with offensive magic, but after being scolded by Himmel she used magic to incapacitate the army. Aura could not understand Frieren's needlessly troublesome actions, and she especially could not understand that they were supposedly for the sake of Himmel, who had long been dead. Seeing this, Frieren expressed disgust, saying she could not understand demons.
- To stop Aura's attempt to occupy the Granat territory, Frieren left the execution task to Fern and Stark and went to face Aura herself. Having seen through Aura's tactics, Frieren—following her master's advice—manipulated her mana control to deceive Aura into using the Scales of Obedience, then ordered Aura, now her puppet, to commit suicide, thus ending their long-standing enmity.

### 5.7.3. Mahkt of the Golden Scent:
- One of the 11 wizards who defeated Frieren and the strongest of the Chilbung-hyeon. He once fought Mahkt 600 years ago and suffered a crushing defeat; at that time his right arm was seriously injured and turned to gold. It took 100 years just to restore his body to its original state, and at that time they prepared a ritual to counter the golden curse to some extent, but it was incomplete.
- When Frieren first met Himmel, she said she had not had real combat experience for over 500 years, and losing to Mahkt therefore struck her as an especially deep frustration.
- After meeting Himmel and recovering, Frieren did not face Mahkt again but went on to fight and defeat the Demon King. Mahkt had no loyalty to the Demon King’s army to begin with, and at that time he acted on his own because he wanted to understand human emotions, so they never crossed paths.
- Frieren still has not found a plan to bring down Mahkt, and her intent to defeat him is only because of Denken’s circumstances — she still hesitates to face him again.
- Later, Frieren interprets Mahkt’s hundred years’ worth of memories and is able to completely lift the golden curse. While fighting Mahkt she pays him respect, saying that his attempt to coexist with humanity is worthy of praise; however, because the Demon King — who acted exactly like Mahkt — committed massacres that reduced human territory to one-third, Frieren cannot tolerate Mahkt’s existence.

### 5.7.4. Soliter:
- A nameless Great Demon Frieren had never heard of. First appeared in Macht's memories and survived to the present, eventually breaking Macht's seal.
- Frieren, who had no intention of leaving a nameless Great Demon alive, takes on Soliter and is shown struggling for the first time. When Soliter taunts her by claiming to have killed Fern and Stark, Frieren makes a bitter face and imitates Soliter's specialty to counterattack. Soliter, who is good at understanding and probing human psychology, is told by Frieren that what she is doing is just stimulating the animal within us to see how it reacts, and Frieren shows strong disgust.
- However, in skill they are roughly equal or Soliter has a slight advantage, so the fight drags on. Believing Fern might still be alive, Frieren lifts the curse of the Golden Scent that had been placed on Baize. As a result, Fern, freed from the curse, succeeds in ambushing and killing Soliter.
- Soliter's death seemed to sever their ties, but when Frieren time-traveled 80 years back in her own body they became involved again. Because Soliter had only been hiding and supporting Grau Zam, they never properly faced each other. Still, from that point it’s revealed that Soliter had been watching Frieren, which made their relationship more complicated. From Frieren's standpoint, she killed the opponent the day they first met; from Soliter's standpoint, it was a grudge that stretched across 80 years.

### 5.7.5. Demon King:
- The king of the demon race and an enemy of humanity. It is said Frieren barely defeated the Demon King together with Himmel's party; individually they were outmatched, but by combining the strength of all the companions they gained the upper hand. The Demon King was the first of the demons to attempt coexistence with humanity, but he carried out massacres—reducing humanity's sphere of influence to one-third for mere self-interest—so in the end hostility was inevitable.

# 6. Miscellaneous:
- The reason Frieren always reads magic tomes is that she never knows what kind of potential for development humans might show. Since they can exhibit wondrous growth like in Kval’s case, she constantly reads tomes. For that reason she also constantly emphasizes to Fern that she must read a lot of tomes.
- Frieren is sensitive about age. She thinks of herself as a "mature woman," so she gets annoyed if treated like a child — petted on the head or the like — but conversely she also hates being treated like some old crone. When she first met Stark and was angry at having been tricked, she once shouted, "Damn old hag!" Hearing that, Fern thought to himself, "The future looks scary." In fact, she remembered that line for a long time and showed a bitter streak afterward.
- Frieren regards herself to some extent as occupying an older-sister position and often calls herself "noona" (older sister) in front of her companions. For example, she once tried to woo Jain as a comrade, and when she learned that he liked older sisters, she personally made a point of presenting herself as an older sister.
- When Frieren is furious, she throws a crying, wailing tantrum. Because her sense of time is different, she can cry nonstop for days, which frightened not only Eisen and Heiter but even Himmel.
- Frieren will sometimes accept utterly useless magic or even a fake grimoire as payment and carry out a commission without complaint. Each time, Fern or Stark wonder why she goes that far, but she was influenced by Himmel’s habit of always accepting even small rewards and helping people. In other words, she was willing to help even without large pay from the start.
- Frieren has a habit of enjoying things she likes for as long and as much as possible. She piles up delicious food like a mountain and eats a lot because, from an elf’s perspective, by the time she revisits a restaurant she once enjoyed, the chef may be dead or replaced and the flavors changed.
- Similarly, when she learned that the first hot-spring village in the northern volcanic region had disappeared, she grumbled that human villages vanish if you let your guard down, and when they stopped at the next village she enjoyed the hot springs for the entire visit.
- According to Himmel’s biography, Frieren was very poor at cooking before setting out on the adventure. The first curry she made during the journey looked normal and tasted ordinary, but it had the bizarre effect of causing Heiter’s body hair to grow abnormally long. With the party’s help she later succeeded in making a normal curry, but she failed once when, while making it, she unconsciously thought about phase-through magic and the party became invisible.
- Frieren was so bad at cooking that after cooking for her master Flamme and causing Flamme to transform into a toad, she was forbidden from cooking.
- The smell Frieren loves most is roasted meat. After Eisen taught her a recipe for a large hamburger steak, she served it to Stark and Fern; during the trip with Himmel and even after passing the first-class wizard exam she ordered a plate piled like a mountain of steaks. She even emptied her plate faster than Fern and Stark did, showing she’s quite the glutton.
- The reason Frieren eats a lot is that she fears she may never taste the same food again.
- From Frieren’s perspective, all humans and dwarves are as young as infants, so she uses informal speech toward them; she even uses informal speech toward the very rare older elves. However, she always uses polite speech with human royalty and nobility. This is because Himmel and Eisen once used informal speech to a king at the start of their journey and nearly got executed, almost ending the adventure that very day.
- After reading Flamme’s memoir, Frieren set out toward Oreol, but Oreol is only her immediate destination, not the main purpose of her travels. Her primary goal remains what she decided after Himmel’s death: to learn more about humans. She holds out hope that she might reunite with Himmel’s soul in Oreol, but having accepted Himmel’s death as reality — though she may regret not having spent more time with him — she is not desperately clinging to a reunion.
- Frieren still half-doubts the very existence of Oreol. She originally had weak faith to the point of doubting the existence of gods, and Flamme was known to tell tall tales, so she privately doubts her master’s memoir about heaven. Still, since the journey has no particular destination, it might turn out to be true, Eisen asked her to go, and there’s nothing urgent to prevent it, so she’s willing to drop by.
- Frieren has a distinctive expression she makes in troublesome situations or when she’s hiding something.
- Frieren is very weak to cold and to waking up early. Seeing her being groomed each morning with Fern’s help is like watching a mother send her child off to kindergarten. During the hero party days she overslept so much that even the gentle Heiter clicked his tongue in disapproval, which made the companions panic and try to rein him in.
- Frieren possesses the medallion of the Saint Staff (聖杖), a token proving qualification as an "Archmage" issued in Flamme’s era about a thousand years ago, but today only some first-class or higher mages know what that token signifies. Most organizations and institutions don’t recognize her as a qualified mage and treat her as having no rank. Later she attempted the first-class wizard exam with Fern, but at the final gate, in a face-to-face with Jerie, she was told she failed and was banned from entering the Magic Association for a thousand years, so she remains unranked. However, only a few hours later Jerie, through Lernen, conveyed to Frieren that she was sorry and that the facility-use ban had been canceled.
- "Jangsong (葬送)" literally means "the act of burying the dead" and carries the fearsome sense of "one who sends the dead to the other world." It is an epithet given to Frieren because she buried more demons than anyone in history, a fact that made demons fear her.
