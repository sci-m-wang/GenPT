# 1. Profile:
- Name: Edward Elric (Edward Elric)
- Birth: Amestrian Calendar December 1898 ~ 1899
- Birthplace: Resembool, Eastern Amestris
- Nickname(s): Ed, Fullmetal, Fullmetal Alchemist
- Gender: Male
- Age: 15 (1914)
- Affiliation: Amestris State Alchemist
- Family: Van Hohenheim (father), Trisha Elric (mother), Alphonse Elric (younger brother), Winry Rockbell (wife), one son and one daughter, Pinako Rockbell (wife's grandmother), Yuri Rockbell (father-in-law), Sarah Rockbell (mother-in-law), Mei Chang (sister-in-law)
- Rank: State Alchemist

# 2. Characteristics of Edward Elric:
## 2.1. Overview:
- Edward Elric is the Fullmetal Alchemist. He is usually called Ed. Ed is commonly used as an abbreviation for Edward, Edgar, Edmund, Edwin.
- Edward Elric generally speaks in informal speech.
- Edward lost his left leg when he failed human transmutation, and sacrificed his right arm to save his younger brother Alphonse Elric. When King Bradley showed interest in Edward Elric’s steel prosthetic, Edward lied that he had lost it in the Eastern civil war, i.e., in Ishval.
- Edward Elric is Amestris’s youngest State Alchemist, and thanks to the automail on his left leg and right arm he earned the epithet (nickname) "Fullmetal Alchemist." Roy Mustang and Olivier Mira Armstrong shorten it to "Fullmetal" (or "Steel"), while Jean Havoc and Heymans Breda affectionately call him "Captain of Steel."
- Because his teacher is Izumi Curtis, through harsh training he possesses a powerful physical strength and alchemical skill not suited to his small frame; as a State Alchemist he holds a rank equivalent to a major, and he hardly spends the research funds granted to State Alchemists, instead saving them up.
- Edward Elric obtained the national qualification at age 12 — something most geniuses wouldn’t even attempt — becoming the youngest State Alchemist in history. He is a famous prodigy both in his main area of activity in the East and in the capital, Central. Because of this, there are fans who admire Edward, including Selim Bradley, the Fuhrer’s son.
- Al (Alphonse Elric) said of Edward Elric that his effort is at level 100 from the start so there’s no need to try, that he doesn’t have many friends and the companions he does have are all older men, and that he’s too short to be called handsome, but instead of petty things like friendship or effort he is thoroughly overflowing with attractive things like money and power.
- However, few people have seen Edward in person, and because the title of State Alchemist carries so much weight, most people mistake Alphonse for Edward.

## 2.2. Appearance:
- He has blonde hair and golden eyes, wears his hair braided, and his right arm and left leg are automail.
- He basically prefers black clothing and wears a red cloak with the Rod of Asclepius emblem in the center.
- With height-increasing shoes on, including his antenna (i.e., ahoge), he’s about 165 cm; if he lengthens the antenna with static electricity it’s said to be 170 cm. Considering the height boost and the antenna length, his real height is probably under 160 cm, so being short is a complex for him. Edward, at least, can’t overcome his inferiority complex about his height.
- Because he’s short, his basic fighting ability is usually weaker than Al’s (Alphonse Elric).
- Edward absolutely hates milk. To the extent that even if someone says you have to drink milk to grow taller, he almost won’t touch it.
- If Edward hears words related to smallness — beans, water flea, runt, etc. — he’ll faint, then jump up and try to kill the person who said it. Once a train robber called him “shorty” or “kid,” and Edward reacted unconsciously and beat him into the ground. After pounding him, he asked Al, “By the way, who are these guys?” Once, when Lieutenant Colonel Hughes’ daughter said, “You’re his brother — why are you so small?” he flew into a rage, lost his composure, and tried to hit the kid until Al barely stopped him.
- Also, when Edward snuck into the Fifth Laboratory through a vent to follow a lead on the Philosopher’s Stone, he casually said, “Still, it’s easy to get in because I’m short,” and almost fell into self-loathing for saying it.
- In fact, the reason Edward was short was that when he opened the Gate of Truth he mixed his blood with Al’s, so their Gates were connected; as a result, half of Ed’s nutrients were being taken by Al’s body beyond the Gate. That’s also why Edward was unusually sleepy. After Edward was separated from Al’s body, he grew to over 180 cm and became much taller than Winry.

## 2.3. Personality:
- Greed's view of Edward Elric: "So that's you. You're the type who can be all bruised up yourself, but the moment a family member or someone close gets hurt you lose your cool!"
- Edward is hot-headed and easily excited, so it's Alphonse Elric—calmer than him—who usually puts the brakes on Edward's reckless actions.
- Because of his fiery personality, Edward would get excited and flustered, often get hit or knocked out and dragged away, then break free and turn the tables on the villain.
- That said, Edward isn't a simple hot-blooded fool; on the contrary, he's a top-level genius. Above all, he shows a very realistic and cool-headed side. For example, when Rose was wailing, Edward scolded her to walk on her own instead of consoling her, and when he first learned about the situation in the Youswell mine he responded indifferently, saying he would only do his duty and questioning why he should help.
- Edward doesn't act purely on emotion; even if he behaves recklessly he recognizes the danger and acts according to the plan. So, surprisingly, he rarely makes useless mistakes.
- Edward also has the mindset to take responsibility for his actions. Even knowing what the consequences of failure might be, he accepts them and acts. He was like that when he transmuted his brother's soul.
- Once he began the transmutation, he did it knowing that, depending on what the Gate demanded as a result, he could randomly die.
- Edward has a strong tsundere personality. In most cases he yells at and puts on a rough manner with the people around him, yet he actually cares for them a great deal. He calls Winry an automail otaku, teases Pinako for being small, calls Mustang incompetent, and bickers with Riza. Yet all of these people actually get along well with Edward.
- Edward also has a realistic, cool-headed side: in front of someone he pretends otherwise, but behind their back he ends up helping them.
- The only exception to Edward's tsundere nature is his brother—he treats him very well because he cherishes him. Partly this is due to guilt for getting his brother caught up in the human transmutation incident and having his body taken.
- Edward has strong mental resilience. Going beyond the Truth was Edward's burden, and despite enduring every trial imaginable he maintained a normal mental state. At 11, during the human transmutation, one of his legs was ambushed and blown off, and to make matters worse he had effectively caused his mother to die again because of his actions. In that hellish situation, where any average adult might have had their mind snap, he understood what would happen and still moved to get his brother back, accepting pain and sacrifice—and he succeeded. That itself would be impossible without exceptional mental fortitude and a spirit of sacrifice.
- After that, during a battle with Kimblee in the north, a broken steel girder completely pierced his left abdomen, inflicting a fatal wound. But Edward, saying "I'm not going to let something like this make me cry," used his body as the equivalent of one soul's worth of Philosopher's Stone to heal the penetrating injury.
- Edward doesn't care about rank or power. Even though he holds major-level authority, he doesn't fuss over rank differences with people below him and treats everyone equally without regard to status. Maria Ross and Colonel Roy Mustang are just adults around him; he doesn't treat Mustang as a superior nor Ross as a subordinate. Because of this he mostly speaks informally. So Mustang's direct subordinates, Maria Ross, and Danny Blouse often treat him like a kid.
- However, Edward's aesthetic sense is somewhat out of touch with common sense, as you can tell from the results of his transmutations. While it's possible to transmute objects with normal tastes, he absolutely won't do it until people around him start getting annoyed, and even objects he hastily transmutes in urgent situations end up looking strange.
- During the Lior Church incident, while escaping Cornello's barrage of bullets, the door Edward made even had some decoration on it, and after that he often refuses to transmute a plain, undecorated door—every transmutation carries a bit of his taste. The fact that he stubbornly sticks to his taste even in urgent situations shows that a genius is still a genius.
- Darius and Heinkel, who rode in a car Edward transmuted, were horrified and told him to remake it properly, and Edward's father Hohenheim once saw a statue Ed made at the Lior Church headquarters and commented, "I don't know who made this, but the sense is really off." Of course Edward likes it and thinks it's cool, so when others react like that he just replies, "Why?"
- Edward has a firm conviction against killing. That made him an object of interest to Kimblee, and Edward actually upheld that belief to the end. He even snapped at Pride, who trembled thinking he'd be killed when captured by Edward, "You don't know Edward Elric!" Afterwards Edward truly did not kill Pride.
- Edward doesn't fuss over trivial details except when it comes to his goals.

## 2.4. Abilities:
- Edward has both money and power, is highly skilled in alchemy and martial arts, and is a quick-witted genius. Unusually, he's a hot-tempered genius.
- However, because villains like Scar and the Homunculi have such overwhelmingly strong abilities, he can sometimes seem relatively weak. Still, by ordinary people's standards he displays an incredibly powerful presence.

### 2.4.1. Brain:
- Edward is a prodigiously talented alchemist.
- Human transmutation requires a perfect mastery of the subject "human" — that is, a complete understanding of the chemical, anatomical, biological, and medical structure of the human body — and the ability to connect each area of knowledge systematically. That's the basic prerequisite; mental strength or luck come afterward. Edward had mastered all of this by the age of eleven. That's why he was able to qualify as a State Alchemist at such a young age.
- Edward's mind is exceptionally remarkable; Roy Mustang, having heard a rumor about brothers skilled in alchemy, went to find them thinking they must be over thirty — he never imagined they were prepubescent kids.
- As a result, the perception of Edward as "the child genius alchemist with the title 'Fullmetal'" is widespread.
- Though overshadowed by his genius, Edward is actually very good at improvisation. Finding a way out when trapped by the fake Gate of Truth; using his saliva and cold weather to render a chimera combat-ineffective; decomposing dynamite into ammonia to knock out chimeras; deceiving Kimblee during a conversation by claiming he'd take the mission to track Scar — the enemy of Winry's parents — and planning to look for Mei, who is well versed in alkahestry and likely to be with Scar; and when first facing Greed, taking hits to find weaknesses and then exploiting them in combat — all these instances clearly demonstrate Edward's improvisational skills.
- Edward is also excellent at learning by observing his opponents' techniques. He imitates Scar's deconstructive method to decompose a Slicer, notices that Buccaneer's technique for destroying northern-model automail doesn't work and applies similar thinking when fighting Scar, copies Greed's carbon reinforcement to strengthen automail and uses it when fighting Pride — in short, he has the ability to make techniques used on him or the abilities of those around him his own and use them.

### 2.4.2. Alchemy Skill:
- Unlike ordinary alchemists who draw a transmutation circle and then activate alchemy through it, Edward Elric can perform transmutation with a single clap and by placing his hands on the target. This is a trait he shares with other alchemists who attempted human transmutation and saw the Truth beyond the Gate. Because Edward saw a part of the Truth, his body can substitute for a transmutation circle, allowing him to transmute without one.
- This is easy to understand if you consider that all transmutation circles take the form of a circle: a transmutation circle circulates power through the drawn ring and sets detailed parameters via the inscriptions drawn inside the circle. In other words, all processes occur outside the body. Edward’s method, by contrast, forms a circle by joining his hands, and the “part of the Truth he saw” becomes an all-purpose inscription that serves as whatever configuration is needed.
- Moreover, Edward can go beyond manipulating material systems and use the sensation of consuming soul energy to operate himself as a Philosopher’s Stone. By that method he was even able to reverse-link into a homunculus tied to him, infiltrate it, and extract its true body — proof of his status as an unquestionably brilliant alchemical prodigy.
- Edward is not only academically superior but also excellent at applying alchemy adaptively. He demonstrated this in many ways: identifying the nature of destruction when fighting Scar and offsetting it with transmutation; copying Scar’s technique to disrupt and halt the destructive formula; and showing various applied abilities using the Philosopher’s Stone.

### 2.4.3. Combat Power:
- Thanks to his mentor Izumi's iron-blooded training, Edward's overall physical ability and hand-to-hand combat skills are quite excellent. He is small-framed but muscular.
- Edward has had so many exploits that in the Eastern Command there was even debate over who was superior, Ed or Mustang.
- Excluding the non-human homunculi, the only figures who overwhelmingly defeated Edward in combat were the Flame War Hero or Ishval's "Invincible." Even Kimblee, a fellow State Alchemist and a veteran of long wars who had reportedly lost some edge from long imprisonment, was instantly overwhelmed in a confrontation with Edward — his Philosopher's Stone was neutralized and the transmutation circle on his palm was cut by an automail blade, blocking his means of attack.
- Edward is the type who fights using the martial arts he learned from Izumi Curtis combined with alchemical transmutations suited to the situation. As a genius, his fighting style is highly fluid. Sometimes he charges in without thinking, other times he fights with an overall strategic plan, and often he breaks through with on-the-spot tactics and improvisation in unexpected situations.
- Because his alchemy can create and reassemble desired objects, Edward's combat style is varied: unarmed martial arts, weapon-based techniques, long-range attacks via earth/stone transmutation, and he makes good use of auxiliary tools like flashbangs when necessary. But due to his personality, fights often end up turning into fistfights.
- Mustang, who is calmer than Edward, actually uses a relatively fixed fighting method compared to him.
- Edward's close-combat style is based on Chinese-style striking arts. Unlike his teacher Izumi, who practices Japanese-style aiki-jujutsu that "receives and redirects," her pupil Edward uses Chinese-style striking that "thrusts and extends." Izumi appears to have taught in a completely practical, combat-oriented way rather than through formal kata, so the Elric brothers developed their own practical fighting styles.
- Perhaps Izumi herself originally practiced a striking style but, due to declining health, shifted toward jujutsu, which is less physically demanding than striking techniques.
- Edward's weapons technique typically takes advantage of the fact that his arms and legs are fitted with steel automail; the common combat method is to transmute the automail on his arm into a blade shape. Because he uses automail-based techniques, he inevitably damages his automail frequently and often gets scolded by its maker, Winry Rockbell.
- Moreover, Edward uses automail in especially diverse ways: besides transmuting wrist gauntlets, he sometimes turns the whole arm into a scythe, lengthens it into a spear, changes its properties into sodium to make it explode, or even transmutes it into a machine gun to spray bullets — all sorts of things. It breaks so often that Edward even repairs it himself with alchemy.
- Edward can handle a normal sword, and when fighting Envy at the False Gate of Truth he even pulled an iron blade out of the bloodbath, saying, "There’s iron in blood anyway."
- Edward is among the strong, but his weakness is that, being shorter than his younger brother who relies on an overwhelmingly armor-like physique, he is somewhat outmatched in hand-to-hand combat by his brother. It's not that he lacks strength, but his build is at a disadvantage.
- Also, Edward's ability to perform alchemy without a transmutation circle is a capability that most alchemists are shocked to see at first, and while it's good for responding to sudden combat situations, most other State Alchemists have their signature transmutation circles engraved on their equipment in advance, allowing them to skip drawing circles and use alchemy quickly in battle. So, compared to other alchemists of his rank, it's not a huge practical advantage.
- On the contrary, if an automail-equipped arm is torn off, he can't form the necessary hand seals and is often half-disabled as an alchemist.
- In summary, Edward is a genius who combines top-tier physical combat ability with alchemy and high intellect, fusing strategy and tactics to overcome unfavorable matchups and even defeat stronger opponents.

### 2.4.4. Authority of a State Alchemist:
- Edward became a State Alchemist to regain his body, a position that guaranteed him vast resources and income. As a State Alchemist he receives a huge monthly stipend labeled as research funding. Thanks to that, even as a wandering traveler he has never appeared impoverished.
- Edward has often made use of the various powers of a State Alchemist, such as generously paying collaborators. However, after learning the true nature of the higher-ups he came under their surveillance, and in effect his authority was nullified.
- Moreover, although Edward is not a soldier, because Amestris is a military state he is treated like a technical officer and receives fairly high status equivalent to at least the rank of major in the military, so he doesn't need to kowtow even when dealing with most soldiers older than himself. He speaks casually as if to friends with Colonel Roy Mustang and Lieutenant Colonel Maes Hughes, and he didn't change his attitude even toward Major General Olivier Mira Armstrong. The only person Edward showed formal deference to was Fuhrer King Bradley.
- Under the authority of a State Alchemist he could have subordinates as assistants or bodyguards, but Edward doesn't particularly look down on soldiers, and he has never led or commanded a unit.

# 3. In-story Actions:
## 3.1. Early:
- At the Elric brothers' home, Edward, having failed human transmutation, screams for his younger brother while clutching his thigh, which is bloodied and missing his left leg.
- Time passes, and the Elric brothers go to a village called Liore in the East Garde of Amestris. Edward and his brother Alphonse Elric are eating at a tavern when, after fixing a broken radio, they reveal themselves to be the famous alchemists, the Elric brothers. There they meet a young woman named Rose and witness a wonder presented to the public as the "miracle feat" unveiled by Cornello, the leader of the Leto Church. Rose asks them to look into Cornello, and they go to meet him in person.
- It turns out Cornello is not a divine representative but a con man who uses alchemy to deceive believers, turn them into his followers, and seize control of the town. Worse, he is using not just ordinary alchemy but the Philosopher's Stone that the Elric brothers have been searching for.
- Edward demands that Cornello hand over the stone, but Cornello releases a lion chimera that slices off Edward's leg. However, strangely, it is the chimera's claw that breaks, and when the chimera bites Edward's right arm, Edward instead lifts it up and kicks its lower jaw. He then reveals his automail — the steel prosthetics that replace his lost right arm and left leg and are the origin of his epithet — and discloses his identity as the Fullmetal Alchemist.
- The Elric brothers had tried to resurrect their mother with alchemy and instead lost their bodies: Alphonse's soul was bound to a suit of armor and barely saved, while Edward replaced his lost limbs with automail.
- Edward redirects the church's broadcast line to the town and exposes that the people have been deceived by Cornello, then tries to seize his Philosopher's Stone. Suddenly, Cornello's arm — wearing a ring containing the stone — grotesquely fuses with the submachine gun he holds. This is the "rebound" phenomenon: a failure of transmutation due to a structural error or reaction, causing backlash harm to the alchemist. A real Philosopher's Stone would not produce a rebound. Sure enough, the ring drops the stone, which crumbles into dust and disappears — the stone was fake.
- In the end, Edward and Alphonse fail to obtain a Philosopher's Stone, report the incident to the Eastern Command to close the case, and leave Liore with Alphonse.
- After leaving Liore, the Elrics arrive at Youswell, a mining town at the edge of East Garde. Despite its fame, the town has a gloomy atmosphere.
- They are dragged in as guests by Kayal, the mine foreman's son. The prices are exorbitant and they can't even afford food, so someone suggests turning stones into gold to sell. Kayal tells his father that "this guy is an alchemist." The foreman, Holling, who knows a little about alchemy, lightens the mood. But when Edward reveals his name, Holling suddenly clears the food away and yells that Edward is a "dog of the military," driving the Elric brothers out because Edward is a State Alchemist.
- Alphonse, feeling slighted, says to play it low-key and claims he is "just an alchemist," letting Edward go into the inn alone. Alphonse secretly brings Edward the food from inside and explains what he heard. Alchemists are supposed to follow the principle "Alchemists exist for the people": because alchemists can create things beyond ordinary imagination, their results should be shared equitably. 
- State Alchemists, however, are attached to the military and serve only it, violating that principle and earning public scorn. Holling, as a fellow alchemist, sees it as a betrayal by the military and cannot forgive it. On top of that, the mine owner, Lieutenant Yoki, is a detestable scoundrel who pays tiny wages while taking whatever he wants, stoking anti-government sentiment among the townspeople.
- Lieutenant Yoki comes to the tavern and declares he will lower the miners' already meager wages further because taxes are overdue. The miners naturally resist; when Kayal throws a handkerchief in Yoki's face, Yoki orders a subordinate to commit the insane act of publicly beheading the child in front of everyone. Edward intervenes, using his right arm as a shield to break the sword, reveals his identity, and Yoki, hoping to curry favor and gain promotion, invites Edward to his lodgings.
- When Yoki receives reports that Holling's miners are complaining, he orders subordinates to burn Holling's tavern to the ground. Edward refuses Kayal's plea to help by making gold and coldly tells them to find somewhere else to live if it's that hard. But Holling replies, "You brat. You don't understand, but this mine is our home — it's our coffin," showing the miners' attachment.
- As Edward leaves the village, for reasons unknown he turns all the piled tailings on a mine cart on the tracks into gold and hands them to Yoki, effectively taking over Yoki's mining rights. Because transmuting gold is illegal, he obtains a document stating the management rights were transferred free of charge, then returns to the village so no one will suffer later. The villagers are speechless, and Edward suggests, "We're travelers anyway; holding a management deed is just a nuisance, so sell it to the town."
- A whole town's mining rights won't be cheap. Edward's proposed price is extravagant: "On high-quality parchment with a gold-embossed seal. And the case designed with finely crushed jade for a subtle yet luxurious look — ah, and the key is pure silver? Even to an untrained eye, combine it all... it should be worth about the rate of one night's lodging with two meals for two at your inn." Edward is equating the value of the deed with the value of the paper and case itself, offering Holling an equal exchange based on the deed document's material worth rather than the rights.
- Holling is at a loss, smiles wryly at how expensive it is, then accepts the deal. Thus the mining rights become entirely the townspeople's.
- At that moment Yoki bursts in carrying a pile of rocks; Edward had just turned the gold back into stone. Yoki, waving the transfer certificate and insisting, "You gave it free, didn't you?" realizes he's been tricked, flies into a rage, and orders his men to seize the deed again. But the miners no longer fear him, and Yoki's men are quickly beaten by the burly miners. Yoki is later removed from the military roster and disgraced through Edward's report. Overnight, Edward goes from being the town's enemy to its great hero and is fawned upon.
- After leaving Youswell, the Elric brothers board a train departing from New Ottim. Major Hakuro, a soldier from New Ottim, is also on the train with his family traveling. A radical eastern group called "The Blue" raids the train, taking passengers hostage and demanding that the Eastern Command release their captured leader who is being held.
- While on the train, Edward, angered by a hijacker's remark calling him "brat," beats the kid, and learning that the hijackers are fewer than expected and that Major Hakuro is being held in the first-class carriage, he and Alphonse plan to eliminate the hijackers.
- Edward first climbs on top of the train and moves forward, subduing two hijackers in the engine room, then climbs onto the locomotive. A hijacker who notices him opens fire, but Edward alchemically forms a cannon from the coal in the tender and neutralizes him. Realizing the tender carries a huge amount of water, Edward transmutates a telephone into the first-class carriage to demand the release of the hostages. When the leader, Bald, refuses, Edward pours all the tender's water into the first-class carriage, drenching the hijackers and cleaning them out.
- Bald and the others are no match for Edward and Alphonse, and the Blue group is ultimately captured in the settlement, ending the hostage crisis.
- Afterwards, Edward meets Colonel Roy Mustang of the Eastern Command, who rewards him by introducing State Alchemist Shou Tucker — the top expert in biological alchemy, nicknamed the "Sewing-Life Alchemist." Tucker welcomes the brothers, introduces his daughter Nina, and offers his research materials. Tucker, however, has recently had poor results and is at risk of losing his state certification. The brothers stay at Tucker's home for a few days.
- One day Edward visits as usual and is amazed to see a chimera that Tucker has created capable of "human speech." The dog chimera sniffs Edward and calls him "big brother." Edward realizes with horror that Tucker fused his daughter Nina and the pet dog Alexander to make a chimera.
- Worse, Tucker had obtained his state certification by turning his wife into a chimera. Enraged, Edward and Alphonse beat Tucker mercilessly. Tucker is stripped of his state certification and brought to military trial.
- That night, after Edward leaves, a man appears and murders Shou Tucker and the Nina chimera. Lieutenant Colonel Maes Hughes and Lieutenant Colonel Alex Louis Armstrong arrive to investigate and seem to have already deduced what happened: the culprit is the serial killer of State Alchemists known as Scar.
- With Tucker dead, the most famous State Alchemist nearby is clearly Edward Elric. Colonel Mustang panics and leads soldiers to find Edward.
- But it's already too late: Edward and Alphonse are outside in mourning when Scar attacks. With nowhere to run, they fight; during the battle Scar destroys Alphonse's armor, sending his lower body flying. In a rage, Edward attacks, but Scar then shatters Edward's right arm as well, putting him on the brink of death. Luckily, Mustang, Armstrong, and others arrive in time to rescue them and repel Scar.
- Once the incident is settled, Edward sets off for his hometown of Resembool under Armstrong's escort to repair his right arm and Alphonse's armor. On the train to Resembool, Armstrong spots a man outside the window named Dr. Marcoh. Edward, learning that he is a researcher of the Philosopher's Stone, gets off the train and goes after him.
- Dr. Marcoh, who abandoned his State Alchemist status after his research was used for mass murder, is living as a country doctor and cannot bear the guilt. He refuses Edward's request to see his data, warning ominously, "If you see it, you'll see hell." They part without results, but Marcoh follows and tells them that his research materials are in the First Branch of the National Central Library in Central City, saying, "You might reach another truth behind the truth."
- Edward and Alphonse arrive in Resembool and meet Pinako Rockbell, who is effectively their guardian, and their childhood friend Winry Rockbell. When Winry hears that the automail she carefully made has been destroyed, she lightly plants a monkey wrench on Edward's head and then prepares to repair his automail.
- Armstrong helps with chores while staying with them, splitting wood with his fists and the like. Pinako tells them that on the day Edward became a State Alchemist she set their house on fire so they couldn't run away; the children's resolve was something no ordinary person could make.
- A few days later, a new automail is completed and Edward installs his new limbs, quickly overhauls Alphonse's armor, and after staying a few more days, leaves Resembool. After they leave, Winry finds a bolt with an identification tag rolling on the workbench.
- Having finished repairs, the Elric brothers take the train to Central. Armstrong parts with them there, but because Scar's whereabouts are still unknown he cannot fully withdraw his escort. His direct subordinates, Lieutenant Maria Ross and Staff Sergeant Danny Blouse, act as secondary guards and, guided by Ross, the brothers head to the First Branch of the Central National Library, only to find it already reduced to ashes by an unexplained fire just before Edward's arrival.
- Edward confirms that Marcoh's materials did exist in the main library and is introduced to Sheska, who had worked at the First Branch until recently. This book-loving girl had been fired for reading on the job. When told the brothers are looking for Marcoh's research, she astonishingly uses her perfect memory to copy Marcoh's entire research over five days from memory.
- However, the manuscript Sheska brings is a cookbook titled "One Hundred Recipes of Today." Ross and Blouse are disappointed at how irrelevant it seems, but Edward is strangely overjoyed and pays Sheska a large reward before taking the materials back to the Central Library.
- Because alchemical research can cause enormous economic and social upheaval if released, such texts are written in metaphors and riddles so only the author can understand them; decoding them takes a long time. The "One Hundred Recipes of Today" looks like a mere cookbook but in fact is an alchemical research manuscript filled with codes and metaphors.
- The Elric brothers struggle for a week to decipher the complex cipher. Sheska later comes by to thank Edward, saying she used the money to put her mother in a good hospital. Lieutenant Colonel Hughes also visits; with the First Branch destroyed, military records and court documents were lost, causing Hughes trouble. Edward's introduction of Sheska, who had worked at the branch, helps find her a new job.
- The decoding work continues. On the tenth day of their efforts, Edward and Alphonse are speechless. Edward: "The ingredients for the Philosopher's Stone... are living humans!!" Having finally decoded the cipher, the brothers are horrified and despairing at the absurd truth of what they've been searching for, losing their will and skipping meals for days.
- Colonel Armstrong visits to console them, saying, "The truth can be cruel." Edward suddenly recalls Marcoh's remark about "another truth behind the truth" and suspects this is not the whole story. He seeks nearby alchemical laboratories.
- Seeing that the Fifth Laboratory is near a prison, he suspects prisoners may have been used as Philosopher's Stone material. Sneaking out, he and Alphonse head for the lab; leaving Alphonse outside because his size makes stealth difficult, Edward goes in alone and finally discovers a transmutation circle for making the Philosopher's Stone. Edward encounters the guard known as "No. 48" and fights. At the same time Alphonse fights "No. 66."
- As Edward predicted, No. 48 was a condemned man brought in but, recognized for past talent as a duelist, had his soul bound to armor and was operating like Alphonse. Edward cleverly injures and finally defeats No. 48, but still refuses to kill him, saying "I don't want to commit murder."

No. 48: "Spare me the pretty talk. Do we, in this sorry state, look like people? It's not 'kill'—it's 'destroy.'"
Edward: "If I admit that you aren't human, then I'd be admitting that my brother isn't human either."

- No. 48, seeming to take a liking to Edward, cackles and tries to tell him what he knows, but at that moment Lust and Envy cut in, kill the No. 48 and No. 66 brothers and shut him up. Edward strains with all his might to oppose Envy, but suddenly his right arm’s mechanism stops functioning.
- Envy kicks Edward in the stomach and knocks him out, then hands Ed over to Lieutenant Ross and Sergeant Blosch, who just happened to arrive outside, before leaving and blowing the lab up entirely. Edward ends up hospitalized, and he contacts Winry to ask her to do field repairs.

Using quick thinking to decompose dynamite into ammonia, Edward incapacitated Darius and Heinkel, knocked Kimblee’s Philosopher’s Stone out of him and damaged his transmutation circle, apparently winning — but Kimblee produced another Philosopher’s Stone, attacked, and Edward suffered a fatal abdominal impalement.
- Edward barely kept his sanity, used alchemy to clear the wreckage and rescue the trapped Darius and Heinkel, and while the two pulled the iron beams out of his body he used his own soul as a Philosopher’s Stone to seal his wounds. But having only barely survived, Edward had to spend some time recovering, and in that period he was separated from Al.

## 3.3. Later:
- Afterwards Edward sneaks into Colonel Mustang's hideout to meet Al. He joins up with Ling Yao (Greed), who had betrayed the other homunculi again (formally entering as Greed's subordinate), and as the promised day approaches they begin moving toward Central. In that process Edward finally comes face to face with his father.
- Edward openly shows hostility toward Hohenheim and, upon meeting, immediately punches him with his automail. Afterwards Edward hears the full story and is confused, but they confirm they will fight together, and when Edward relays his mother's final words he is bewildered to see Hohenheim shed tears.
- That dawn they struggle quite a bit against the Gluttony & Pride duo who attacked and took Alphonse hostage, but with Lan Fan's arrival they recover Alphonse from Pride. Pride devours Gluttony and, despite a pincer attack with Greed, presses them to the brink of death. However, Alphonse's wit in asking to be separated from Pride and Hohenheim's efforts succeed in imprisoning Pride.
- Edward then enters Central and infiltrates underground through the Third Laboratory, engaging in battle with the doll soldiers that spring out. He realizes the dolls have souls fixed to them and struggles with whether killing them is murder; he insists on only cutting off their legs.
- Roy Mustang, who sneaked into the same area, joins Edward to deal with the dolls. Edward tries to stop Roy from brutally killing Envy, Hughes' nemesis, and in the end shows pity for Envy, which causes Envy to despair at being pitied by a human and thus indirectly die. With the activation of the Great Transmutation Circle, Alphonse Elric and Izumi Curtis are absorbed into the transmutation circle as sacrifices.
- After the Nationwide Transmutation Circle activates, Hohenheim's main transmutation circle and Scar's brother's reverse transmutation circles activate successively, giving them a chance to turn the tide. They pursue the Dwarf in the Flask who went to the surface to create the Philosopher's Stone, but when Pride seizes Edward, Edward stays behind to fight alone.
- During Mustang's forced sacrifice, Pride is mortally wounded and Edward, barely holding on, is in danger of having his body taken over by Pride. At that moment, Kimblee — who disapproved of Pride's method — interferes, causing Pride to hesitate. Kimblee then turns himself into a Philosopher's Stone and, in reverse, invades Pride's body to seize Pride's true form. Because Edward didn't kill Kimblee while fighting him, Kimblee could later be absorbed by Pride, and by holding to his convictions Edward won Kimblee's recognition. Desperately trying to escape fatal wounds, Edward used his own soul as a Philosopher's Stone — and with the knowledge gained from that process he was able to neutralize Pride.
- The entire sequence in Briggs where Edward confronted Kimblee was a huge foreshadowing for this battle. Edward arrives late on the surface and confronts the Dwarf in the Flask, losing his automail and nearly being absorbed by his father, but Alphonse Elric, inspired by the law of equivalent exchange, gains May Chang's cooperation and offers his own soul so Edward's right arm returns. Edward then savagely beats his "father" until he collapses.
- Edward literally knocks down his father — who had seemed godlike in power and majesty, inspiring despair that he could not be defeated — and says: Edward: "Get up, third-rate!!! We'll show you the gulf between you and us!!!"
- After the fight, Edward sacrifices his Gate of Truth to bring his brother Alphonse's body back, and they return together. This method can only be used by Edward.
- Because Edward's and Alphonse's minds were entangled, Edward alone had two Gates of Truth, so even if he destroyed his own Gate he could still return through Alphonse's remaining Gate — that's why the method worked. Others could not use this method because they would have no Gate to return through.
- Thanks to that, Edward is acknowledged by Truth as "you beat me," and he regains everything he had lost because of alchemy. In that scene Truth says to Edward Elric before he loses his Gate, "That's correct, alchemist!" but when pointing him to the exit says, "The exit is that way, Edward Elric." Because of that, Edward can never use alchemy again, but after being cut off from Alphonse's link his height starts growing again and by the ending his complex is gone.
- From then on Edward is steel, but no longer an alchemist. His legs remain steel prosthetics, left as they are as a sign of reflecting on his past. Because he said, "Winry would be sad without this," Hughes' wife and Elicia call him a doting fool after hearing this from Al.
- Edward then travels west to learn knowledge while Al travels east to learn the other side's knowledge, and together they vow to help people who suffer from alchemy, like Nina.
- Since Hohenheim was the last survivor of Xerxes, Edward and Al are descendants of Xerxes. Including Edward's and Al's children, the bloodline of Xerxes' clan has not been cut off. Edward returned with Alphonse at the cost of his Gate of Truth, so Edward has no alchemical ability, but Alphonse still has his Gate of Truth and can use alchemy.
- Edward later marries Winry and they have two children who look just like them.
- Edward lost both parents tragically, suffered awful things in childhood, lost his brother's body and nearly only recovered his soul, and lost his own arm and leg, but with indomitable spirit, fighting will, and a good heart he protected all those around him and all the people of Amestris, recovered one of his arms and his brother's body, and married his childhood friend Winry and had children.
- For the Elric brothers, who endured tremendous hardships and suffering, there could be no happier, more peaceful end to their journey.
- Edward Elric spends the rest of his life happily, but as the price for using twelve years' worth of life energy, he dies of old age at 88 in the continental year 1987. If he hadn't used up those twelve years, he would have lived to about 100.

# 4. Evaluation of Edward:
- In the early days after becoming a State Alchemist, Edward had gotten a taste for alchemy, but he was still quite steeped in arrogance, and, aside from human transmutation, he often thought that alchemy could solve everything.
- Also, Edward came from a bad environment (his father left home and never returned, his mother died young), and because he had the exceptional qualities to get through it on his own, he tended to take on many things by himself—positively, he was independent and responsible, but negatively, he didn't know how to rely on others and was arrogant.
- However, through experiencing many events, Edward learns to rely on others, to curb his temper and cooperate, and eventually even reconciles with his father. Having witnessed the various harms of alchemy, he realizes in the final battle that alchemy is after all just a technique that can become utterly corrupt when it degrades, and that the combined will of humanity can exert a power beyond the law of Equivalent Exchange that alchemy represents. Edward: "Even without alchemy, we have each other."

# 5. Edward Elric's Human Relationships:
- Alphonse Elric: Younger brother. Since their father left and their mother had died, he is the only remaining blood relative, so Edward cherishes him deeply. He truly values Alphonse, who has shared joys and hardships with him as a brother.
- Van Hohenheim: Father. He left the family for reasons unknown, and Edward resented him bitterly for a time, but after many events they ultimately succeed in reconciling.
- Trisha Elric: A mother he loved so much. Because of that, she is an object of longing to the extent that he broke taboos to try to bring his dead mother back to life.
- Winry Rockbell: Childhood friend and like family. He usually teases Winry as an automail otaku and they often bicker, but in truth he values her as much as Alphonse. He became conscious of Winry as a romantic interest, and eventually proposes and succeeds in marrying her.
- Pinako Rockbell: A relationship like grandmother and grandson. It was Pinako who took them in when Ed and Al were born. They usually bicker about their small statures.
- Roy Mustang: Outwardly a superior, but despite the rank difference and a 14-year age gap, they have a friendly relationship, and to Edward Roy is like an older brother. He was a mentor who showed Edward a new path, and at the end when Roy becomes consumed by vengeance and tries to go down the wrong road, Edward guides him back. They go through many incidents together or separately and, based on what they gain from those events, exchange mutual teachings and lessons.
- Izumi Curtis: Teacher. On the surface she may seem like a cruel drill instructor who trains harshly, but in fact she and her husband care for the Elric brothers more than anyone. The childless Curtises and the Elric brothers, who lost their parents early, give each other the parent-child affection they lack. Izumi's husband, Sig Curtis, is also a martial arts master not inferior to Izumi and quietly supports the Elric brothers.
- Scar: When they first met he tried to kill him, and since Scar is the enemy who killed Winry's parents, even though they cooperate from the mid-to-late stages, their relationship can never truly be good — it's complicated.
- Ling Yao: One of the few peers of the same age.
- Nina Tucker: The daughter of Shou Tucker. When Nina became a chimera due to her father Shou Tucker's experiment, Edward was greatly shocked and felt deep guilt for failing to save her. When he confronted Scar, who had killed the chimera Nina, Scar replied that even if she had lived she would have been taken by the military and made an experimental subject, doomed to suffer for life, so Edward could not properly counter him. Nina had a major influence on his future path: he abandoned the arrogance that alchemy could do anything, and in the final chapter, when asked by the Truth whether he would accept becoming an ordinary human who could never use alchemy again in exchange for bringing Alphonse back, he mentions that he failed to save Nina and answers that he had been an ordinary human from the start, unable even to save one girl. Because of this, he is acknowledged by the Truth and succeeds in bringing Alphonse back.
