# 3. Iseri Nina's characteristics:
## 3.1. Background:
- Before starting the band, she was an ordinary girl who lived by reading the room and adapting to those around her without any particular goals.
- She attended school in her hometown Kumamoto until her first year of high school, then dropped out and went to Tokyo on a whim. While she was planning to study hard at a cram school for university entrance exams, she happened to cross paths with Kawaragi Momoka, the guitarist of the indie band Diamond Dust. Momoka noticed her hidden musical talent and scouted her, and she joined the rock band Togenashi Togeari to work as the vocalist, frontman, and singer-songwriter.
- Dropped out of school: She dropped out after suffering deep emotional wounds and a distrust of people due to bullying at school, her father's improper handling of it, and conflicts with friends.
- Also, her father imposed a peculiar and strict family motto,

1) Meals must be eaten at home with the family gathered.
2) No cell phone use in the living room.
3) Have a family meeting every Sunday night.

- Her parents bragged that they were proud of living differently from others and went around speaking and behaving soaked in baseless confidence, and because of this they were quite well-known at school.
- Some classmates bullied her, but when her parents found out they covered the whole thing up instead of helping, saying not to make needless fuss. In particular, her father — a prominent figure known in the public as a famous educator — took the lead in concealing the incident out of concern for his own social standing, and because of that she was left half estranged from him (the reasoning being that if the daughter of a famous educator got into scandal and became a delinquent, of course that father's reputation would be ruined. In short, he prioritized his authority over his daughter's welfare).
- Because Nina was among the top academic performers and he couldn't bear to see her suffer, her father made a deal that the bullying incident would be dropped if the school would work to recommend her for admission to a prestigious university — in other words, he asked the principal to write a recommendation letter for Nina.
- But from Nina's point of view she couldn't accept the perpetrators getting off without any punishment, and as she became treated even more oddly and isolated by other students and suffered severe trauma, she ended up quitting school.
- She would sometimes get angry while checking social media on her phone because she saw the ringleaders of the school violence posting happily with no sign of guilt.
- Even when Nina was refusing to attend school, her sister said their father scolded her simply because he was ashamed to have a daughter who refused to go to school.
- It was clear her father was someone who cared a great deal about how others perceived him, and the fact that he quieted the school bullying with a recommendation letter for a prestigious university felt much more like seeking the title of “father of a daughter who goes to a famous university” than an act for Nina — and Nina thought the same.
- Simply put, her school years, when she should have been in full bloom, were ruined by vicious group bullying and her father's wrongdoing.
- After that, when she insisted on quitting school her father gave practical warnings about fewer choices in the future and being treated like a dropout, but she believed he was not genuinely worried about her and was cold and dismissive from lack of interest, so she stubbornly decided to study on her own and take the college entrance exam, and as mentioned above she went up to Tokyo (she wanted to show her parents and high school classmates who looked down on her that she could succeed at a prestigious university — she was being willful because she didn't want to lose).
- However, aside from covering her initial living expenses her parents mostly lost interest, and she endured unexpected hardships including being scammed by the uncle who had loudly promised to find her a nice apartment.
- The uncle colluded with a real estate agent and unloaded an unsellable leftover unit in a peripheral area, not in Tokyo. He exploited the fact that Nina was naive about the world and wouldn't be able to tell from the address whether it was in Tokyo. Momoka said, "Dude, you got scammed by your uncle, lol," and that's how she found out.
- After she started band activities she went back home and had a proper conversation with her parents. At that time her father — who is weak to alcohol — drank and there was an ashtray full of cigarettes, which depicted how much he had agonized as a parent. He effectively tore up the family motto, tried to go to the school and undo the previous deal to get the perpetrators punished and an apology from the school, abandoned his pride as an educator and went around asking acquaintances for educational advice, and most decisively he sided with Nina, saying she was the victim — his behavior changed completely. Her sister said, "After all, Dad loves you," revealing that Nina's belief that her father didn't worry about or care for her was a misunderstanding.
- Of course, that doesn't erase the father's past mistakes, and the sister added "after all" to stress that the father had been in the wrong and that Nina had every reason not to feel adequately loved by him.

## 3.2. Kawasaki's Mad Dog (川崎の狂犬):
- "せからしかー!!!! (시끄릅다ー!!!!)" (She got into an argument with a man on the street, swung a streetlight and shouted "세카라시카!" and the man ran away.)
- Nina's personality is delicate but rough and explosive; if provoked she can be dangerous. Contrary to her outward appearance, which might seem gentle and quiet, she has a temper, is bold with a sly streak of craziness, and can be violent—when her switch flips she doesn't filter what she says and barges in like a wild boar (Subaru described her as an angry bull). In short, she's fiery and scrappy, with a wild-horse temperament.
- She can surprisingly accept and admit faults or mistakes that could be seen as her own immaturity, but when it comes to things she firmly believes are right she is extraordinarily stubborn and never gives in. Never. She clashed with the school's delinquents and became the target of bullying and assault, but she never once begged for forgiveness or yielded. Her will to express that she is right and to resist and break the world's prejudice and oppression became the spirit of the Togenashi Togeari Band.
- Nina hates lies and has a strong, fighting, and straightforward spirit that pushes hard for what she believes is right. (This also applies to choices she has made herself. She threw away a lifelong habit and aptitude for studying and, only after entering high school, began a rock band and rock music—an extremely precarious situation for a novice musician and singer-songwriter who has bet her life on it—but she has never once regretted or feared her choice.)
- Nina dropped out and moved to Tokyo to show her family she wasn't wrong, and after meeting Momoka she continued with the band to prove that the song Momoka believed in—the song that made the current Nina—was not wrong.
- Nina really hates lying; when Subaru tried to use trickery to persuade Momoka she reacted strongly and stopped him. Even toward Tomo, who treats her relatively gently, she calmly accepted and acknowledged harsh criticism of her guitar skills, but when Tomo said something intended to flatter her that didn't reflect his true feelings, Nina immediately asked with a stern expression, "Why are you lying?"
- Later, Tomo listened to Nina's guitar playing and criticized it with excessively sharp words—so sharp that even Subaru recoiled—due to personal trauma, but Nina wasn't upset and said she'd work harder. Though Nina is stubborn, she is by no means the kind of self-righteous person who insists she's always right about everything.
- She has many mental instabilities, but except in situations related to her trauma (mainly when entangled with peers—though Nina can be thoughtful at times, she is uniquely aggressive and often shows an embarrassing side with peers she meets for the first time; meeting Subaru for the first time is a prime example), her mental resilience is very strong. Characters whose narratives begin from admiration often become overly dependent on their object of admiration; Nina is obsessive about Momoka but surprisingly not dependent. In episode 8 she proves this by showing she respects and admires Momoka but is ready to cut ties anytime she feels it's necessary, demonstrating that she is far from being dependent.
- Though single-minded, violent, and rough in some ways, her core values are healthy and upright. She was deeply wounded by her father and school violence, developed a twisted tendency to reject or avoid peers and to feel inadequacy, but she has never once blamed her family or others for her personality or current situation.
- Nina has a strong sense of justice—she rescued a bullied child without hesitation even though it could have put her in danger—and when her characteristic stubbornness pops up in conversations with band members they say “さすが 肥後もこす” (as expected, Higomokosu). Higomokosu refers to the pure, righteous, and stubborn personality typical of people from Kumamoto Prefecture.
- Nina is also extremely "strong against the strong, gentle to the weak." The more you try to suppress her with force, the more violently and roughly she fights back: she reacts overly aggressively to threats from people bigger and stronger than her—such as the delinquents who beat her in the past, the man who complained "you're noisy" while she was crying holding a streetlight, or Momoka, who almost struck her in a fit of temper after the fireworks festival live—but she is very lenient, gentle, and kind toward people weaker than her.
- Strong-against-the-strong, gentle-to-the-weak temperament: she clearly condemns Hina—who turned away from a bullied victim and cut off Nina, who went to help—as wrongdoing and treats her as a coward and hypocrite unworthy of being called an "empty box" until she understands Hina's true feelings; by contrast, the student who truly committed the cowardly and ungrateful act of betraying the one who saved them and joining the delinquents in tormenting Nina is met with understanding and even defended by Nina. (Hina's act regarding the bullying was bystanding, but this student's act was participation. Behavior-wise it was far worse, but the student had just been freed from ostracism and was in a state of diminished mental capacity and therefore could not defy the delinquents, so Nina does not blame them; when she talks about it she only feels hollow rather than angry.)
- Thus, Nina applies an excessively strict standard to Hina, who had the power to choose, but toward the victim student—who is weak socially, physically, and mentally—she is so kind that she almost looks like a pushover.

## 3.3. Etiquette:
- Because Nina was taught by her father from childhood by sitting together at the table for every meal, she surprisingly doesn't use profanity or swear words unless her emotions are driven to the extreme. And when her hands are free she always clasps them together in front (the other members fidget with their hands in ordinary ways—putting them in their pockets, resting them on their hips, or letting them hang at their sides—but Nina politely clasps hers. She also walks with her hands clasped). When sitting on the floor she always sits in seiza; every little action is imbued with manners. Seeing this side of her, Momoka once said to Nina, "You're actually a young lady, aren't you?" and even mistook her for a rich family's daughter.
- Nina's household was economically average, but because her father held a certain authority in youth education, he strictly taught her manners. So Nina also displays good etiquette.
- Pinky finger: the characteristic pinky-finger insult of Togenashi Togeari, where she raises her pinky instead of the middle finger; it's a compromise Nina devised between the urge to express burning emotions and the thought that she can't use such vulgar, low-class expressions.
- Also, even when she's angry she makes a point of saying thanks after meals and then leaving, and in the past bullying incident she refused the adults' forced apology and tried to occupy the broadcasting room to sing as she pleased; yet when actually leaving the principal's office she performs a polite belly-bow—an unbalanced behavior. She showed the most temper and grievances while at the same time offering the most bows.
- This is a habit completely ingrained in Nina, so even now, after she broke away from her father and began the life of a free rocker, bandman, and singer-songwriter, it remains in every one of her actions, and since Nina doesn't particularly dislike this side of herself or try to erase it, it seems unlikely to disappear.
- Nina also shows the naive, country-girl ignorance about the ways of the world: she once fell for Momoka's lie and wholeheartedly believed that "Fuck you" meant "thank you," and she had never once eaten gyudon (a cheap, quick dish treated as a national fast food in Japan) before the main story, so she was extremely surprised when she first saw it ("This is... gyudon!!!" she exclaimed).
- This too was because her father strictly forbade eating out, so she never had a chance to try it.

## 3.4. Outsider:
- Nina is extremely avoidant and rejecting when it comes to relationships because of her father's wrongdoing and the pain and sense of betrayal from school violence. She craves and wants people's attention, yet when they come close she rejects and avoids them, becoming a cold, twisted person. For example, when Subaru keeps paying attention to get close to her, she puts up walls, but inwardly she knows full well about his kindness and has at times fallen into self-loathing, calling her own overly sharp reactions pathetic. She also shows emotionally unstable, twisted behavior, at times lashing out violently—swinging objects at passersby, for instance.
- Even if not entirely because of her past, Nina’s personality was always severely twisted: clumsy at dealing with people, overly sensitive, lacking tact, and stubbornly single-minded.
- She has a particularly strong wariness toward kids her age, especially those wearing school uniforms. While other instances of Nina’s aggressive behavior might have some kind of reason, her mistrust of peers stems purely from personal trauma, and the targets are often truly innocent.
- Because she gets along very well with Subaru and Tomo, she can seem healed at a glance, but in truth Tomo stopped going to school long ago and has a personality she feels affinity with, and Subaru is simply someone she became personally close to. She even gets extremely tense just from seeing kids buying food at the school shop.
- It’s not something that can be easily fixed, so Nina often torments herself with feelings of inadequacy (when children come near she feels hostility; after they pass, she feels sadness and self-disgust).

# 4. Iseri Nina's actions in the story:
## 4.1. Past:
- Nina had apparently long been dissatisfied with the overly stifling atmosphere at home, but until she quit school there had been no major incident to make that dissatisfaction explode, so she obediently followed her parents' wishes and was rigorously educated; as a result she was an outstanding, high-achieving model student.
- However, when bullying occurred in Nina and Hina's class, Nina's previously smooth life changed dramatically. Originally Nina was not the target of the bullying, but one day she witnessed a student being forcibly dragged out of the classroom, and when that friend sent a pleading look to Nina and Hina, who remained in the room, Nina decided to save the victim.
- Nina said the student had asked them for help because they were friends, but Hina countered, "He clings to us because there's no one else around."
- Hina, who had been a close friend at the time, warned, "If you go to save him, they'll make you their next target, and if that happens I can't take your side," and even scolded her saying, "Why would you go that far? It annoys me when you act like that," trying to dissuade her. But Nina replied, "I don't like it; I can't just shut up and watch something like that," and left the classroom.
- In the end, Nina was badly injured while saving that student and had to be taken to the hospital (her injuries were severe enough that she was hospitalized and treated, and she wore gauze on her right forehead). Hina, as she had warned, cut ties with the injured Nina, saying she could no longer take her side, and even criticized her: "When you behave like you're the only one who's righteous, it makes me look like the bad person."
- After that, they sometimes spoke in places out of other students' sight, like the rooftop, but those conversations were filled with reproach like "This is your fault for not listening to me," which only pushed them further apart.
- Eventually they broke off their friendship completely over the difference in opinion. Nina's father arranged a three-way meeting with the other student's parents and the school, and the school tried to mediate by having the victim—Nina—and the perpetrator apologize to each other and shake hands. Unable to bear it, Nina murmured "Empty Box" by Diamond Dust Momoka, found the courage to defy her father for the first time, refused the perpetrator's handshake, ran to the broadcasting room, barricaded the door with a pipe chair and occupied it, and used the school broadcast to sing "Empty Box" and shout whatever she wanted.
- In conclusion, Nina did the right thing by helping a friend, but as a result she became a target of bullying and was assaulted; afterward the ostracism continued and she suffered damages to school property and thefts (her shoes were stolen, her desk was drenched, and she was assaulted), enduring painful days.
- More shocking to Nina than the bullying itself was being abandoned by people she trusted—like Hina and her father—and even the friend she had saved joined in tormenting her.
- That friend even justified herself and shifted the blame onto Nina, saying, "If you apologize to them now they'll forgive you, and I have to do this because of you," (the friend was trembling with fear while saying this—she was probably being threatened by the delinquent group that had bullied her).
- Ultimately, unable to bear the bullying and her resentment toward her father, Nina refused to attend school and ended up quitting. When her father, refusing to accept it, asked how she would live with only a middle-school education, she told him she would get into university on her own, received a modest support payment such as a security deposit, and left home.

## 4.2. Moving to Kawasaki:
### 4.2.1. Idol — Meeting Momoka:
- On the first day she left her hometown Kumamoto and arrived in Tokyo, she got lost in the crowded subway and, because her smartphone battery died, failed to contact the real estate agent she was supposed to meet, causing the misfortune of being unable to get into her rented room.
- She eventually managed to charge her phone at a nearby café, but it was too late to get into the room that day. A call from her mother—half worry, half scolding—stressed her out and she hung up. While there, she saw on social media that Kawaragi Momoka, the guitarist and vocalist of the indie band Diamond Dust whom she had liked for a long time, was doing a street live nearby. She ran there and had a fateful meeting with Momoka. When she told Momoka she couldn’t get into her place, Momoka, as a fan service, bought her a gyudon and let her stay the night at her place.
- At this time she learned from Momoka that Kawasaki is not part of Tokyo but belongs to Kanagawa, and she also first met Rupa and Tomo in Kawasaki, who later become her bandmates.
- Nina was happy to have the chance to stay overnight at Momoka’s house, her favorite singer, but she also learned the shocking facts that Momoka was thinking of giving up being a musician and returning to her hometown, and that she had signed away the copyrights to all the songs she composed while active in Diamond Dust.
- In the end, Momoka got annoyed with Nina for being nosy and meddling in her personal choices, and Nina was disappointed and conflicted because Momoka dismissed her worries as the spoiled complaints of someone who had received financial support.
- Nina left Momoka’s house. Momoka, worried, followed her and they sang together at karaoke; then Nina received Momoka’s beloved guitar from the retiring musician and they said goodbye.
- But Nina’s desperate plea that she didn’t want Momoka to give up miraculously called Momoka back. They teamed up with two bands—a male and a female band that had originally been at odds with Momoka—and held a street live, performing Nina’s favorite Momoka song, 'Empty Box'. Through that song they discovered Nina’s talent—outstanding singing and perfect pitch despite no formal musical training—and convinced by this, Momoka decided to continue as a musician.

## 4.2.2. Band Formation:
- Because Momoka cleaned out her room intending to go home, she suddenly had nowhere to live, so the roles reversed and she ended up living in Nina's apartment for a few days. Nina's illusions as a fan were completely shattered as she watched Momoka, who stayed up late every night, woke up late, drank and vomited — a total mess.
- Momoka assumed that Nina, who had done the street live with her, would naturally join the band (Nina told Momoka, "Let's live with our middle fingers up together!" — Momoka took this to mean "let's do rock together," but Nina had simply meant "hang in there and live your own life"). In truth, Nina was satisfied just to have become acquainted with Momoka and planned to keep studying. Momoka repeatedly urged Nina to take the vocalist spot, insisting that Nina’s vocals and skill shown that day made her cut out to be a rock band vocalist, but Nina stubbornly refused the offer until the day Momoka returned to her own room.
- They parted ways encouraging each other, but life afterward was still far from smooth. This reveals the particular fear of peers typical of kids ostracized at school: Nina could communicate fine with passersby, neighbors, and adults like Momoka, but merely facing fellow high school students made her tense and feel almost panicked.
- Even brushing past peers on the street put her on edge and in a bad mood, so there was no way she could make friends at prep school. Living alone in her apartment, repeating study and sleep, Nina found herself so lonely and unable to concentrate on studying that she realized the chaotic days living with Momoka a few days earlier had been better, and she cried alone over her own helplessness.
- Then, happy to get contact from Momoka for the first time in a while, she ran over, but felt displeasure and discomfort when she saw the band members Momoka had recruited — Awa and Subaru the drummers — and even recalled the times she’d felt excluded and bullied between Momoka and Subaru.
- Subaru, aware that Nina was uncomfortable, tried to leave her space or speak to her as politely as possible and did his best to be considerate, but the more he did, the worse Nina’s insecurity and discomfort became. Eventually she made her annoyance obvious at the meal, sulked heavily, clashed with Momoka and stormed out of the restaurant.
- While loudly scolding herself for her immature behavior and engaging in self-loathing, a passing man complained that she was being noisy, and she tried to swing a light fixture like a flail to attack him. She swung it, but only the lampshade/cover was wrecked and, luckily, the lamp itself didn’t break.
- Back in her room, sinking into self-hatred and crying alone, she is reached by Momoka and Subaru, who chased after her to comfort her. Moved by their sincere appeals, she honestly accepts their kindness and high praise, and the three of them form a band.

## 4.2. "New Kawasaki (temporary)" period: 
### 4.2.1. First performance:
- A few days later, she becomes obsessed with a composition app Momoka taught her to use (an app called Figure, available only on iOS — Nina’s phone is an iPhone) and studies pitch so intensely that she neglects even her study time at home. When Momoka points this out, Nina immediately straightens up and goes to study.
- This was also the most indecisive period for Nina, repeating avoidance between studying and the band without committing to either. However, from this time her heart was already clearly leaning toward the band.
- Meanwhile, Subaru keeps trying to get closer to Nina — actively talking to her, offering to walk home with her, and so on — but Nina’s avoidant tendencies kick in and she rejects Subaru, unintentionally hurting him. The next day, when Subaru offers a meal as thanks for returning his phone, she again refuses using the excuse of studying, and when Subaru, unable to bear it, even begins to feel he might need to leave the band, Nina expresses that she appreciates his consideration but can’t honestly accept his kindness.
- During the conversation, Nina learns that Subaru heard about her past from Momoka and, upset, lashes out with an aggressive line: "It must be nice to be in a position to look down on people." Subaru, stung, snaps back and they get into a shouting match. In the process, however, Nina discovers that although Subaru is treated as the heir of a famous actor family, he actually has no interest in acting and feels rebellion and freedom through band activity. This becomes a turning point that brings them closer: Subaru realizes he should treat Nina naturally rather than approach her more than necessary, and Nina stops putting up the same walls against Subaru.
- From then on, Subaru’s way of calling Nina shifts from "Nina-chan" to "Ni-na", and Nina also begins calling Subaru "Subaru-chan" and drops formal speech.
- They hold their first live show as a band on a small outdoor stage, but Nina — who has no experience as a musician and only ever performed and sang improvisationally — gets so nervous during a planned, prepared live show that she can’t sing. Momoka persuades her to go on, but under Momoka’s judgment that Nina will only grow if pushed to go all-out, she’s given a cute but tacky and ridiculous idol-like stage costume and is too embarrassed to go on stage.
- However, Momoka points out each of Nina’s twisted personality traits and tells her that those very things are rock, which gives Nina courage and they manage to perform successfully. During preparations for this outdoor stage they created the group name "New Kawasaki (temporary)", and they draw the attention of Benishoga, a group that would later become their comrades and unite as a team.
- Also, perhaps thanks to Momoka’s reverse-psychology tactic working, after performing once in that embarrassing outfit, Nina never again refuses to go on stage for fear of embarrassment.
- Gaining confidence from their first show, Nina becomes more serious about band activities and even starts to form long-term goals, vaguely thinking she would like to play guitar along with singing like Momoka in the future.
- But one day Subaru receives a text during practice and then disappears. Practice is cut short that day, and when Nina returns home she asks Momoka if this means Subaru has a problem with Diamond Dust. Momoka shows Nina a photo from high school of them laughing and enjoying the band — explaining that the girls’ band got big but she only quit — and seeing Momoka’s smile from those high school days makes Nina admire band activity even more.
- The very next morning, though, Subaru sends a text saying he’s quitting the band without explanation. Naturally unable to accept it, Nina drags Momoka and barges into Subaru’s acting school. Subaru, unusually, puts on an oddly prim and proper act and pretends not to know Nina and Momoka; when their argument is overheard by his grandmother, Subaru awkwardly lies that Nina and Momoka are actor friends, showing unnatural behavior and speech.
- While the grandmother steps away, Subaru confesses the whole story — that he didn’t truly intend to quit the band, and that his grandmother, who believes he is devoting himself to acting, unexpectedly showed up and he blurted out that he was quitting. Nina doesn’t accept this and stays angry. Because of Subaru’s lie, she’s forced into an impromptu short skit in front of his grandmother; using the lines of an ex-girlfriend role, Nina takes a jab at Subaru: "I was serious about doing the band with you — how can you say you’re quitting so easily?"
- Subaru, offended, keeps arguing with Nina even during rehearsal. Eventually he calls Nina over to his place and confides that his grandmother, who impulsively said she wanted to be an actress, has put a lot of expectations on him. Nina tells him that he shouldn’t lie to his grandmother any more, and in response Subaru says he’ll be honest with his grandmother after this drama shoot and plans to genuinely do the band with Nina — and the two become more relaxed friends.
- However, on the day of the drama shoot, after talking with Subaru’s grandmother, Nina sees the lonely side of the grandmother — believing there’s no family who likes her profession except her granddaughter — and her genuine smile at the thought of working together with her granddaughter. Impulsively feeling she mustn’t disappoint the grandmother, Nina stops Subaru from telling the truth. Subaru, his rare resolution thwarted, confronts Nina; she stalls and doesn’t reveal the truth. Afterward Nina calls Subaru a "liar" and Subaru calls Nina a "Righteousness Monster."

## 4.2.2. Live-house performance:
- After the team’s cohesion had strengthened, Shin Kawasaki, aiming to raise their profile in earnest, rented the live house Momoka had connections with from her Diamond Dust days, booked a buzzworthy show, and planned to sell their allotted number of tickets. However, unlike Momoka—who had plenty of experience and name recognition and thus no problem—and Subaru, who was green as a band member but savvy and well-connected, Nina was a loner and was the only one who couldn’t sell any tickets at all.
- On top of that, lacking the nerve to take money and not realizing the tickets had been bought with their own money (more precisely Momoka’s money — at this point Momoka had voluntarily been covering all band activity costs because she was the only adult), she made mistakes like giving one away for free to the person next to her at cram school who was just carrying a spare.
- Hearing this, Momoka and Subaru explained the ticket-quota system, and Momoka told Nina, “If it’s a ticket bought with someone’s own money, they’ll come even if they don’t want to because they’d hate to waste it; a free ticket doesn’t feel like a loss, so they’re likely not to come.”
- Moreover, the person who’d gotten a free ticket from Nina surprisingly showed interest and asked if there were three more tickets for friends, but Nina, without any explanation, bluntly told them to pay this time and even put up a cold wall saying “if you don’t want to, don’t buy,” thereby blowing the opportunity to sell the tickets.
- Subaru said she was too inept. As Subaru explained, since the person had taken it free at first without knowing the circumstances, demanding full price out of the blue would make them think it was a sales trick or scam, or at least leave them feeling cheated; they needed an explanation of why last time was free and this time costs, but Nina just cut them off with “if you don’t want to, say so.”
- Afterwards, Rupa from beni-shouga secretly bought two tickets at the place where Nina was venting and offered them to Tomo.
- Meanwhile, Nina saw the face of Diamond Dust’s new vocalist Hina and thought she resembled a friend from her past also named Hina, but unsure, she went with Subaru to see the live in person and, seeing her up close, confirmed that it was the friend she’d cut off after the group-bullying incident in high school. Of all songs, the one they happened to be singing then was “Empty Box,” a song that symbolized rock spirit to Nina.
- Nina was furious, saying they were defiling Diamond Dust, which she revered second only to her life, ran out of the venue, and let out an unintelligible shriek so she wouldn’t actually curse (she’d been strictly raised and couldn’t bring herself to spit out a swear; in the end the filter failed and she let out the double curse くそが！死ねえーー！！).
- As Nina sadly stared at the empty shoe locker, the memory of the bullying incident flashed in her head, the scene where that friend stood behind and said, “See, what did I tell you.”
- Already dissatisfied with Diamond Dust’s direction, Nina began to openly antagonize them after that, and this attitude eventually led to conflict with Momoka, who found it distasteful.
- Nina wanted Momoka to empathize, saying that aside from her personal grudge against the new vocalist, Diamond Dust now wasn’t the Diamond Dust she had loved — didn’t Momoka feel the same? — but Momoka kept deflecting by mentioning Diamond Dust’s commercial success (“they’re more successful and famous than when they were with me”) and wouldn’t speak her true feelings. Practice was halted and they moved to an izakaya, but the argument only intensified.
- Eventually, when Nina said, “Momoka, it’s the band you poured your soul into, right?” Momoka, hiding her true feelings, denied it with “don’t presume,” which made Nina flare up and keep provoking her — “So was Diamond Dust really that great or what?” — and their emotions boiled over to the point they splashed the drinks they were holding into each other’s faces.
- However, when Nina earnestly pleaded that Diamond Dust in Momoka’s time had been the band that changed her life, Momoka self-deprecatingly said that the one at fault was herself, not Diamond Dust, and both cooled off before it turned into a full-scale fight.
- Subaru, who’d stayed safely in the middle the whole time, perhaps annoyingly, poured oolong tea over someone’s head. On the way home, heavily drunk, Momoka—piggybacking on Nina—murmured “sorry…” Subaru thought Momoka was apologizing to Nina, but Nina realized Momoka was apologizing to the Diamond Dust members.
- Taking Subaru’s advice to make use of her hostility toward Diamond Dust, Nina came out at the live wearing clothes labeled “School Refusal (不登校).” (The stage outfits had words revealing each member’s wounds: Momoka wore 脱退 (withdrawal), Subaru wore 嘘つき (liar). On Nina’s back were her name and the number “27” using goroawase, and above it the small text SCHOOL REFUSAL (등교 거부아).) She delivered an explosive performance that literally ripped the stage apart.
- From the audience you can see Rupa and Tomo come and admire them, and through this performance they win their recognition. You can also spot the person from cram school who took Nina’s free ticket; they could have been upset at being scolded for no reason, but fortunately they came to the live and cheered.

## 4.3. Formation of Togenashi Togeari:
### 4.3.1. Alliance with Beni-shouga (beni-shouga):
- After the last live show the band began to get noticed, and they received many comments like "The Diamond Dust without Momoka is a different band than before," so Nina showed this to Momoka, but Momoka dismissed it as the opinion of a few, saying she knew how many fans Diamond Dust actually had. She wanted to promote Shin Kawasaki as a band that inherited the spirit of Diamond Dust from Momoka's era.
- Also, when Nina heard that Momoka had no particular plans for the future she sulked, but Subaru poked her, pointing out that Nina herself wasn't a full-time musician either — she had one foot in school and one in the band.
- Nina decided the band needed to grow more and, following the advice of the live house manager, chose to first build subscribers and buzz on the internet. However, the methods suggested — making outfits cute, posting band member photos emphasizing cuteness on SNS — risked repeating Diamond Dust's mistakes. (Nina didn't want her own photos posted, so without telling Subaru what they were for she took a bunch of pictures and asked, "Can I post these on SNS?" and was rebuffed. She panicked, saying, "What if my grandmother finds out?")
- Nina still said she'd do anything because she wanted to beat Diamond Dust, but this reopened the conflict that had only been superficially resolved before the live house show: Nina, who wanted to beat Diamond Dust, versus Momoka, who claimed she had no real grievances with Diamond Dust.
- After practice Nina and Subaru talked about the band's future at a rice bowl restaurant. When Subaru said it might be reckless to pick a fight with professionals already, Nina replied, "I don't want to give up before the fight. We're not wrong." That line moved Tomo, who had been hesitating about whether to propose joining, and Tomo formally proposed a merger.
- However, Tomo seemed to want to keep the name Beni-shouga (beni-shouga) and absorb Shin Kawasaki unilaterally, and tried not to say "merger" outright, phrasing things in ways that were easy to misunderstand, so Nina and Subaru couldn't make sense of what she meant for a long time. When she said "I came to scout," they answered, "The thing that measures combat power?" and when she said she had come to pick up band members they asked if she was trying to steal Subaru.
- Nina and Subaru recognized Tomo and Rupa's band as Beni-shouga (beni-shouga), a fairly well-known indie band, and confirmed their playing ability was excellent. They lacked nothing in recognition or skill, had a clear goal that filled every gap Shin Kawasaki currently had, and, beyond that, their direction — preferring to play the music they wanted rather than chase mass appeal — matched almost perfectly. Their positions didn't overlap at all and fit together as if the two were born to merge, which made Nina and Subaru ecstatic. But when Nina tried to contact Momoka, Subaru stopped her, sensing Momoka would clash, thinking she wouldn't want Budokan.
- Still, Subaru didn't want to lose them either, so he said he'd come up with some scheme and told her never to call him a liar.
- Eventually, under Subaru's suggestion they lured Momoka and got her to join a joint practice with Beni-shouga (beni-shouga) (they didn't talk about merging the bands, only about practicing together. If it was only practice, Momoka wouldn't ask about Beni-shouga's band goals. Subaru had told Nina not to call him a liar when he explained the plan, but Nina, who likes honesty, immediately called him a liar, and Subaru protested that he had no other option).
- Momoka unconsciously resisted the idea of challenging the pro scene again since she had already run away once, but she thought that if she saw Beni-shouga and Shin Kawasaki produce a great performance together before any talk of going pro, she wouldn't be able to refuse.
- In fact the combination of Beni-shouga (beni-shouga) and Shin Kawasaki was perfect, and after practice Nina bluntly told Momoka that Beni-shouga wanted to merge and that the goal was Budokan. She pleaded with Momoka as a fan and a colleague: "Don't stay a loser — win," and "I want you to prove that you weren't wrong."
- The reason Nina said that was because Subaru had analyzed Momoka's psychological state and Nina had heard that while Momoka enjoys herself fully when playing in the band, outside of that she suffers from self-hatred, feeling like a loser who ran away from Diamond Dust.
- After practice the members decided to solidify their future direction by entering a rock festival as a stage to prove their performance and gain recognition. Nina saw Diamond Dust's name on the participant list and became self-conscious again, watching Momoka, who was troubled on the veranda.

### 4.3.2. Fireworks Festival Live, and the Conflict and Resolution with Momoka:
- Despite Nina's heartfelt persuasion, Momoka remained unconvinced; she didn't openly get angry or refuse, but she gave no answer, expressed no opinion, and a cold war ensued. She also kept teasing Momoka gently. In the end, putting aside irreconcilable issues, they try to decide a band name but after long discussion still can't make up their minds.
- When choosing the band name, at first Nina, Subaru, and Tomo speak at the same time: Nina says "Chocolate," Subaru says "Dream Girl," and Tomo says "Undine."
- They worry over the band name all day, and when they get home Nina's older sister is waiting for her. In fact, the entrance exam prep school had reported back to the family that Nina's grades were falling and she was frequently absent because of band activities. Their furious father wanted to come himself, but the sister said she'd go check instead and waited until late. The sister says it's lucky she came, and Nina doesn't deny it, naturally accepting the sister's offer of a lap pillow.
- The sister and Nina talk about the school violence issue. Nina's father didn't cover up the bullying simply because he wanted to avoid making a fuss; he intended to put the school in debt through this incident so they'd write Nina a recommendation letter to a prestigious university. But Nina couldn't accept getting a recommendation by dishonest means, so she refused to return to school. From the father's standpoint — himself the author of a well-known book on education — he could never accept his daughter's refusal to attend.
- The sister thinks their father was clearly wrong in handling things, but perhaps because she doesn't fully understand Nina's inner situation she says things in a pragmatic way: that Nina essentially threw away an opportunity and if she quits high school and prepares for exams in Tokyo yet neglects studying, the sister can't cover for her. She leaves a warning and exits the room.
- Later, through Mine, an acquaintance of Momoka, they get a live gig at a fireworks festival. While traveling to the venue (Momoka was supposed to drive but didn't know how to drive a manual, so Rufa drove instead), they joke about it being fine to nap and say it's a rare chance for them all to be together and want to play. Subaru notices and suggests a simple game where whoever raises their hand most after he shouts a descriptor wins; he says "people with few friends" and five people raise their hands, so Subaru wins immediately.
- They stop at a rest area and go into the snack stand; seeing students crowded there, they hurriedly just grab bottled water. Although Subaru has become a close friend, most of their peers still feel uncomfortable around Nina. She gives water to Tomo, who was suffering from motion sickness, and when the car is left with just the two of them, she asks why Tomo quit high school.
- Tomo says one day at home she saw her mother being affectionate with a strange man as if she were a different creature; she despised her mother, saw her as someone she couldn't rely on, and immediately thought of becoming independent. Because there are many examples of minor or young newcomers succeeding as artists, she thought she could do it too, quit school at once, and gathered members in Tokyo. She only left school because she wanted to devote herself to music and found school time to be a waste.
- At that moment Tomo looks resignedly at the water bottle in her hand while outside the bus students chatter and have fun.
- Nina, thinking deeply, feels she left home without thinking compared to Tomo who had thought things through, and falls into introspection. Later, the five of them take a photo and, seeing the result, comment it looks like an idol group, only to be scolded by Subaru to shut up.
- They meet Mine in the area where the live is to be held, and Nina notices Momoka acting different than usual while talking with Mine. Having come to Tokyo, Momoka admired the rock she saw in Mine and probably reflected a lot of herself in that image. But the warm moment is brief; Momoka bluntly denies what Nina had said before that her songs would work in the pro world, and declares she will quit the band after this live.
- Hearing this, a dejected Nina asks Mine if she had ever wanted to quit music; Mine admits that after several failures such thoughts inevitably arise, but she still hasn't given up. As someone who, despite being able to take the seemingly correct path, chose to do what she loved, Mine says fear and anxiety are inevitable — and yet she didn't quit. Seeing Mine's smile, Nina realizes something.
- Nina entrusts Momoka, whom she would normally have carried on her back, to the other members and runs to the lake, shouting that she's made up her mind. As Nina lets out her inner cry, fireworks explode as if blessing the decision, and Nina murmurs with certainty before returning.
- On the day of the live, just before going on stage they still haven't decided a band name and the members hesitate about how to introduce themselves. Impulsively, Nina goes up on stage first and begins the MC. The problem is she started the MC because she had something she wanted to say, not to introduce the band, so flustered she desperately looks around and sees a spectator's T-shirt with "Togeari Togenashi Togetoge" written on it and, in a fit of temper, introduces the band as "Togenashi Togeari."
- The members are horrified that she named the band without consulting them, while the unaware spectator is delighted, saying it's the same as his shirt. Nina finishes her remarks saying there's someone she wants to make sure recognizes how great her music is, and begins the performance.
- Singing the first song as Togenashi Togeari, "All the Nameless Things (名もなき何もかも)," Nina successfully performs in front of the audience and then drops the bombshell that she will quit her cram school for entrance exams.
- Momoka reacts to Nina's sudden act by first criticizing, "A performance stage is for the audience, not a place to yak about your personal problems," but Nina retorts that she wanted Momoka to listen. Momoka then scolds her, "Who do you think you are, stirring things up on your own?" Nina fires back, "Then is it okay for you to drop a bomb and say you're quitting the band right before the show?"
- After a verbal spat, Nina provokes Momoka, "Then why didn't you quit the band and go back home? You started the band again — don't run away." Momoka is momentarily moved and tries to slap Nina, but Nina says, "If you're going to hit me, then hit me. You'll only find out you're the same as them," so Momoka cannot bring herself to strike. In the end they disband without reconciling.
- From Nina and Momoka's conflict, the phrase "used to violence" refers to "them," meaning the ringleaders who absurdly bullied Nina at school. Momoka couldn't bring herself to slap Nina because Nina had been saved by Momoka's music and believed Momoka was different from those who'd unjustly acted violently toward her; if Momoka resorted to absurd violence against Nina, that belief would be shattered.
- Nina inherits a guitar she used to play from Mine and starts playing guitar with it. As promised, Nina quits the cram school and sends a letter home saying, "You don't need to send me living expenses anymore, and I'll return the money I received," declaring her independence. She starts a part-time job at the gyudon shop where Rufa and Tomo work and which is their regular hangout.
- At the gyudon shop, Nina is about to scold when Rufa is verbally abused by a troublesome customer who says, "Who do you think you are, an arrogant foreigner talking back?" (Rufa had only said, "I'm sorry, sir" in response to the customer's irrational words but was accused of backtalk.) Nina tries to intervene, but Rufa blocks her physically and calms the customer down and sends him off. Rufa says this kind of thing is commonplace and part of why he needs rock music.
- Later Momoka comes in as a customer and asks if Nina has time after, showing her the Diamond Dust venue from her car (the mood at the gyudon shop is chilly because everyone is still sulking, but once they get in the car together the atmosphere softens a bit and Nina admits she doesn't hate driving with Momoka).
- Momoka claims those people aren't wrong; they're just living their own lives, and that to succeed in music she'd have to abandon her musicality and pander to fans. She denies Nina's point.
- But Nina refuses to generalize from a single Diamond Dust example and says that's not proof Momoka's music can't work in the pro world, dismissing Momoka's counterargument with a fair argument.
- Momoka answers Nina's earlier question, why she restarted the band: she says she saw in Nina someone who didn't seek others' approval and didn't grovel but simply played the music she loved, and that past version of herself was someone she wanted to watch over. She says she hopes Nina won't be bound by anything (Diamond Dust, the pro world).
- Nina, completely enraged, slaps Momoka's cheek and declares she is not Momoka's past self, ordering Momoka not to imprison her in memories.
- Nina recalls the past: Nina, her father, the perpetrators and their parents, and a principal-like teacher. The principal brushes off all the school violence Nina suffered as trivial kid's play, saying it's settled if both sides agree, and forces Nina to shake hands in reconciliation with the perpetrators. Seeing the perpetrators grinning, Nina mumbles "empty box" — Momoka's phrase — storms out, and occupies the broadcast room.
- What Nina said to hesitant Momoka about truly starting music: "There was someone who, after hearing your song... thought, 'I have to keep living'... (Nina, cornered by school violence and conflict with her parents, had even contemplated suicide.) What you need to protect isn't the you in memories... It's the desire to convey your song to someone... to pass on your feelings, your joy, your anger, your sadness to someone — that's why you started the band, right? That's why you sang at the school festival and came to Tokyo!? You became a pro!? What are you afraid of!! Why are you so scared!? Here they are...! Because of you someone got courage, got energy, and because you existed someone could soar... someone who felt happy singing with you... someone ready to stake their life with you!!"
- Nina pleads, recalling her past, asking Momoka to understand that someone was saved by Momoka's song. Overcome with emotion she can't continue and sobs that Momoka mustn't use her as an excuse to run away. At that moment, members of Diamond Dust come out of their waiting room and speak to Momoka; Momoka, unwilling to confront them, flees and the conversation is cut off.
- As Nina's gaze meets Hina — an old friend and the current vocalist of Diamond Dust — Momoka gets into the car, and without even making room for Nina she says, "You just take the train and come separately," and drives off. But Nina takes a shortcut through the platform and, in a reckless act, cuts in front of the car Momoka is driving; Momoka barely avoids hitting Nina and stops the car.
- Nina pleads that Momoka has done nothing wrong and need not run away; Momoka yells that she's noisy, and their positions remain at odds.
- Diamond Dust's members, having followed Nina, approach Momoka, but Momoka refuses communication by honking the horn loudly. When Nina ignores the horn, gets into the car, and takes Momoka's hand, the horn stops and Diamond Dust's members are able to communicate with Momoka.
- The Diamond Dust members declare their ambition to Momoka: as they promised before, they'll climb to a high position and play the music they want, proving they were not wrong. Momoka bites her lip in frustration. Nina likewise responds that they'll also show they're not wrong (Nina behaves very coldly and antagonistically).
- Unable to stand Nina's declaration, Momoka explodes and curses, "How dare you say that while doing that trashy music!" She publicly challenges Diamond Dust and Hina to compete at a festival, saying she'll battle them with music; Nina leaves together with Momoka.
- After challenging Diamond Dust, in the car Nina tells Momoka:
"Nina: After all, I like you, Momoka."
"Momoka: (choked up) What is that..."
"Nina: What do you think? A confession."
- On the way back, Nina confesses positively that she likes Momoka, causing Momoka to break down in tears. Nina quietly turns up the volume on the silent "empty box" and sheds a few tears herself.
- Afterwards she reports to Tomo, Rufa, and Subaru that they've reconciled, and Momoka officially sets Diamond Dust as their rival and aims to go pro. Thus Momoka formally joins, and with the whole team's direction unified, this moment marks the official formation of Togenashi Togeari.

## 4.4. Showdown with Diamond Dust:
### 4.4.1. Preparing a New Song:
- Nina gets scolded for not practicing what she was supposed to in the band's rehearsal room and just basking in the air-conditioning. Her home's air conditioner had broken, and it had been so hot she couldn't sleep all night. The topic soon changes to which song they'll perform at the summer festival; Nina doesn't feel there's any problem, but Momoka feels the song needs fixing before they start serious rehearsals.

Eventually Nina, along with Tomo and Rupa, flee to Subaru's house; ignoring the homeowner's complaints, she soaks up the AC freely. She shamelessly insults Tomo and Rupa's place, saying "The breeze is much better than that junk AC." Nina brazenly asks "What did I do wrong?" and gets flak from Subaru for being shameless, but Rupa points out that Tomo isn't sulking because of them.

- When Momoka asks how they all ended up at Subaru's, Nina is pointed out as the cause and scolded, but she shifts the blame to her broken home AC instead of herself.
- Subaru tells her not to barge into someone else's house and to try to tough it out somehow; Nina says she tried to survive by freeloading off other places' AC — reading displayed magazines while watching clerks and buying nothing, or spacing out on benches at big supermarkets — but it's hard to kill time outside without spending money, so she couldn't stand it and decided to mooch at the members' houses.
- But Momoka got startled by Tomo's snake and locked herself in the bathroom. Subaru complains, "Even if you're locking yourself in, why there of all places do you choose to make our bathroom unusable?" Momoka protests it's strange that among four girls she's the only one afraid of snakes, but Nina sneers "Is there really anything to be afraid of?" and taunts Momoka.
- As the two push and pull at the door, Subaru worries they'll break it, but Rupa smashes the door and Subaru's worry comes true.
- With the bathroom door at Subaru's house destroyed, the next morning all four are berated by an exasperated Subaru — "Don't think about coming to my house again" — and are chased out, facing his angry scolding. Only Tomo's snake is spared and allowed to stay because it's susceptible to heat.
- Momoka goes to her part-time job, leaving Nina, Tomo, and Rupa. Nina asks them to evaluate her guitar playing. Tomo, as with Momoka, hedges saying he's not a guitar expert or "Isn't it fine?" to avoid a direct critique. But listening to Nina's very beginner-level song, he can't help but let out a few harsh remarks. Nina seems totally unconcerned, but Tomo lashes out: "With that kind of make-believe guitar playing you'll never be able to use it on stage in your life," a verbal assault.
- After going home Tomo agonizes, certain that Nina was hurt by his words; Rupa comforts him saying Nina looked happy, but Tomo can't accept that and sulks.
- The next day Nina goes to Subaru and asks him to listen to her guitar playing. Subaru, about to refuse saying she's here again to freeload the AC because of the day before and yesterday, lets her in after Nina convinces him she needs serious counseling. On Subaru's broken bathroom door there's a note: "I'll definitely pay for it in 36 monthly installments — Nina."
- Subaru asks what Tomo said before listening to Nina's playing; upon hearing Tomo's critique he asks if she hasn't lost her drive because of such an abusive remark. But Nina doesn't even understand why she'd lose motivation. Subaru says he agrees with Tomo and bluntly states that while it might pass at an amateur level, it's not acceptable for the pro level they're aiming for.
- Nina asks Subaru didn't he also practice to reach his current level, but Subaru says Tomo would have plenty to criticize about his own skills too. He points out that Nina asked Tomo for an evaluation, so Tomo gave a harsh review; she didn't ask Subaru, so he had nothing to say.

- Nina finally stops by Momoka's to play for her and asks if they could possibly perform together on stage, but Momoka, with a slightly displeased expression, gives the harsh assessment "Are you serious?" Nina replies as if she expected that answer: "I suppose so?"

Tomo responds with a smile, and through this interaction grows, concluding that the Togenashi Togeari Band are people he can trust and communicate with; he then expresses to Momoka the thoughts he'd been keeping to himself and begins to actively participate in the band's activities.

### 4.4.2. Confronting the Past:
- The Togetoge members had been working hard preparing for the fest, and with windfalls like Benisho (beni-shouga) drawing in fans and their performance at the Fireworks Festival, they were hoping revenue would go up — but in reality the merch table was dead and only Rupa’s hug fanservice was wildly popular with women. Momoka quips, “Is our band really that popular?” and Tomo ribs Nina, “It’s because Nina gave the band such a weird name.”
- Then Saomi Miura, an employee of the famous entertainment company “Golden Archer,” approaches Togetoge. She says she’s a fan of Diamond Dust — specifically the Diamond Dust from the period when Momoka was in it — adds that she doesn’t like the current Diamond Dust, and explains she approached Togetoge because the old Diamond Dust is here now.
- Nina jumps up excitedly when their direction is praised, but Momoka stops her. Momoka points out that when Diamond Dust joined a label in the past they were told they couldn’t succeed with their current style; she agrees with that assessment, but insists that the members assembled now will never bend their direction. At first glance they seem to be saying the same thing, but what Momoka is really doing is preempting them: “You may say that, but if you join a label you’ll have to change direction, won’t you? We absolutely won’t.”
- Miura genuinely liked Momoka’s Diamond Dust and Togenashi Togeari that inherited that style, but that was only her personal opinion; as a company they’d naturally want to invest in people who guarantee commercial results. In the end Miura leaves, saying that if they’re going to join a label while keeping their musical integrity they should leave solid results at the fest to make it easier to convince the company.
- Fired up by the festival performance making the path to going pro feel more solid, the Togetoge members are full of energy, and Momoka asks Nina to write the lyrics for the new song they’ll perform at the fest. But despite answering confidently, Nina can’t write any lyrics at all, and when Tomo nags her during her part-time shift, Nina puts on a line like, “You don’t get it. Lyrics don’t come out that quickly,” only to be shot down by Tomo: “You dare act all high-and-mighty — do you know how many songs I’ve written?”
- Then Nina’s mother shows up at the donburi shop without warning and even comes to the counter looking for Nina herself. Luckily Rupa, quick on the uptake, tells Nina’s mother that Nina isn’t working today, but Nina is creeped out that her mother even figured out where she works.
- Feeling a bad premonition, Nina rushes home, but unsurprisingly her father is camped out there, so she ends up taking refuge at Subaru’s house. Subaru doesn’t know Nina’s circumstances (at this time only Momoka among the Togetoge members knew Nina’s past), and asks why she won’t even talk to someone without knowing the reason. Nina answers that, to her, he’s like a grandparent; their values are different, they can’t communicate, and she doesn’t even want to speak. (At this point Nina’s relationship with her father is hostile.)
- Moreover, when Nina says she has to write lyrics and this is an important time for Togetoge and she doesn’t want to be disturbed — “for Togetoge” — Subaru criticizes her, saying it just sounds like running away.
- The next morning Nina calls her older sister to try to figure out the situation. But her sister scolds her with the obvious point: did you think sending a note like that and then cutting contact would make the parents accept it and not care? Face them and talk properly. Irritated, Nina hangs up. Seeing that no one is camped in front of the house anymore she relaxes and approaches the front door, but the lock has already been changed by her parents so her old key no longer works and she can’t get in.
- With no choice, Nina goes back to the donburi shop and calls the landlord, angrily saying, “I’m the one actually living there — why did you change the lock without permission? Put it back right now.” (Her rented place has her father listed as guarantor.)
- Rupa and Tomo present her with train tickets back to her hometown as a gift they’ve prepared. Rupa says, as Tomo already knows, that he himself had something to say to his parents by phone but postponed it because of work, and by the time he wanted to say it his parents had already died and he could no longer tell them. You never know when that might happen to you, so talk properly while you can.
- This time they go to Momoka’s house to talk, and when Nina says she doesn’t know whether her parents really want to talk — “they always acted like they didn’t care what happened to me” — Momoka, from behind, adds a sound effect on her guitar to needle Nina (Momoka is smiling, seeming to enjoy it).
- In the end even Momoka tells her, “Go have a proper conversation with your parents,” and adds one more thing: “What still sits heavy in your heart isn’t the exams or the bullying — it’s that your father didn’t take your side,” hitting the nail on the head of Nina’s inner wound. She even tells Nina not to come to her house again until she’s talked things out.
- Nina finally returns home and faces her parents at the front door. A family meeting begins, including her sister, and Nina’s sister tells their parents that Nina is trying to make the band her profession and is on the verge of turning pro.
- The father, smoking heavily in another room, lashes into Nina, saying she did that without reporting to the family and asking if she thought about how sad her mother would be. Nina replies that when she left home she told them to do as they liked, and she’s doing just that — what’s the problem? The father retorts, “I never said all you had to do was get into university.”
- Nina insists she already lives independently while her father insists she’s still under parental protection — their opinions run parallel, but in reality the father’s view begins to hold weight. He points out that the apartment she’s living in was arranged through their mother and that their names are on the guarantor form, stressing that Nina is a minor under adult supervision. The ashtray is full of cigarettes.
- Nina, enraged, accuses her father of having brushed off the bullying she suffered at school as if it never happened and told her to just endure it and keep going to school. She angrily asks if he thinks that was right because minors must meekly follow adults’ orders. She decides once again that he’s not someone she can talk to and tries to leave, but her father comes out of his room and orders her to come with him because there’s a place they will go tomorrow.
- The next morning Nina follows her father without knowing the destination, but at some point realizes they’re headed to her old school and refuses to go. However her father has already spoken to the school and, saying she’s not the only one who has things to say, forces her to go.
- In front of the principal the father makes Nina confront the situation and shows that he got written apologies from the offending students. But the students didn’t bow their heads — in fact their faces weren’t shown — and while the father apparently pulled himself together and fought desperately after Nina left home, all he managed to get was a mechanical apology letter. Moreover the apology used phrasing like “acts that could be perceived as bullying,” failing to properly acknowledge the bullying and deceiving Iseri and her daughter; and at the end it took a tone of wanting to smooth things over quickly, asking for cooperation in resolving things and even framing the fact that the victim student withdrew from school as something to be accepted.
- The principal offers words of apology but continues to dodge the school’s responsibility, while the father accuses the school of having known and looked the other way, asking how they can claim no responsibility and now, belatedly, appear to be trying to properly resolve Nina’s school violence case. For Nina it all seems like after-the-fact noise, and she angrily tells them to stop; when her father takes her side — “You don’t need to say that. You’re the victim.” — Nina is stunned, then bites her lip, seeming angry.
- In truth going all the way to her old school yielded nothing practical, but at least Nina could see her father’s sincere effort — even if it was like closing the barn after the horse has bolted — to do everything he could after she ran away. Nina felt that too, but found it hard to accept, and the measures were indeed too late. The wound isn’t fully healed.
- Only when the sun is setting does Nina return home; ignoring her mother’s question about dinner she goes back to her room and begins preparing to return to Tokyo. When her sister asks if she really intends to do the band, Nina says that that song (Momoka’s “Empty Box”) encouraged her when she was having a hard time.
- At that time no one had been on her side, but that song alone stood by her. When she’s in the band she feels like she’s flying, and even if it’s just the illusion of soaring, she says she will still do the band.
- Her sister offers to let her rest her head on her lap, but Nina refuses, not because she’s angry but because, unlike when her sister first came to her room, she has begun to emotionally separate from her family.
- Her sister becomes convinced that Nina is not using the band to run away but is serious about it, and stops questioning her further, though she asks her to at least eat a meal. Nina calmly says, with a smile as if it’s okay now, that she had once tried to kill herself; hearing this her sister starts crying, tears roll down Nina’s face, and her sister says she’s grateful Nina stayed alive.
- When her sister brings curry to the room to eat alone together, Nina asks why they’re eating dinner alone in her room — what happened to the family rule “dinner is always in the living room with the family”? Her sister says that rule hasn’t been kept since Nina left. The reason is that their father became strange: he drank despite not being able to handle alcohol, getting anxious about Nina, and even as a proud educator he swallowed his pride and went asking acquaintances for advice. Her sister tells her that their parents love her. Nina eats while crying.
- The next morning, as Nina is about to leave the house, from beyond the door her father is listening to Momoka’s “Empty Box” and says it’s a good song, and perhaps his words slightly soften her, so she says “sorry, I’m a stubborn daughter” and leaves.
- From talking with her sister she realizes she was loved even if it wasn’t in the way she wanted, but still thinking their values wouldn’t align she almost leaves in silence with a stern face. Surprised by her father’s remark that “Empty Box” is a good song, she gives a sort of farewell. The family stops her for a moment: her father tells her to take care, and her sister, saying it’s a gift from their father, hands over the house (rental) key. Nina bows in thanks and rushes out, then returns to hug her father tightly and asks him to come hear her song before they part.
- She accepts hugging her father but, as if not yet ready for the reverse, quickly pulls away when he tries to hug her. She then smiles brightly and hooks her pinky finger with his.
- She boards the shinkansen back to Tokyo. Her completed lyrics are visible on her phone screen. Unlike when she first left for Tokyo, having resolved all her issues with her family, she returns looking healthy and happy. When she gets back to Kanagawa the Togetoge members are waiting — they say if she’d come back just one day later Momoka would have driven off with the car. Expressing her thanks she runs to the members, smiling that she came up with good lyrics.

### 4.4.3. Summer Rock Festival:
- They complain that the rehearsal room they borrowed to practice for the festival is small. 모모카 teases 니나 but smiles proudly. Also, 토게토게 had originally been scheduled to play on the festival’s first day at a time that isn’t very popular, but 모모카 says that because the band originally booked had to back out, they’ve been moved to the second day slot with a much bigger audience, and she expresses a quiet mix of tension and expectation as if a tailwind is blowing.
- At 모모카’s suggestion, 토게토게 goes to scout the venue in advance. 니나 and 모모카 shudder at the thought of facing 다이더스, who perform on a large, eye-catching stage, from an unfavorable, cramped stage with low name recognition; the members all agree to perform on the second day. Before leaving, 니나 asks 모모카 to lend her a guitar on the day of the festival, and 루파 tells her that 모모카 deliberately started working at a new gyudon shop where 루파 and 토모 aren’t around, saying that 니나 is beginning to stand on her own.
- Later 니나 thinks about why she does music, and her reason is: “I… want to believe I’m not wrong.”
- On the day of the festival, 스바루 says he’s going to go see the other performances; 니나 tries to go with him but is refused. While resting in the waiting room, 미네 drops by to say hello, and they meet the guitarist of the two-person band from episode 1, 쿄코.
- 니나 is startled along with 모모카 (she reflexively grabs 모모카), but 쿄코 says she likes 니나’s voice and shows her liking; the three of them even take a commemorative photo with funny faces. Back in the waiting room, 모모카 joins them and they make a promise with 스바루 to keep playing in the band together.
- When 다이아몬드 더스트’s set begins, everyone from 토게토게 goes to watch at 모모카’s suggestion. Seeing 다이더스 play “빈 상자,” 니나 recalls the past—lying on the rooftop with 히나 and being told to give up, to stop acting like the tragic heroine—and sets the Black Isle on fire.
- The 다이더스 members mimic 니나’s pinky-finger gesture to provoke 토게토게; most of the audience don’t notice, but this also shows that 다이더스 regards 토게토게 as a rival. 니나 vows to win over all those audience members, responding with a confident smile.
- After sound check and as the stage time approaches, 토게토게 gathers their pinkies and shouts a cheer, finally appearing before the festival audience. 니나 borrows 모모카’s guitar and plays it roughly, then announces to the crowd that she can’t really play guitar, returns it, and while doing MC work she and 모모카 get into a slight argument, which leads to 스바루 awkwardly introducing the members.
- Then, seeming to deny the provocations from 다이더스 and 히나 who had made the finger gesture at them, they completely overwhelm 다이더스’ stage with a flashy, polished stage presence, technique, and their own emotionally charged, brutal singing, capturing the attention of the festival crowd and successfully finishing their festival set.

### 4.4.4. Head-to-head:
- After successfully finishing the fest, Togenashi Togeari is recognized for their achievement and signs with the entertainment agency "Golden Archer." While taking profile photos in the stage outfits from the festival, someone mentions the photos were retouched, so Nina asks Tomo, who’s beside her, to check whether they were edited (Nina claims her nose looks more defined in the photo).
- Nina and the members step into the professional world. When they visit an instrument shop with Momoka and Subaru and Momoka says their pay will be 200,000 yen, they cheer loudly. Momoka nags them to read the contract properly, and Subaru jokes from the side that if they don’t read contracts they might end up unknowingly appearing in adult media, scaring them.
- The topic shifts back to the fest and Nina, upset that Daidus got more attention, is scolded by Momoka not to ego-surf. Momoka adds to sullen Nina that the real competition starts now, and Subaru teases that Momoka is clearly conscious of it too. In the end Nina and Subaru both get a playful “chup” in unison.
- Later, after Rupa and Tomo finish their last part-time shifts, Togenashi Togeari holds a celebration party at Momoka’s house. Before Rupa and Momoka can properly get things going they open alcohol, and when Subaru tries to ladle out their hotpot for once, Nina and Tomo complain that they like their napa cabbage well-cooked. Subaru grumbles at the bother, and Momoka, watching this, says a new song idea just occurred to her and leaves a note.
- Miura, who will be in charge of Togenashi Togeari, introduces four Golden Archer staff — including the agency’s engineer Nakata — to the band members. But because their builds and names are similar, Nina and the others show hesitant expressions.
- Especially Nina has trouble remembering names; seeing this, Momoka confidently tries to show off but messes up spectacularly, drawing laughter from Subaru and Nina. After that, the members keep misnaming Nakata as Tanaka.
- While pressing ahead with new song production and practice, the members receive a joint-competition proposal from Diamond Dust’s agency: a drama theme song contest where the more popular song between the two bands will be chosen. It’s like asking them to try hitting a rock with an egg, and aside from Nina, the members are reluctant. Miura explains the pros and cons: associating with Daidus would instantly bring attention, but if they lose it could create a framing that their skills are inferior. The TogeToge members vote by show of hands: Nina and Rupa vote to accept the offer; Momoka, Subaru, and Tomo vote to reject it, so the decision leans toward refusing.
- Nina, who doesn’t want to lose to Daidus, encourages her worried bandmates that they should just sing their own song, and she tells Miura she wants to accept the proposal too, trying to persuade the others.
- After work, Nina visits a city shrine with the TogeToge members and draws out Momoka’s true feelings by recalling past compositional failures, persuading the members once more. Tomo, Subaru, and Nina pray, believing in a “god of rock,” asking for good sales (they each increase the numbers they pray for — a million, ten million, a hundred million copies). Finally, Momoka says she wishes to beat Daidus in performance, and Nina, moved, hugs Momoka.
- As the new song nears completion and Momoka still feels uneasy, Nina watches her with concern. Nakata (at this point Nina thanks him but again calls him Tanaka, misnaming him), Miura, and the staff tell them they like TogeToge’s new song and encourage them, and Nina thanks them while asking about the streaming release date.
- That night Nina wakes up and prays to the god of rock for 100 million streams — or even just 10,000 — and checks the streaming count. But Nina’s hopes are crushed: the play count at that moment is only 103. TogeToge is utterly defeated in the initial sales battle.
- The next day, Nina dreams of her school days. When she went to rescue a classmate being bullied, Hina told her to leave them alone and even told Nina to give up, insulting her for being stubborn. But Nina didn’t back down and went to rescue the friend.
- Faced with the pitiful play counts, TogeToge holds a strategy meeting at Golden Archer headquarters with Miura. Momoka, seeing the situation, starts to say, “Exposure has definitely increased since before, so the lack of response means that—” but Miura cuts her off, flatly saying, “This is the agency’s responsibility,” apologizing to the members and promising to think of countermeasures.
- After the meeting, Momoka leaves without properly greeting the members; only slow-to-notice Nina doesn’t realize, but in truth the main reason TogeToge lost was that their song simply didn’t resonate with the public, and Miura took responsibility to avoid hurting Momoka.
- Nina can’t accept that, and Rupa tells her, “Van Gogh only sold one painting in his lifetime,” emphasizing that commercial success and artistry are different things. Subaru takes a different angle: “People are governed by information,” he says, asking whether someone would watch a game video with 5,000 views or 1,000,000 views, stressing that in a contest where you can’t gain buzz in the first week, TogeToge was bound to lose.
- To show the vast difference in recognition, a Diamond Dust advertising truck happens to pass by, and Subaru restrains Nina, who angrily raises both middle fingers at the unfortunate truck.
- Moreover, at the rock fest, although Daidus and TogeToge had different stage sizes and fan pools, nobody else knew there had been a showdown between them at this festival except the participants. Some netizens occasionally compared TogeToge and Daidus because of Momoka’s presence. Because of that, despite the huge popularity gap, TogeToge wasn’t directly compared to Daidus and was able to be judged purely on musicality.
- But this contest is a fully public, head-to-head showdown where Daidus would be recognized by the public as TogeToge’s rival, and timing-wise it was tantamount to Diamond Dust’s agency stepping on the weaker band — a ruthless act of stomping on new growth.
- As expected, unlike the praise-filled fest, mocking commenters and rekkas swarm in and insult TogeToge based solely on numbers, and they start the framing that TogeToge’s skills are declining. Nina, already furious, feels terrible, thinking: why do they care if we didn’t even win?
- The flood of criticisms despite only 103 plays means most of those people haven’t even listened and are just hurling insults. In fact, after they secured a bit more plays later, Momoka mentions that many netizens, listeners, and rock fans said positive things: “I like it,” “I don’t get why people are openly trashing this classic Japanese rock / alternative rock / near-alternative metal masterpiece and comparing it to Daidus, which is closer to idol pop,” “To those who trashed TogeToge and those rekkas and trash journalists — shut up and actually listen before you judge!!” — along with cheers, expectations for the members, and a rise in follower counts.
- In the middle of this, Hina contacts Nina with a follow request on X, and when they meet again, Hina makes a gesture of reconciliation, saying she didn’t join Daidus to pick on Nina and asking not to misunderstand. But she soon reveals that Miura had actually gone to Diamond Dust’s office and asked them to hold a joint live with TogeToge. Their schedules are open, so if the members themselves agree it could happen anytime. Accepting would be a de facto surrender — a chance to turn the match around from a losing frame — but commercially they could sell tickets borrowing Daidus’ fame and cover losses.
- As a Diamond Dust song plays from a nearby shop, Hina flaunts that they are overwhelmingly popular and says if Nina admits she was wrong and apologizes, she might help them — raising her pinky and provoking Nina with an offer to join their agency. Angry, Nina throws down money for the meal like slamming it on the table, cuts Hina off, and leaves.
- What Hina said has reached the other TogeToge members through Miura, and Nina is contacted. Everyone thinks it’s not a bad offer and would accept, but Nina is the only one who vehemently opposes it, knowing it’s the result of Miura having bowed his head to them behind their backs. Momoka tries to reason, saying who would refuse such a safe bet when so much money is at stake, but bows her head to Nina, saying it was her song that failed in the first place.
- Crushed, Nina tells the others that Miura went to bow to Daidus without their knowledge and that Hina deliberately brought this news to provoke her. She grabs Momoka’s guitar, climbs onto the kotatsu, wails out a desperate guitar solo, tries to persuade her bandmates, then collapses to the floor expressing how furious and upset she is.
- Subaru, perhaps lacking the stomach for such melodramatic youthful fervor, reacts with a stunned “here we go again.”
- Calming down, Nina reveals past events to the members. She had left the classroom to rescue the ostracized kid but nothing good came of it. She was beaten by the ringleaders, cut off by Hina, and told she was the bad one — that Nina’s righteous stubbornness made Hina feel like the villain and hurt her. On top of that, the kid Nina saved sided with the bullies and harassed Nina, telling her to apologize to the gang and seek forgiveness — an absurd demand.
- Nina defends that the kid had said that out of fear, but hearing this, the others get angry too (adult members Momoka and Rupa are just shocked, but Subaru and Tomo can’t hide their displeasure).
- Young Tomo explodes and says he wouldn’t have saved someone like Hina, but blushes and says Nina’s stubbornness is precisely why he loves her songs and pledges to stand with her. Touched, Nina hugs Tomo, the atmosphere becomes warm, and the group gradually narrows their choice to reject the offer and confront Daidus head-on to protect Nina’s and Togenashi Togeari’s pride.
- However, since they belong to an agency, they can’t just discard the results Miura worked hard to achieve with no responsibility. They decide to go to the agency, bow, and apologize, promising to repay any losses from their own pockets and to leave the agency to challenge the match as independents.
- Though it’s a decision they knew would invite resentment, Miura, seeing TogeToge’s sincerity and vision, steps forward to say the agency will take responsibility for contractual issues and losses. Nakata, who was the sound staff, offers to work for them for free if they can still be around in ten years, handling things warmly (Nina properly calls him Producer Nakata). Togenashi Togeari returns to being independent without major problems.
- The solo live Togenashi Togeari holds draws only a small audience — not even half the capacity of a small live house. Nina tells a brief story of her past to the crowd, thinking about Hina and realizing that Hina must have formed her own stubbornness from listening to Diamond Dust’s music (she realizes that her being not wrong doesn’t automatically mean the other person is wrong, and smiles with relief).
- Still, Nina promises they will prove they weren’t wrong; she asks the audience to witness the beginning of that proof and performs the final song of season 1, “Flower of Fate.” The show ends successfully amid the audience’s cheers and high praise, and together with her bandmates she takes her first real steps as true bandmen and rockers.
- Afterward, Nina resumes studying and lyric-writing and feels a lingering sense of incompleteness that makes it hard to focus. Seeing Diamond Dust’s national tour poster, she messages the band members and they set out on a trip together in Momoka’s car.
